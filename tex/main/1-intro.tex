\section{Introduction}

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has become a widely adopted paradigm for aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences and improving their reasoning capabilities~\citep{zeng2025simplerl, guo2025deepseek}. As tasks shift from short-form chat to long-horizon reasoning---mathematical problem solving, code generation, and multi-step planning---the computational cost of maintaining a critic (value) network becomes prohibitive. Critic-free methods such as REINFORCE++ Baseline~\citep{hu2025reinforce}, GRPO~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath}, and RLOO~\citep{ahmadian2024back} have emerged as practical alternatives, eliminating the critic and saving approximately 25\% of GPU memory in comparable settings~\citep{hu2025reinforce}.

Despite their efficiency, critic-free methods face two fundamental challenges that become increasingly severe as sequence lengths grow:

\textbf{Challenge 1: Advantage scale and token-mass imbalance.} Without a learned value function, the advantage must be estimated from
reward signals alone. Including each reward in its own mean baseline produces a deterministic $(n-1)/n$ scaling of the RLOO estimator
(\Cref{app:rloo-proof}). Separately, token expansion weights responses by their length, so zero-sum response advantages need not have zero
token-weighted mean. A common positive scale preserves this imbalance, while subtracting a token mean can change advantage signs.
This motivates sign-dependent normalization that meets the token-level moment constraints while preserving each advantage's sign.

\textbf{Challenge 2: Causal off-policy mismatch.} PPO-style LLM-RL optimizes a policy $\pitheta$ using trajectories generated by a fixed
rollout policy $\piroll$ and a frozen PPO snapshot $\piold$. Distributed staleness, numerical effects, and architectural
discontinuities (e.g., MoE routing flips~\citep{liu2024deepseek}) can amplify the divergence between $\piroll$ and $\pitheta$, producing
large per-token importance ratios $\rho_t=\pitheta(y_t\mid c_t)/\piroll(y_t\mid c_t)$. Because generation is autoregressive, an early ratio
deviation shifts the
future context distribution, so token-independent ratio interventions may fail to respect the causal structure of error accumulation.

These challenges interact in the sampled update: each contribution combines an advantage estimate with an importance ratio.
The population surrogate error decomposes into a true advantage factor and a context-distribution shift factor
(\Cref{eq:error-decomp}); the true advantage factor is distinct from the sampling variance of its estimator.
RLOO supplies a baseline conditionally independent of each sample's own response given the prompt, while TIS and IcePop operate on individual importance ratios.
ProMax combines control of empirical advantage statistics with a prefix-based rule for retaining ratio-weighted contributions.

We present \textbf{REINFORCE Pro Max}, combining adaptive advantage normalization (\Cref{sec:reinforce-max}) with prefix-level Causal
Trust Region masking (\Cref{sec:reinforce-pro}). Under the finite conditional model stated in the preliminaries,
\Cref{prop:surrogate-equivalence} establishes the exact population connection of the raw critic-free surrogate to the per-step
advantage surrogate. \Cref{prop:promax-objective-split} then isolates the changes made by
normalization, masking, and clipping on each finite batch.
\Cref{thm:promax-certificate} combines these identities with independent-group
sampling and the Adaptive bound to give a performance certificate for the
complete ProMax objective. Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Adaptive asymmetric normalization} (\Cref{sec:adaptive-norm}): We introduce a normalization scheme that applies different
    scaling factors $\alpha, \beta$ to positive and negative advantages. The scheme provably preserves the sign of each non-zero advantage
    (and the relative ordering within positive and negative groups). With both sign groups nonempty, the ideal closed-form factors
    enforce zero empirical mean and unit empirical variance on non-zero tokens
    (\Cref{def:adaptive-norm}); \Cref{prop:normalization-safeguards} gives exact moment formulas and residual bounds for the
    implemented additive stabilizer and clamps. For actual binary-reward RLOO
    groups, \Cref{prop:binary-rloo-normalization} identifies the exact
    token amplitudes and shows that the ratio of the two scales is the ratio
    of the class-conditional mean response lengths.
    For the complete on-policy binary population update, including numerical
    safeguards and content-dependent lengths,
    \Cref{cor:binary-local-improvement} gives a covariance-margin condition
    for local true-reward improvement at a fixed prompt with accepted gates
    and inactive clipping.

    \item \textbf{Prefix Causal Trust Region masking with causal structure (\Cref{sec:prefix-is}):} We propose a prefix-cumulative masking
    strategy constructed theoretically from the per-token ratio $\rho_t=\pitheta/\piroll$ and implemented from the cached
    $\piold/\piroll$ factor. Motivated by long-horizon trust-region analyses for LLM-RL
    (e.g., TRM~\citep{li2025trm}), we apply a per-position prefix gate to the token-level loss, respecting the autoregressive causal
    structure. This differs from TRM, which uses a sequence-level acceptance gate $M^{\mathrm{TRM}}(x,y)$ to discard entire trajectories.
    Under the explicit conditions in \Cref{thm:prefix-tighter}, the mask propagates early-deviation rejection and confines late-deviation
    rejection to a suffix. A single-spike construction gives prefix rejection with full-sequence acceptance; sustained violations make
    all three masks reject. These results characterize conditional comparisons rather than a single dominance ordering.

    \item \textbf{A unified finite-sample reward guarantee}
    (\Cref{thm:promax-certificate,cor:covered-policy-certificate}):
    We connect the actual clipped ProMax objective to expected reward improvement through explicit gate,
    normalization, clipping and sampling terms, using the Adaptive bound from the
    trust-region framework~\citep{li2025trm}. Its complete finite-tree proof
    retains history dependence (\Cref{thm:adaptive-bound}). The statistical
    proof uses complete RLOO groups as independent blocks,
    retains random response lengths and finite prompt averaging, and controls false improvement certificates
    after selecting from a finite family fixed before evaluation.
    \Cref{cor:promax-energy-certificate} removes the unknown mean response length by observed-token
    rescaling and controls sampling error through additive conditional ratio energy.
    The exact shared-baseline variance in \Cref{prop:rloo-group-variance} quantifies the effect of
    responses per prompt while retaining the separate variance across prompts.
    A fixed finite cover extends the guarantee to policies fitted on the evaluation data
    within a pre-specified class, with an explicit approximation and complexity cost.
    Uniform logit-change bounds give explicit certificate inputs and a sufficient
    cover radius without requiring a minimum token probability
    (\Cref{cor:logit-calibration}).
    The objective identities and statistical bounds are verified in Lean on the same finite autoregressive policy model used by the formal statements. This does not formalize a floating-point neural-network implementation. The statistical guarantee is stated for unconditioned iid evaluation groups; optional training-time whole-group score filtering changes that law and is covered by a separate finite counterexample.\
\end{enumerate}
