\section{Implementation Divergence and Trust-Region Anchoring}

\subsection{Details on Off-Policy Mismatch in LLM-RL}
\label[appsec]{app:mismatch}

Modern LLM-RL pipelines decouple inference from training: rollouts are produced by an inference engine (e.g., vLLM or SGLang) under the
rollout policy $\piroll$, while gradient updates are computed in a separate training stack under the training policy $\pitheta$. This
architectural separation can introduce off-policy mismatch even when parameters are nominally aligned.

\paragraph{Mismatch Sources}
Common sources of mismatch include backend discrepancies (different attention kernels, precision formats, or reduction order), MoE routing
discontinuities (Top-$K$ routing can flip under small perturbations), and distributed staleness (actor--learner lag between rollout and
consumption). These effects can compound autoregressively over long horizons and lead to volatile per-token ratios.

\paragraph{Anchoring and the Apparent Three-Distribution Structure}
Practical pipelines can exhibit an apparent three-distribution structure: (i) a rollout engine generates tokens according to an
inference-time distribution, (ii) a training framework computes gradients under $\pitheta$, and (iii) some implementations recompute the
behavior log-probabilities inside the training stack, yielding a recomputed anchor that can differ from the true rollout distribution even
when parameters are identical. Importantly, this third object is not an additional policy in our theory; it is an alternative
implementation-time anchor for the denominator of $\rho_t$.

\paragraph{Trust Region Setup}
\citet{qi2026rethinkingtrustregionllm} argue that the trust region (and hence the denominator of the importance ratio) must be defined with
respect to the \emph{original} behavior policy that generated the data, rather than a recomputed policy distribution evaluated inside the
training stack. Accordingly, we denote this rollout-time behavior policy by $\piroll$, define
$\rho_t := \pitheta(y_t\mid c_t) / \piroll(y_t\mid c_t)$, and assume access to rollout-time cached log-probabilities
$\log \piroll(y_t\mid c_t)$. The theoretical development therefore involves only the policy pair $(\piroll,\pitheta)$, consistent with
long-horizon trust-region analyses for LLM-RL (e.g., \citet{li2025trm}).

\paragraph{PPO-Style $\piold$ Mismatch}
The implementation has a frozen PPO snapshot $\piold$ in addition to the
data-generating rollout policy $\piroll$. Its clipped PPO ratio and its
vLLM correction ratio are respectively
\[
    r_t^{\theta/\piold}
    =\frac{\pitheta(y_t\mid c_t)}{\piold(y_t\mid c_t)},
    \qquad
    w_t^{\piold/\piroll}
    =\frac{\piold(y_t\mid c_t)}{\piroll(y_t\mid c_t)}.
\]
Their product is the behavior-to-current ratio
$\rho_t^{\theta/\piroll}=r_t^{\theta/\piold}w_t^{\piold/\piroll}$.
The prefix mask is computed from $w_t^{\piold/\piroll}$ and is detached,
while gradients flow through the PPO ratio. The abstract prefix theorems
apply to whichever policy pair supplies the ratio; applying them to the
current policy during stale updates requires the finite-prefix coupling
condition in \Cref{sec:prefix-is}.

\subsection{Why PPO Clipping Can Leak Gradients Under Mismatch}
\label[appsec]{app:ppo-clip-leak}

\paragraph{PPO Clipped Surrogate}
PPO~\citep{schulman2017proximal} constrains policy updates by clipping the
current-to-snapshot ratio. In the notation of this paper, the ratio is
\begin{equation*}
    r_t^{\theta/\piold}
    := \frac{\pitheta(y_t \mid c_t)}{\piold(y_t \mid c_t)}.
\end{equation*}
The clipped surrogate takes the form
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{CLIP}}(\pitheta)
    = \E_{\piroll}\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}
        w_t^{\piold/\piroll}\,
        \min\!\Big(
        r_t^{\theta/\piold} \hat{A}_t,\;
        \mathrm{clip}(r_t^{\theta/\piold}, 1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}}, 1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}})\,\hat{A}_t
    \Big)\right],
\end{equation*}
where $\hat{A}_t$ is an advantage estimator and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}}>0$ is the clipping threshold.

\paragraph{Asymmetry and Gradient Leakage}
The clipping operator is asymmetric in the sign of $\hat{A}_t$. When
$\hat{A}_t<0$ and $r_t^{\theta/\piold} \gg 1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}}$, the
unclipped term can still be selected:
\begin{equation*}
    r_t^{\theta/\piold} > 1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}},\ \hat{A}_t<0
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    r_t^{\theta/\piold}\hat{A}_t < (1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}})\hat{A}_t
    = \mathrm{clip}(r_t^{\theta/\piold}, 1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}}, 1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}})\,\hat{A}_t,
\end{equation*}
hence $\min(\cdot)$ returns $r_t^{\theta/\piold}\hat{A}_t$. In this regime, the gradient magnitude still scales with
$r_t^{\theta/\piold}$, so extremely large ratios
can produce large updates even though they lie far outside the nominal trust region.

\paragraph{Why This Is Benign in Standard RL}
In classical on-policy settings, large ratios typically reflect genuine policy changes that over-emphasize low-quality actions; keeping the
unclipped penalty for $\hat{A}_t<0$ can be desirable as it discourages such actions.

\paragraph{Failure Mode Under Implementation Divergence}
In LLM-RL pipelines with inference--training mismatch, a large
rollout-to-snapshot coefficient can arise from numerical or backend
discrepancies rather than meaningful policy improvements. For example, a
routing discontinuity in a Mixture-of-Experts model can yield a token $v$ such that
\begin{equation*}
    \piroll(v\mid c_t) \approx 0.001,
    \qquad
    \piold(v\mid c_t) \approx 0.9,
    \qquad
    w_t^{\piold/\piroll} \approx 900.
\end{equation*}
Such spikes reflect implementation-level divergence and can multiply the
PPO loss by a massive, erroneous detached coefficient. This motivates using
a detached prefix trust-region mask to discard mismatched tokens rather than
relying on PPO clipping, which acts on the separate current-to-snapshot ratio.

\section{REINFORCE Max: Supplementary Details}

\subsection{Leave-One-Out Baseline Properties}
\label[appsec]{app:rloo-proof}

We summarize standard properties of the leave-one-out baseline estimator used in \Cref{sec:reinforce-max}; see
\citet{ahmadian2024back} for a detailed treatment.

\begin{remark}[Properties of the Leave-One-Out Baseline]
\label{rem:rloo-variance}
Fix a prompt $x$ and define its conditional reward objective by
\begin{equation*}
 J_x(\theta):=\mathbb E_{y\sim\pitheta(\cdot|x)}[R(x,y)].
\end{equation*}
Draw $n \ge 2$ i.i.d.\ responses $\{y^{(1)}, \ldots, y^{(n)}\} \sim \pitheta(\cdot|x)$ with rewards
$r_i = R(x, y^{(i)})$. Let
\begin{equation*}
    f_i = \nabla_\theta \log P^{\pitheta}(y^{(i)} \mid x)
\end{equation*}
denote the trajectory score function for sample $i$, and define the per-sample policy gradient contribution with baseline $b$ as
$g_i(b) = (r_i - b) f_i$. With the leave-one-out baseline $b_i = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j \neq i} r_j$, the estimator
\begin{equation*}
    \hat{g} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} g_i(b_i)
\end{equation*}
satisfies the following statements, provided the usual score-function regularity conditions hold: the $n$ responses are conditionally
i.i.d. given $x$; $r_i$ and $f_i$ are integrable, $f_i$ has a finite second moment, the products needed below are integrable (in
particular $r_i f_i$ and $(r_i-\mu(x))^2 f_i f_i^\top$), and differentiation may be interchanged with the expectation defining
$J_x(\theta)$. In particular, the normalized policy has support independent of $\theta$ (or obeys the corresponding boundary
conditions), so that $\E[f_i\mid x]=0$. Under these conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Conditional unbiasedness:} $\E[\hat{g}\mid x] = \nabla_\theta J_x(\theta)$.
    \item \textbf{Conditional baseline independence:} For each $i$, $b_i$ is independent of $(r_i, f_i)$ given $x$; in particular, $\E[b_i f_i\mid x] = 0$ and
    $\Cov(f_i, b_i\mid x) = 0$. The moment assumptions ensure that these products and covariances are well-defined.
    \item \textbf{Mean-baseline scaling:} Let $b^{\mathrm{mean}} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} r_j$ and
    $\hat{g}^{\mathrm{mean}} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r_i - b^{\mathrm{mean}}) f_i$. Then deterministically
    $\hat{g}^{\mathrm{mean}} = \frac{n-1}{n}\hat{g}$, and hence $\E[\hat{g}^{\mathrm{mean}}\mid x] = \frac{n-1}{n}\nabla_\theta J_x(\theta)$.
    \item \textbf{Second-moment decomposition:} Let $\mu(x) = \E[r_i \mid x]$ and $\sigma^2(x) = \Var(r_i \mid x)$. Then
    $\Var(b_i \mid x) = \sigma^2(x)/(n-1)$, and
	    \begin{equation*}
	        \E\!\big[(r_i - b_i)^2 f_i f_i^\top \mid x\big]
	        = \E\!\big[(r_i - \mu(x))^2 f_i f_i^\top \mid x\big] + \Var(b_i \mid x)\, \E[f_i f_i^\top \mid x].
	    \end{equation*}
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

For a prompt distribution $P$ independent of $\theta$, the paper's global
objective is $J(\pitheta)=\mathbb E_{x\sim P}[J_x(\theta)]$.
If differentiation also commutes with this outer expectation, then
\begin{equation*}
 \mathbb E_{x,Y_{1:n}}[\hat g]
 =\mathbb E_{x\sim P}[\nabla_\theta J_x(\theta)]
 =\nabla_\theta J(\pitheta).
\end{equation*}
For a fixed finite prompt law, this follows by differentiating a finite
weighted sum; the joint prompt/response sampling law and the complete RLOO
derivative are checked by \path{joint_prompt_response_mass_normalized},
\path{joint_prompt_response_mass_nonneg} and
\path{finite_prompt_rloo_policy_gradient} in \path{formal/PromptGradient.lean}.
Fixing one prompt cannot substitute for this averaging step: two equally
likely prompts with Bernoulli success probabilities $\theta$ and $1-\theta$
have a constant global reward $1/2$, while the first prompt's reward
derivative is $1$. Their valid policies on $0<\theta<1$ and the derivative
separation are checked by \path{two_prompt_policy_valid},
\path{two_prompt_reward} and \path{fixed_prompt_gradient_ne_global}.

The same statement does not require a finite prompt set. Let $\mu$ be an
arbitrary prompt measure and interpret the global objective as the Bochner
integral $\int J_x(\theta)\,\mathrm d\mu(x)$. If the conditional policy
regularity above holds $\mu$-almost everywhere, the conditional RLOO
differential is almost-everywhere measurable, and the prompt integrand is
locally Lipschitz in $\theta$ with an integrable dominating bound (together
with the corresponding measurability and integrability conditions), then
differentiation under the prompt integral gives
\[
 \mathrm D J(\theta)
 = \int \mathbb E[\hat g\mid x] \,\mathrm d\mu(x).
\]
These are the standard dominated differentiation conditions; they make the
outer exchange explicit rather than treating it as an automatic consequence
of conditional unbiasedness. The finite-response RLOO calculation and this
measure-level extension are checked by the following two Lean theorems:
\begin{center}
\path{ae_prompt_rloo_policy_gradient}\\
\path{measure_prompt_rloo_policy_gradient}.
\end{center}
They are both defined in \path{formal/PromptGradientMeasure.lean}.

\begin{remark}[Relationship Between the Two Estimators]
The deterministic proportionality $\hat{g}^{\mathrm{mean}} = \frac{n-1}{n}\hat{g}$ implies that the mean-baseline estimator is a
rescaled version of the leave-one-out estimator. Its smaller raw variance is entirely due to the constant scaling factor. In practice,
this shrinkage can be absorbed into the learning rate, but it breaks exact unbiasedness whenever the target gradient is nonzero. The main advantage of the
leave-one-out baseline is that $b_i$ is independent of sample $i$ given the prompt, which simplifies moment calculations and avoids self-correlation terms
that arise when the baseline depends on $r_i$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The leave-one-out baseline is equivalent to the leave-one-out control variate from the classical REINFORCE literature~\citep{williams1992simple}. In the LLM-RL setting with $n$ samples per prompt, each sample's baseline is conditionally independent of its own trajectory given the prompt, making it particularly well-suited for group-based sampling strategies.
\end{remark}

The stochastic statements in this remark are conditional on the stated
independence, integrability, and differentiation assumptions. The finite
Lean audit verifies the deterministic RLOO scaling, zero-sum, and same-sign
degeneracy identities, as well as the independent product-space control-variate
identity and scalar and cross-coordinate second-moment decompositions. The
finite i.i.d. leave-one-out construction is checked directly, including
$\Var(b_i)=\sigma^2/(n-1)$ and every entry of the displayed outer-product
identity. Extensions to general probability spaces and conditional expectations
retain the stated regularity assumptions.
One finite product-space building block is now checked explicitly: if $p$ is
the trajectory distribution, $u$ is an independent baseline-sample
distribution, and the score $f$ has zero $p$-mean, then

\begin{equation*}
    \sum_{i,j}p_i u_j\,(r_i-b_j)f_i
    = \sum_i p_i r_i f_i.
\end{equation*}

This is the finite form of the control-variate step
$\E[b_i f_i]=\E[b_i]\E[f_i]=0$ and is verified by
\path{finite_independent_baseline_score} in
\path{formal/RLOOProbability.lean}. The theorem does not by itself discharge
the general probability-space and differentiation--expectation obligations.
As concrete finite checks, \path{finite_iid_rloo_unbiased_two} proves the
two-sample leave-one-out estimator exactly, while
\path{finite_iid_rloo_unbiased} proves the arbitrary finite group-size case
directly: for $m+1$ independent draws from a finite mass function $p$ (with
$m\ne0$), the average leave-one-out estimator using the other $m$ rewards has
expectation $\sum_y p(y)r(y)f(y)$ whenever $\sum_y p(y)f(y)=0$. The abstract
interface \path{finite_rloo_independent_baselines} remains useful when baseline
laws are specified independently of this concrete i.i.d. construction.
The finite-dimensional score version is checked componentwise by
\path{finite_iid_rloo_unbiased_vector}; hence the displayed vector estimator's
expectation equals the target score expectation on finite response support.
The derivative connection is now also checked on finite response support.
Let $p_\theta(y)$ be locally nonnegative and normalized, differentiable at
$\theta$, and let $R(y)$ be independent of $\theta$. Define its score
differential as $s_\theta(y)=p_\theta(y)^{-1}D p_\theta(y)$ on positive-mass
responses and as zero otherwise. Local nonnegativity implies
$D p_\theta(y)=0$ at a zero-mass response. Thus
\begin{equation*}
    \sum_y p_\theta(y)s_\theta(y)=0,
    \qquad
    D\!\left(\sum_y p_\theta(y)R(y)\right)
       = \sum_y p_\theta(y)R(y)s_\theta(y)
       = \E[\hat g].
\end{equation*}
In a finite-dimensional Euclidean parameter space this differential is
identified with the usual gradient. The certificate
\path{finite_iid_rloo_policy_gradient} in \path{formal/PolicyGradient.lean}
proves these identities from local normalization, nonnegativity, and
differentiability, rather than assuming a zero-mean score or an exchange of
derivative and finite expectation. On positive-mass responses,
\path{policyScore_is_log_derivative} identifies $s_\theta(y)$ with
$D\log p_\theta(y)$. The general probability-space statement above retains
the listed integrability and differentiation assumptions. This certificate
concerns the raw on-policy RLOO estimator; the changes introduced by
normalization, prefix gates, off-policy weights, and clipping are treated
separately in \Cref{prop:promax-objective-split}.
The connection to the paper's autoregressive model is explicit:
\path{tree_iid_rloo_policy_gradient} in \path{formal/TreePolicyGradient.lean}
constructs the response masses by multiplying history-dependent token
probabilities and proves the result for the recursively defined terminal-reward
objective used in the performance-difference theorems. Differentiability of
the response masses follows from differentiability of the conditional token
probabilities. This permits common absorbing EOS transitions and makes no
independence assumption across positions within a response.

For a scalar score coordinate, the corresponding finite second-moment
calculation is also checked. If $p$ is the trajectory mass, $u$ is an
independent baseline mass with mean $\mu$, then
\begin{equation*}
    \sum_{i,j} p_i u_j (r_i-b_j)^2 f_i^2
    = \sum_i p_i (r_i-\mu)^2 f_i^2
      + \left(\sum_j u_j(b_j-\mu)^2\right)
        \left(\sum_i p_i f_i^2\right).
\end{equation*}
This is the scalar finite product-space form of the conditional second-moment
decomposition in the statement above and is verified by
\path{finite_independent_baseline_second_moment}. The cross-coordinate version is
verified by \path{finite_independent_baseline_cross_moment} and gives each entry
of a finite-dimensional outer-product decomposition. In
\path{formal/RLOOMoments.lean}, \path{finite_loo_baseline_marginal} derives
the baseline law by separating sample $i$ from the other $n-1$ draws in the
actual i.i.d. product mass. From this construction,
\path{finite_loo_baseline_variance} proves $\Var(b_i)=\sigma^2/(n-1)$,
and \path{finite_loo_score_cross_moment} proves
\begin{equation*}
    \E[(r_i-b_i)^2 f_{i,k}f_{i,l}]
    = \E[(r_i-\mu)^2 f_{i,k}f_{i,l}]
      + \frac{\sigma^2}{n-1}\E[f_{i,k}f_{i,l}]
\end{equation*}
for any two score coordinates $k,l$. No independent-baseline variance formula
is assumed in this final certificate. This is a per-sample second-moment
identity; the covariance of the group-averaged estimator also involves
cross-sample terms because its leave-one-out baselines share samples.
The exact scalar group-average variance is now given in
\Cref{prop:rloo-group-variance,app:rloo-group-variance}. Its finite-law
identity applies to any zero-mean scalar signal, including each score
coordinate here and the trajectory ratio residual used in the reward
certificate.

\subsection{Autoregressive RLOO Population Bridge and Random Reduction}
\label[appsec]{app:rloo-reduction}

The finite-tree formalization also connects the estimator actually used by RLOO to the population surrogate in the
performance-difference analysis. Let $p$ be the rollout tree policy, $q$ a candidate policy, and let
$\rho_t(y)$ be the candidate-to-rollout token ratio at the $t$th history on a finite horizon. The rollout trajectory
residual used in the formalization is $h(y)=\sum_t(\rho_t(y)-1)$ and has zero $p$-mean. Consequently, for a group of $m+1$ i.i.d. trajectories, the concrete leave-one-out
estimator satisfies
\begin{equation*}
    \E_p\!\left[(m+1)\,\widehat{\mathcal L}_{\rm RLOO}\right]
    =(m+1)\,\mathcal S_p(q),
\end{equation*}
and its group-size-normalized expectation is exactly the raw population surrogate $\mathcal S_p(q)$. This allows
arbitrary history dependence across response tokens; only the trajectories in the group are i.i.d. It is proved by
\path{response_ratio_residual_mean_zero}, \path{iid_rloo_surrogate_increment_expectation}, and
\path{group_surrogate_ratio} in \path{formal/RLOOSurrogate.lean}.

The bridge concerns the raw, unclipped surrogate. Prefix gating, adaptive normalization, off-policy weighting, and PPO
clipping remain the explicit correction terms in \Cref{prop:promax-objective-split}; they are not silently absorbed into
an unbiasedness claim. The reduction itself must also be stated precisely. If a response contributes $Z$ over $L>0$ active
tokens, response-level averaging estimates $\E[Z/L]$, while global token-level averaging estimates
$\kappa=\E[Z]/\E[L]$. The exact bias decomposition is
\begin{equation*}
    \E[Z/L]-\kappa
    =-\frac{\E[(L-\E[L])Z/L]}{\E[L]}.
\end{equation*}
For the independent-block analysis, $Z$ and $L$ instead denote full-group
sums. A group response mean remains the mean of its individual $Z_i/L_i$,
not the ratio of group sums: for contributions $(1,-1)$ and lengths $(1,3)$,
these quantities are $1/3$ and $0$, respectively. This arithmetic distinction
is checked by \path{group_ratio_ne_response_mean} in
\path{formal/BatchReduction.lean}.
For i.i.d. blocks with $L\ge\ell>0$, the self-normalized token estimator has the finite bounds
\begin{equation*}
    \E\!\left[(\bar Z_n/\bar L_n-\kappa)^2\right]
    \le \frac{\E[(Z-\kappa L)^2]}{n\ell^2},
    \qquad
    \Pr\left(|\bar Z_n/\bar L_n-\kappa|\ge\varepsilon\right)
    \le \frac{\E[(Z-\kappa L)^2]}{n\ell^2\varepsilon^2}.
\end{equation*}
These are checked by \path{ratio_expectation_gap},
\path{iid_token_mean_mse_of_ratio}, and \path{iid_token_mean_tail} in
\path{formal/BatchReduction.lean}.\par
The finite performance certificate in \path{formal/BatchPerformance.lean}
composes this estimation error with a valid population lower bound and
explicitly states that it is for a fixed candidate policy evaluated on
independent blocks; it is not a uniform guarantee after selecting the
candidate from the same data.

\subsection{Proof of the ProMax Performance Certificate}
\label[appsec]{app:promax-certificate}

Fix the candidate family before drawing evaluation blocks. A block is the
pair $(x,(y_1,\ldots,y_n))$, with mass
$P(x)\prod_{i=1}^n P^p(y_i\mid x)$. The finite history tree constructs each
trajectory mass from its conditional token kernels; summing over responses
and prompts gives a normalized block law. This is checked by
\path{block_mass_normalized}. Conditional on $x$, the response residual
$h_j(y)=\sum_t(\rho_{j,t}(y)-1)$ has zero rollout mean. Each $b_i$ is a
function of the other $n-1$ responses and is independent of $y_i$ given $x$.
Therefore
\begin{equation*}
    \E[Z_j\mid x]
    =n\E_{y\sim p(\cdot\mid x)}[R(x,y)h_j(y)]
    =n\mathcal S_{j,x}.
\end{equation*}
The equalities use the actual leave-one-out baseline; shared baselines are
not replaced by independent copies. Prompt averaging proves
$\E[Z_j]=n\mathcal S_j$, while $\E[L]=n\mu_L$.
These steps are checked by \path{block_increment_expectation},
\path{block_length_expectation}, and \path{block_ratio_target}.

For the full objective, let $D=\sum_b L_b$ and use weights $m_{b,i,t}/D$.
At the comparison policy $p$, the frozen-snapshot PPO ratio is
$r_0=p/\piold$ and satisfies $r_0w=1$. At $q_j$ it is $r_j=q_j/\piold$ and
satisfies $r_jw=\rho_j$. Subtracting the two exact objective decompositions
gives
\begin{align*}
    \Delta_j S_{\mathrm{PM}}+\Delta_jG-\Delta_jN
      +\Delta_j C_{\mathrm{clip}}
    &=\frac{1}{D}\sum_{b,i,t}m_{b,i,t}A_{b,i}(\rho_{j,b,i,t}-1)\\
    &=\frac{\sum_b Z_{j,b}}{\sum_b L_b}.
\end{align*}
The second equality uses the zero ratio residual on shared absorbing
padding. The gate, normalized signal, snapshot coefficients and clipping
terms remain in the first expression without independence requirements.
\path{response_residual_eq_token_sum} and
\path{group_increment_eq_token_sum} connect the token indices to the
history-tree ratio trace; \path{corrected_batch_change_eq_rloo_ratio} and
\path{corrected_block_objective_eq} verify the complete conversion.
Configured uniform-scale signals are included in the actual normalized
signal, so their difference from raw RLOO is retained in $N$.

Write $F_j=Z_j-\kappa_jL$. The population identities give $\E[F_j]=0$.
The blocks are independent, so
$\E[\bar F_j^2]=v_j/K$, even though siblings within a block are dependent.
Since $\bar L\ge n\ell$, the mean-square error of
$\widehat\kappa_j$ is at most $v_j/(K(n\ell)^2)$.
Markov's inequality on its square gives the individual tail bound, and a
finite union bound gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-uniform-confidence}. No
independence between candidate estimates is needed.
\path{rloo_uniform_estimation_bound} verifies this result with the block
law and its population means constructed above.

Finally, \Cref{thm:adaptive-bound} yields
$J(q_j)-J(p)\ge\mathcal S_j-B_j=\mu_L\kappa_j-B_j$.
On the simultaneous estimation event,
$\kappa_j\ge\widehat\kappa_j-\eta_j$ for every candidate, proving the
performance bound. If the selected candidate has nonpositive true gain
but passes the strict threshold, its estimation error must exceed its
margin. That event is a subset of the union event already bounded above.
\path{rloo_performance_from_estimate} and
\path{rloo_selected_false_improvement_bound} check these conclusions,
including the actual Adaptive cost and true reward values on the same
finite prompt/history model.

\subsection{Proof of the Observed-Length and Drift-Energy Certificate}
\label[appsec]{app:promax-energy-certificate}

For one prompt, define $h(y)=\sum_{t=1}^{|y|}(\rho_t(y)-1)$ and
$e(c)=\sum_a p(a\mid c)(q(a\mid c)/p(a\mid c)-1)^2$.
Normalization and $q\ll p$ imply
\begin{equation*}
    \sum_a p(a\mid c)(q(a\mid c)/p(a\mid c)-1)=0.
\end{equation*}
Zero-probability actions are omitted, or assigned zero weighted
contribution. Hence for every real $u$,
\begin{equation*}
    \sum_a p(a\mid c)\bigl(u+q(a\mid c)/p(a\mid c)-1\bigr)^2
    =u^2+e(c).
\end{equation*}
Induction over continuation length on the normalized history tree yields
\begin{equation*}
    \E_p[h(Y)^2\mid\text{prefix }c]
    =h(c)^2+\E_p\!\left[\sum_{t>|c|}e(c_t)
                         \mid\text{prefix }c\right].
\end{equation*}
The formal recursion is defined from each prefix kernel, including
unreachable prefixes, without conditioning on a zero-probability event.
At the root and after prompt averaging this gives
$\E[H_j(Y)^2]=\mathcal E_j$. The Lean theorems
\path{local_ratio_residual_mean_zero}, \path{residual_square_step},
\path{trajectory_residual_second_moment}, and
\path{response_residual_energy} verify these steps.
\path{ratio_energy_eq_prefix_sum} identifies the recursive cost with the
explicit sum of prefix-law expectations. No token independence is used.

Within an actual group, each leave-one-out baseline lies in
$[-R_{\max},R_{\max}]$. Thus $|A_i|\le2R_{\max}$ and the finite
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality gives
\begin{equation*}
    \left(\frac{Z_j}{n}\right)^2
    \le\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n A_i^2H_j(y_i)^2
    \le4R_{\max}^2\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n H_j(y_i)^2.
\end{equation*}
Expectation under the constructed conditional-i.i.d. group law proves
$\E[(Z_j/n)^2]\le4R_{\max}^2\mathcal E_j$.
This inequality uses the actual shared-baseline statistic; it does not
factor dependent sibling contributions. It is verified by
\path{rloo_estimator_square_bound},
\path{rloo_estimator_second_moment_bound}, and
\path{block_surrogate_second_moment}.

The common batch denominator cancels exactly:
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{D}{Kn}\left(\frac{\sum_b Z_{j,b}}{D}\right)
    =\frac1K\sum_b\frac{Z_{j,b}}n.
\end{equation*}
The right-hand side averages unbiased group contributions, independently
across the $K$ blocks, even when reward, length, and gate decisions are
dependent within blocks. Its variance is at most
$4R_{\max}^2\mathcal E_j/K$.
\path{corrected_objective_rescaling} combines the full ProMax objective
with this cancellation, and \path{rescaled_surrogate_unbiased} proves its
expectation using the actual block law.
\path{rescaled_surrogate_uniform_tail},
\path{rescaled_performance_from_estimate}, and
\path{rescaled_selected_false_improvement_bound} complete the simultaneous
confidence, performance, and selection guarantees.

Finally, a conditional energy bound $e(c)\le d_j^2$ implies
$\mathcal E_j\le Td_j^2$ by summing prefix expectations. A ratio bound
$|q_j/p-1|\le d_j$ need only hold on positive-$p$ actions; shared zero
actions impose no artificial requirement $d_j\ge1$.
\path{local_ratio_energy_of_bound} and
\path{family_ratio_energy_of_ratio_bound} prove this implication.
After absorbing EOS, both kernels put mass one on EOS and the energy is
zero. The resulting square-root horizon scaling concerns the statistical
margin only, and does not remove the separate policy-distribution-shift
cost from the reward bound.

\subsection{Exact Shared-Baseline Group Variance}
\label[appsec]{app:rloo-group-variance}

Fix a prompt and suppress its index. Write $X_i=R_i-\E[R_i]$,
$Y_i=H_j(y_i)$, $\theta=\E[XY]$, $a=\E[X^2]$, $b=\E[Y^2]$, and
$c=\E[X^2Y^2]$. Both $X$ and $Y$ have mean zero. Let
$S_X=\sum_i X_i$, $S_Y=\sum_i Y_i$, and $S_{XY}=\sum_i X_iY_i$.
For every realized group, expanding the leave-one-out sums gives
\begin{equation*}
    U=\frac{nS_{XY}-S_XS_Y}{n(n-1)}.
\end{equation*}
Reward translation leaves the RLOO statistic unchanged, so this equality
uses the actual reward estimator. These statements are checked by
\path{rloo_eq_sample_covariance} and \path{rloo_reward_translation}.

Direct calculation under the finite i.i.d. group law gives
\begin{align*}
    \E[S_{XY}^2]&=nc+n(n-1)\theta^2,\\
    \E[S_{XY}S_XS_Y]&=nc+n(n-1)\theta^2,\\
    \E[S_X^2S_Y^2]&=nc+n(n-1)(ab+2\theta^2).
\end{align*}
For example, add one independent pair $(X,Y)$ to the running sums.
Conditioning on the previous sums, expanding the fourth-degree polynomial,
and using $\E[X]=\E[Y]=0$ leaves the recurrence
\begin{equation*}
    \E[S_X^2S_Y^2]_{n+1}-\E[S_X^2S_Y^2]_n
       =c+2nab+4n\theta^2.
\end{equation*}
The mixed third-sum recurrence is $c+2n\theta^2$.
The finite product mass is decomposed exactly using
\path{iid_expect_cons}; \path{iid_sample_sum_square},
\path{iid_sum_product_cross}, and
\path{iid_centered_sums_mixed_square} prove the displayed moments by
induction. In particular, the terms from overlapping sample indices are
included, rather than discarded by an independence assumption.

Since $\E[U]=\theta$, expansion of
$\E[(nS_{XY}-S_XS_Y)^2]/[n^2(n-1)^2]-\theta^2$ yields
\begin{equation*}
    \Var(U)=\frac{c-\theta^2}{n}
              +\frac{ab+\theta^2}{n(n-1)}.
\end{equation*}
This is verified by \path{centered_rloo_variance} and
\path{actual_rloo_variance}. The first coefficient is $\Var(XY)$ and
the second is nonnegative; their signs are checked by
\path{covariance_variance_coefficients_nonneg}.
Under $|R|\le R_{\max}$, one has $|X|\le2R_{\max}$,
$a\le R_{\max}^2$, and $c\le4R_{\max}^2b$. The coefficient of
$\theta^2$ after collecting terms is $(2-n)/[n(n-1)]\le0$. Therefore
\begin{equation*}
    \Var(U)\le
       \left(\frac4n+\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\right)R_{\max}^2 b.
\end{equation*}
\path{actual_rloo_variance_bound} and
\path{rloo_variance_factor_rate} verify the bound and $c_n\le5/n$.

For the autoregressive model, the conditional covariance target is exactly
the prompt surrogate: $\theta_{j,x}=\mathcal S_{j,x}$, as verified by
\path{response_centered_covariance}. The same history-tree ratio energy
equals $b=\mathcal E_{j,x}$. Thus \path{response_rloo_variance_exact}
and \path{response_rloo_variance_bound} instantiate the generic identity
and bound on actual response groups.
Expanding $U_j-\mathcal S_j=(U_j-\mathcal S_{j,x})+
(\mathcal S_{j,x}-\mathcal S_j)$ makes the cross term vanish under the
conditional block law, giving
\begin{equation*}
    \Var(U_j)=\tau_j^2+\E_x[V_{j,x}(n)].
\end{equation*}
\path{block_surrogate_variance_exact} proves this equality directly from
the prompt/response product mass. \path{group_surrogate_mse_exact} then
proves the exact variance divided by $K$ for the full empirical estimator;
\path{group_surrogate_uniform_tail} and
\path{group_selected_false_improvement_bound} supply the sharper
confidence and selection guarantees. The bound taking the minimum with
the earlier energy-only estimate is checked by
\path{family_rloo_variance_combined_bound}.

\subsection{Policy Fitting Within a Fixed Cover}
\label[appsec]{app:policy-cover}

Fix the policy class, centers and assignment map in
\Cref{cor:covered-policy-certificate}. These objects may be constructed
conditionally on independent training data, but not chosen after observing
the evaluation blocks. Write $j=\nu(\theta)$ and let $U_\theta$ denote
the actual RLOO group mean. For every realized group,
\begin{align*}
    |U_\theta-U_{\theta_j}|
    &\le\frac1n\sum_i|R_i-b_i|
        |H_\theta(x,y_i)-H_{\theta_j}(x,y_i)|\\
    &\le2R_{\max}\zeta.
\end{align*}
The leave-one-out baseline remains the mean of the other responses in this
same group. No independence among the $n$ summands is used in this
deterministic bound. Averaging the $K$ groups preserves the bound. On the
population side, the previously proved RLOO/tree identity gives
\begin{equation*}
    |\mathcal S_\theta-\mathcal S_{\theta_j}|
      =|\E_p[R(H_\theta-H_{\theta_j})]|
      \le R_{\max}\zeta.
\end{equation*}
Consequently, for every sample batch and every class member,
\begin{equation*}
    |\widehat{\mathcal S}_\theta-\mathcal S_\theta|
    \le|\widehat{\mathcal S}_{\theta_j}-\mathcal S_{\theta_j}|
          +3R_{\max}\zeta.
\end{equation*}
The event that any member exceeds its covered margin is therefore a subset
of the finite union of center deviation events. Applying the exact group
variance bound proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:covered-policy-confidence}.
If a fitted policy satisfies the positive-improvement threshold but has
nonpositive true gain, the Adaptive lower bound forces it into that same
deviation event. This proves the selection claim for any function of the
evaluation data taking values in the fixed class, including continuous
parameter fitting. The finite sampling space makes this event well-defined
even for an infinite class index set.

The Lean proof in \path{formal/PolicyCover.lean} verifies the RLOO
perturbation bound, population comparison, uniform event transfer, and
reward lower bound. It also derives the sufficient radius $Td$ from the
finite token sum; the response-level cover is not inferred from an empirical
check on the observed batch. The connection to the implemented objective is
the same pointwise rescaling identity used in
\Cref{cor:promax-energy-certificate}; applying it to a fitted policy does
not require that policy to be independent of the batch.

\subsection{Certificate Calibration from Normalized Weights and Logits}
\label[appsec]{app:logit-calibration}

We prove \Cref{cor:logit-calibration} using finite normalized-weight
inequalities. They supply sufficient numerical bounds for the existing
certificate; the general reward and cover theorems retain their original
conditions.

\paragraph{Normalized-weight energy.}
Let $p$ be a finite probability mass function, let
$0<\ell\le v_a\le u$, and write $Z=\sum_a p_av_a$,
$q_a=p_av_a/Z$. Then $\ell\le Z\le u$ and $q$ is a probability
mass function with exactly the support of $p$.
Actions of zero $p$-mass contribute zero to all expectations below.
The pointwise inequality $(v_a-\ell)(u-v_a)\ge0$ implies
\begin{equation*}
    \E_p[v^2]\le(\ell+u)Z-\ell u.
\end{equation*}
Since $((\ell+u)Z-2\ell u)^2\ge0$, the right-hand side is at most
$(\ell+u)^2Z^2/(4\ell u)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
    \chi^2(q\|p)
    =\E_p[(v/Z-1)^2]
    =\frac{\E_p[v^2]}{Z^2}-1
    \le\frac{(u-\ell)^2}{4\ell u}=:C(\ell,u).
    \label{eq:normalized-tilt-energy}
\end{equation}
This energy bound is sharp: put probability $u/(\ell+u)$ on weight
$\ell$ and probability $\ell/(\ell+u)$ on weight $u$.
Then $Z=2\ell u/(\ell+u)$ and equality holds.

\paragraph{Reverse KL and TV.}
Dividing the pointwise quadratic inequality by $v_a>0$ and averaging
gives $Z+\ell u\E_p[1/v]\le\ell+u$. The nonnegative square
$(Z-\ell u\E_p[1/v])^2$ now yields
\begin{equation*}
    Z\E_p[1/v]\le\frac{(\ell+u)^2}{4\ell u}.
\end{equation*}
Using $\log x\le x-1$ for $x>0$, we obtain
\begin{equation*}
    D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)
    =\E_p[\log(Z/v)]
    \le Z\E_p[1/v]-1\le C(\ell,u).
\end{equation*}
Finally, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation*}
    \mathrm{TV}(p,q)
    =\tfrac12\E_p[|v/Z-1|]
    \le\tfrac12\sqrt{\E_p[(v/Z-1)^2]}
    \le\tfrac12\sqrt{C(\ell,u)}.
\end{equation*}
The sharpness claim above concerns the energy bound, not equality in
these additional KL and TV inequalities.

\paragraph{Softmax and common shifts.}
For $p_a=e^{z_a}/\sum_b e^{z_b}$ and
$q_a=e^{z'_a}/\sum_b e^{z'_b}$, direct cancellation gives
\begin{equation*}
    q_a=\frac{p_ae^{z'_a-z_a-c}}{\sum_b p_be^{z'_b-z_b-c}}
    \qquad\text{for every common shift }c\in\mathbb R.
\end{equation*}
If the logit increments lie in an interval $[c,c+\omega]$, their
centered exponentials lie in $[1,e^\omega]$. Thus
$C(1,e^\omega)=(e^\omega-1)^2/(4e^\omega)$.
The shift may depend on the context. This argument retains the full
softmax normalizer, so the bound is independent of the smallest positive
token probability. A shared masked support can be treated as the finite
action set at that row; weights on shared zero-probability actions may be
set to one in the normalized-tilt formulation. The argument does not
permit a change in sampling support.

\paragraph{History trees and the corrected ProMax statistic.}
Apply the local bounds at every prompt and history of length less than
$T$. The additive energy identity gives $\mathcal E_j\le TC_j$,
and averaging local TV under any rollout occupancy preserves the bound
$\epsilon_j=\sqrt{C_j}/2$. Substitution in the already proved Adaptive
bound gives $B_j\le\overline B_j$ from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:logit-certificate-inputs}. Finite prompt averaging
preserves both inequalities. Shared absorbing EOS transitions have zero
local energy and divergence; the uniform horizon bound may overestimate
their contribution.

For the actual RLOO group estimator, the previously proved second-moment
bound becomes $\E[(Z_j/n)^2]\le4R_{\max}^2TC_j$.
Independence of complete groups, Chebyshev's inequality, and a finite union
bound give Eq.~\eqref{eq:logit-confidence}. The exact observed-token
rescaling still identifies this estimator with
$D(\Delta_jS_{\mathrm{PM}}+\Delta_jG-\Delta_jN+
\Delta_jC_{\mathrm{clip}})/(Kn)$.
Combining its deviation bound with the population performance lower bound
proves the stated reward certificate. All gate, normalization, clipping,
random-length, and shared-baseline terms therefore retain their earlier
definitions. A sharper valid group-variance bound can replace this
second-moment estimate through \Cref{prop:rloo-group-variance}.

\paragraph{A sufficient logit-cover radius.}
Let a center $q'$ be a normalized tilt of rollout policy $p$ with
weights in $[\ell_0,u_0]$, and let a class member $q$ be a normalized
tilt of $q'$ with weights in $[\ell_1,u_1]$.
At each context, $q'_a/p_a\le u_0/\ell_0$ on positive-$p$ actions,
and $|v_a/\E_{q'}[v]-1|\le(u_1-\ell_1)/\ell_1$.
Consequently,
\begin{equation*}
    \left|\frac{q_a}{p_a}-\frac{q'_a}{p_a}\right|
    \le\frac{u_0}{\ell_0}\frac{u_1-\ell_1}{\ell_1}.
\end{equation*}
At shared zero-probability actions both totalized ratios are zero.
The triangle inequality over $T$ positions bounds the difference of
the additive response residuals by $T$ times this quantity.
Taking $(\ell_0,u_0)=(1,e^\omega)$ and
$(\ell_1,u_1)=(1,e^r)$ proves the radius $Te^\omega(e^r-1)$.
This provides a sufficient metric for the fixed cover in
\Cref{cor:covered-policy-certificate}; it does not establish how many
centers suffice for a given neural policy class.

\path{formal/LogitBounds.lean} verifies the normalized-weight inequalities,
the energy equality example, softmax normalization and shift invariance,
the token-to-response cover bound, and the finite-prompt composition with
the actual RLOO sampling and reward certificate.

\subsection{Objective Consistency of \texorpdfstring{$\gamma = 1.0$}{gamma = 1.0}}
\label[appsec]{app:gamma-one}

\begin{remark}[On Setting $\gamma = 1.0$ for Sparse Terminal Rewards]
For a response with $T$ active tokens and shaped reward $\tilde r$ assigned only
at the final token, the reward-to-go recurrence $G_t=r_t+\gamma G_{t+1}$ gives
\begin{equation*}
    G_t=\gamma^{T-t}\tilde r,\qquad 1\le t\le T.
\end{equation*}
Thus $\gamma=1$ gives $G_t=\tilde r$ at every active position, as required by
the undiscounted sequence-reward objective $J(\pitheta)=\E[R(x,y)]$ and the
token expansion in \Cref{sec:token-expand}. For nonzero $\tilde r$, requiring
this equality at every suffix length also forces $\gamma=1$.
Both claims are checked by \path{sparse_terminal_return_gamma_one} and
\path{gamma_one_iff_constant_sparse_return} in \path{formal/SparseReturn.lean}.
Choosing $0\le\gamma<1$ introduces position-dependent factors $\gamma^{T-t}$
and downweights earlier token contributions. Such an estimator generally no
longer targets the undiscounted policy gradient; identifying it with a
discounted-objective gradient additionally requires specifying the outer
time weights in the policy-gradient formula. See \citet{williams1992simple}
for background on REINFORCE.
\end{remark}


\subsection{The Normalization Mechanism for Actual RLOO Signals}
\label[appsec]{app:adaptive-mechanism}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{prop:binary-rloo-normalization}]
Let the group contain $k$ rewards $r_+$ and $m$ rewards $r_-$, with
$n=k+m\ge2$ and $\Delta=r_+-r_->0$. The reward sum is
$kr_++mr_-$. Subtracting the other-response mean gives
\[
 r_+-\frac{(k-1)r_++mr_-}{n-1}=\frac{m\Delta}{n-1}=a_+,
 \qquad
 r_--\frac{kr_++(m-1)r_-}{n-1}=-\frac{k\Delta}{n-1}=-a_-.
\]
Both magnitudes are strictly positive. Repeating each response advantage
at its active tokens gives positive and negative first-moment magnitudes
$Pa_+$ and $Na_-$, squared sums $Pa_+^2$ and $Na_-^2$, and nonzero count
$P+N$. Therefore
\[
 \kappa=\frac{Pa_+}{Na_-},\qquad
 D=Pa_+^2+\kappa^2Na_-^2
   =a_+^2\frac{P(P+N)}{N}.
\]
Since $\alpha=\sqrt{(P+N)/D}$ and $\beta=\alpha\kappa$,
\[
 \alpha a_+=\sqrt{N/P},\qquad
 \beta a_-=\sqrt{P/N}.
\]
Substituting the RLOO magnitudes also gives
\[
 \kappa=\frac{Pm}{Nk}=\frac{P/k}{N/m}
        =\frac{\bar T_+}{\bar T_-}.
\]
Multiplication by the token counts gives equal aggregate coefficient
magnitudes $P\sqrt{N/P}=N\sqrt{P/N}$.
All steps use the actual reward group and its response lengths.
\end{proof}

The finite construction in \path{formal/AdaptiveMechanism.lean} indexes
responses by the disjoint union of the higher- and lower-reward groups.
It computes the reward sum and the RLOO signals, then forms the positive,
negative and squared sums using arbitrary finite response-length weights.
\path{finite_binary_rloo_normalization} composes these steps with the
normalization map. The ratio and mass-balance identities are checked in
the same module. The older equal-amplitude algebra remains a special case;
it is not used to assume that binary RLOO advantages are $\pm1$.

\paragraph{What is balanced.}
Before normalization, the ratio of positive to negative absolute token
coefficient mass is
\[
 \frac{Pa_+}{Na_-}=\frac{\bar T_+}{\bar T_-}.
\]
Thus unequal average lengths directly produce the scalar mass imbalance;
class frequency alone does not, because RLOO already assigns different
magnitudes to the two classes. After normalization, the two total
coefficient magnitudes are equal. At the token level, $P\ll N$ gives
$|\hat A_+|\gg|\hat A_-|$, but this is not the assertion $\beta<1$.
For one success and nine equally long failures with rewards $1$ and $0$,
$\alpha=\beta=3$; the negative amplitude grows from $1/9$ to $1/3$.
The Lean counterexample
\path{rare_success_does_not_imply_negative_attenuation} checks these factors.
Importance weights, gates, and score vectors can change the final update,
so coefficient balance is not a gradient-vector balance theorem.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{prop:normalization-scale-invariance}]
For positive $c$, scaling $A$ to $cA$ preserves the two sign sets and the
nonzero-token count. The positive and negative first sums both acquire a
factor $c$, and both squared sums acquire a factor $c^2$. Consequently,
$\kappa$ is unchanged, $D$ becomes $c^2D$, and the ideal factors become
$\alpha/c$ and $\beta/c$. Their products with $cA$ equal the original
normalized advantages at every active position. The same calculation
holds with arbitrary fixed nonnegative token or response-length weights.

For a group of size $n\ge2$, direct rearrangement gives
\[
 r_i-\frac{1}{n}\sum_j r_j
 =\frac{n-1}{n}
   \left(r_i-\frac{\sum_j r_j-r_i}{n-1}\right).
\]
Apply scale invariance with $c=(n-1)/n>0$ to prove the baseline equivalence.
\end{proof}

The same Lean module proves all five finite weighted-moment transformations,
invariance of the resulting normalization map, and the equality obtained
from the two actual baseline formulas.
This equivalence assumes the same ideal normalization;
it does not compare different normalization rules or different prompt groups.
The independent-baseline identities and group-variance bounds elsewhere in
the paper apply to the raw RLOO surrogate before this data-dependent map.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{cor:binary-token-standardization}]
Write $a=a_+>0$ and $b=a_->0$. The token-weighted mean and population
variance are
\[
 \mu=\frac{Pa-Nb}{P+N},\qquad
 v=\frac{P(a-\mu)^2+N(-b-\mu)^2}{P+N}
   =\frac{PN(a+b)^2}{(P+N)^2}>0.
\]
The centered positive and negative coefficients are
$a-\mu=N(a+b)/(P+N)$ and $-b-\mu=-P(a+b)/(P+N)$.
Dividing by $\sqrt v$ gives $\sqrt{N/P}$ and $-\sqrt{P/N}$, respectively,
which are the asymmetric coefficients in
\Cref{eq:binary-normalized-amplitudes}. These variance and square-root
identities are checked by \path{two_level_token_zscore}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{A sign change with actual multivalued RLOO rewards.}
\label{par:multivalued-sign-example}
Consider three responses with rewards $(0,2/3,1)$ and active-token lengths
$(1,1,4)$. Their RLOO advantages and token-weighted moments are
\[
 (A_1,A_2,A_3)=(-5/6,1/6,2/3),\qquad
 \mu_{\mathrm{tok}}=1/3,\qquad v_{\mathrm{tok}}=11/36.
\]
Although $A_2=1/6>0$, its centered value is $A_2-\mu_{\mathrm{tok}}=-1/6$,
so its ordinary token-standardized coefficient is negative.
The ideal asymmetric coefficient is $\alpha A_2>0$ because $\alpha>0$.
Both sign classes are present, and every reward lies in $[0,1]$;
the example uses the actual leave-one-out formula and includes no
zero-advantage tokens. The finite RLOO values, weighted mean and variance,
negative standardized coefficient, and positive asymmetric coefficient
are checked in \path{formal/AdaptiveMechanism.lean}.
This illustrates a distinction for multivalued rewards, consistent with
the exact binary equivalence in \Cref{cor:binary-token-standardization}.

\paragraph{Connection to the numerical implementation.}
If the normalization branch is taken and the product and factor clamps are
inactive, the implementation replaces $D$ by $D+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}$.
The factor identity
\[
 \sqrt{\frac{P+N}{D+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}}}
 =\sqrt{\frac{D}{D+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}}}
  \sqrt{\frac{P+N}{D}}
\]
shows that the two normalized amplitudes have the same multiplicative
shrinkage; their relative factor ratio and zero-mean balance are preserved.
This identity is checked by \path{stabilizer_common_shrinkage}.
Under a reward rescaling, however, $D$ changes while the fixed numerical
stabilizer does not, so exact reward-scale invariance need not survive.
Clamps and fallbacks have the separate explicit formulas in
\Cref{prop:normalization-safeguards}.

\subsection{Numerical Stability}
\label[appsec]{app:numerical}

The adaptive normalization (\Cref{sec:adaptive-norm}) includes several safeguards:

\begin{remark}[Numerical Thresholds vs.\ Divergence Bounds]
The symbols $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}$ and $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$ in this appendix denote implementation hyperparameters used for
numerical stability and uniform-reward detection. They are unrelated to the divergence upper bound $\epsilon$ in \Cref{def:max-div}.
\end{remark}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sum threshold:} If $\sum_{(i,t)\in\mathcal{P}} A_{i,t} < \varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}$ or
    $-\sum_{(i,t)\in\mathcal{N}} A_{i,t} < \varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}$ (default $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}} = 10^{-8}$), normalization is
    skipped and the original advantages are returned.

    \item \textbf{Additive stabilizer:} The denominator inside the square root for $\alpha$ always includes
    $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}>0$. This changes the second moment even when every clamp is inactive.

    \item \textbf{Scale clamping:} $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are clamped to $[\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}, s_{\max}]$ (default $s_{\max} = 10$) to prevent extreme scaling.

    \item \textbf{Intermediate overflow protection:} The quantity
    $\kappa^2 \sum_{(i,t)\in\mathcal{N}} A_{i,t}^2$ (where $\kappa$ is defined in \Cref{def:adaptive-norm}) is clamped to $10^8$ before
    computing $\alpha$.

    \item \textbf{Finiteness check:} If $\alpha$ or $\beta$ is NaN or $\pm\infty$ after computation, normalization is skipped.
\end{enumerate}

The following proposition quantifies how these safeguards change the ideal
empirical constraints. A small deviation requires corresponding bounds on
the stabilizer and clamp residuals; it is not automatic from the presence of
these safeguards. The defaults remain hyperparameters whose empirical
suitability must be evaluated separately.

\begin{proposition}[Moment Residuals of the Implemented Normalization]
\label{prop:normalization-safeguards}
Suppose both active token-sign groups are nonempty and all inputs are finite.
Write
\[
P=\sum_{\mathcal P}A_{i,t},\quad N=-\sum_{\mathcal N}A_{i,t},\quad
Q_+=\sum_{\mathcal P}A_{i,t}^2,\quad Q_-=\sum_{\mathcal N}A_{i,t}^2,
\]
and let $m=|\mathcal P|+|\mathcal N|$, $\kappa=P/N$,
$D=Q_++\kappa^2Q_-$, $\eta=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{num}}>0$.
Before factor clamping, the implemented scales are
\[
D_c=Q_++\min(\kappa^2Q_-,C)+\eta,\qquad
\alpha_0=\sqrt{m/D_c},\qquad \beta_0=\alpha_0\kappa,
\]
where $C=10^8$ is the intermediate cap. Their empirical mean is zero and
their empirical second moment is $D/D_c$. In particular, if
$\kappa^2Q_-\le C$, then the empirical variance $v_0$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    v_0=\frac{D}{D+\eta}<1,\qquad
    1-v_0=\frac{\eta}{D+\eta}\le\frac{\eta}{D}.
    \label{eq:normalization-stabilizer-deficit}
\end{equation}
In general,
\[
    \frac{D}{D_c}-1
    =\frac{\kappa^2Q_- -\min(\kappa^2Q_-,C)-\eta}{D_c},
\]
so the product cap can change the second moment in either direction relative to one.
Let $a,b$ be the final clamped scales and let $\mu,v_2$ be the resulting
empirical mean and second moment on the $m$ nonzero active tokens. Then
\begin{align*}
    \mu&=\frac{aP-bN}{m},
    &|\mu|&\le\frac{|a-\alpha_0|P+|b-\beta_0|N}{m},\\
    v_2&=\frac{a^2Q_++b^2Q_-}{m},
    &\left|v_2-\frac{D}{D_c}\right|
    &\le\frac{|a-\alpha_0||a+\alpha_0|Q_+
               +|b-\beta_0||b+\beta_0|Q_-}{m}.
\end{align*}
The empirical variance after factor clamping is $v_2-\mu^2$.
Positive clamping bounds preserve advantage signs and within-sign ordering.
These statements describe the normalization branch in exact real arithmetic;
when a fallback is taken, the active advantages are unchanged.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Partition the active index set into positive, negative, and zero advantages.
The scaled first and second sums are $aP-bN$ and $a^2Q_++b^2Q_-$.
Substituting $\beta_0=\alpha_0P/N$ gives zero first sum, and
$\alpha_0^2=m/D_c$ gives second moment $D/D_c$.
The stabilizer deficit and product-cap identity follow by subtracting one.
For the factor-clamp bounds, use
\[
aP-bN=(a-\alpha_0)P-(b-\beta_0)N
\]
and $a^2-\alpha_0^2=(a-\alpha_0)(a+\alpha_0)$, with the analogous identity
for $b$, and apply the triangle inequality. Positivity of the clamped
factors gives sign and within-sign order preservation.
The finite-index moment identities, stabilizer and cap formulas, positive
clamps, and residual bounds are verified in
\path{formal/NormalizationSafeguards.lean}.
\end{proof}

\textbf{Fallback conditions.} Normalization is skipped entirely when:
\begin{itemize}
    \item One sign group is empty ($\mathcal{P} = \emptyset$ or $\mathcal{N} = \emptyset$). Assume, as in the token expansion
    convention above, that every sampled response contributes at least one active action token. Under the exact RLOO construction,
    the shaped rewards sum to zero within a prompt group, so this condition implies that every shaped reward is zero. The normalization
    is therefore skipped because its positive/negative constraints are infeasible; no gradient signal is created by this branch.
    \item The uniform-scale mode (\Cref{app:uniform-scale}) is active and the implementation's reward-uniformity
    tolerance is satisfied. In this branch the RLOO signal is intentionally replaced by the explicitly defined
    uniform-scale signal; the tolerance can include near-uniform rather than exactly equal rewards.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Uniform Scale Mode}
\label[appsec]{app:uniform-scale}

The uniform scale mode is an optional rule for using homogeneous reward groups.
When the implementation's reward-uniformity test is satisfied (exact equality
is the ideal sparse-reward case; the configured tolerance can also include
near-uniform rewards), the branch replaces the leave-one-out signal by the
explicit rule below and skips adaptive normalization. For exactly identical
rewards, the ordinary leave-one-out baseline would yield $\tilde{r}_i = 0$
for all $i$. The replacement gives nonzero coefficients only when the common
reward is nonzero; an all-zero group remains zero. A nonzero coefficient
alone does not establish a useful expected update. The population result
below identifies a sufficient reward convention and its limitations.
This statement is for the KL-free sparse-reward configuration: if an additional
per-token KL or other shaping term is enabled, the post-shaping returns need not
remain uniform and the branch must be analyzed with those augmented returns.

\begin{definition}[Uniform Scale]
For prompt groups where
$\mathrm{std}(\{r_1, \ldots, r_n\}) < \varepsilon_{\mathrm{tol}}$, the implementation
classifies the group as uniform-scale eligible, sets the shaped reward to
$\tilde{r}_i = r_i / n$, and skips adaptive normalization. Exact equality of
the rewards is a special case, not an additional premise of the scalar result.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Signed coefficient in uniform-scale mode]
\label{prop:uniform-coefficient-sign}
In the uniform-scale branch, a tolerance-selected reward group uses $A_i=r_i/n$ before the
actual ProMax loss is evaluated.  For an active token, write $a_t\ge0$ for
the action-mask/reduction weight, $M_t\in\{0,1\}$ for the detached prefix
gate, $w_t\ge0$ for the detached rollout-to-snapshot weight, and
$u_t=\exp(\log\pitheta-\log\piold)$ for the differentiable PPO ratio.  Away
from the two PPO clipping kinks, the derivative of the scalar loss with
respect to the current log-probability is
\[
 -a_tM_tw_tu_t\,\frac{r_i}{n}\,I_t,
\]
where $I_t=1$ on the branch through which the current ratio differentiates
and $I_t=0$ when clipping or the detached gate removes that derivative.  In
particular, whenever $a_t,M_t,w_t,u_t>0$ and the unclipped branch is active,
the coefficient has the same sign as $-r_i$ (the sign for gradient descent
therefore reinforces positive rewards and suppresses negative rewards).
For $n>0$, $r_i/n$ is positive, zero, or negative exactly when $r_i$ is
positive, zero, or negative.  This is a statement about each retained
scalar score coefficient; it does not claim a coordinate-wise or full-vector
gradient-direction guarantee.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $\tilde{r}_i = r_i/n$, the implemented scalar token loss is the
masked, importance-weighted PPO term, so its derivative must retain the
prefix gate, detached IS coefficient, and clipping branch.  The finite
real-arithmetic derivative in the non-kink regions is checked by
\path{ppoTerm_hasDerivAt} and \path{gated_ppo_hasFDerivAt} in
\path{formal/PPODerivative.lean}; the finite sum over action positions is
checked by \path{finite_gated_ppo_hasFDerivAt}.  The simpler sign fact for
the uniform coefficient is \path{uniform_scale_token_coefficient_sign}.
Thus a positive reward reinforces the retained sampled score contribution
and a negative reward suppresses it, subject to the gate and clipping
branch.  The exact effect on individual parameter coordinates depends on
the score vectors.
\end{proof}


\begin{proposition}[Population effect of the optional uniform-scale branch]
\label{prop:uniform-population}
Fix a prompt and a differentiable finite response policy $p_\theta$, with
rewards in $\{r_-,r_+\}$, $r_-<r_+$, and $n\ge2$ independent responses.
Write $J_x(\theta)=\mathbb E_{p_\theta}[R]$,
$s(\theta)=\Pr_{p_\theta}(R=r_+)$ and
$u_\theta(y)=\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(y)$, with the zero-mass score
convention of the finite model. Assume the uniformity test selects exactly
the homogeneous groups. At the on-policy point, with all prefix gates
accepted and PPO clipping inactive, compare the sequence-score statistics
\begin{equation*}
    g=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\hat A_i u_\theta(y_i)
\end{equation*}
with uniform scale on and off. The mixed-group coefficients are identical
in both cases and may include the actual asymmetric normalization and
safeguards; coefficients are held fixed when computing the score update.
Then
\begin{align}
    \E[g_{\mathrm{on}}-g_{\mathrm{off}}]
      &=\lambda_n(s)\nabla_\theta s,
      \label{eq:uniform-population}\\
    \lambda_n(s)
      &:=\frac{r_+s^{n-1}-r_-(1-s)^{n-1}}{n}.
      \nonumber
\end{align}
Since $\nabla_\theta J_x=(r_+-r_-)\nabla_\theta s$, the additional
expected term is aligned with the per-prompt reward gradient whenever
$r_+s^{n-1}\ge r_-(1-s)^{n-1}$. In particular, $r_-\le0\le r_+$
is sufficient. For $0/1$ rewards, the extra term is
$s^{n-1}\nabla_\theta J_x/n$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
RLOO is zero on a homogeneous group, including after its ordinary
normalization fallback. Turning uniform scale on replaces that zero by
$r_+/n$ on the all-high event and $r_-/n$ on the all-low event; other
groups are unchanged. The independent response law gives
\begin{align*}
    \E\!\left[\mathbb I_{\mathrm{all\ high}}\sum_i u_\theta(y_i)\right]
      &=n s^{n-1}\nabla_\theta s,\\
    \E\!\left[\mathbb I_{\mathrm{all\ low}}\sum_i u_\theta(y_i)\right]
      &=-n(1-s)^{n-1}\nabla_\theta s.
\end{align*}
These identities follow by separating each response from its $n-1$
siblings in the finite product law. The success-restricted score
expectation equals $\nabla_\theta s$ by finite policy differentiation;
the failure-restricted expectation is its negative because the complete
score has mean zero. Multiplying by the reward and the two factors $1/n$
proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:uniform-population}.
\end{proof}

This is a fixed-prompt, on-policy mechanism statement. The statistic sums
each response's token scores before any random total-token reduction.
If every response has the same deterministic active length $L$, a global
token mean multiplies both sides by $1/L$. A random denominator, off-policy
weights, action-dependent masks, or active PPO clipping require the
corrections in the general objective and reward certificate. Moreover,
different prompts generally have different $\lambda_n(s)$, so the
fixed-prompt result does not establish alignment after summing gradients
of shared parameters across prompts. The result concerns the extra term;
it does not assert that every finite stochastic optimizer step improves
reward. When $0<s<1$, the probability of an all-success group is $s^n$,
and all-failure $0/1$ groups still produce zero coefficients.

\begin{proposition}[Complete binary update under class-dependent lengths]
\label{prop:binary-complete-update}
Consider the same finite on-policy model with $n=m+1\ge2$ iid responses,
but now include the actual token reduction. Assume that every response with
label $q\in\{0,1\}$ has a deterministic active length $L_q$ satisfying
$0<L_q\le L_{\max}$. Let the positive/negative factors be computed from
the length-weighted RLOO advantages using the implemented additive
stabilizer, intermediate product cap, factor clamps and identity fallback,
with numerical lower clamp $\eta\in(0,1]$. Let $H_i$ be the resulting
coefficient of the response score after division by the sampled total active
length. On-policy, with every prefix gate accepted and PPO clipping
inactive, the complete expected sequence-score update satisfies
\begin{equation*}
    \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_i(Y_{1:n})
        u_\theta(Y_i)\right]
      = \Lambda\,\nabla_\theta s,
    \qquad
    \Lambda\ge \frac{\eta}{L_{\max}}>0,
\end{equation*}
where $s=\Pr_{p_\theta}(R=1)$. This holds both with uniform-scale
disabled and with the optional $0/1$ uniform-scale replacement on homogeneous
groups. The multiplier may depend on $p_\theta$, the sibling count and the
lengths, but it is common to every parameter direction.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a fixed coordinate $i$, condition on the $m$ sibling labels. The
implemented factors and the total token denominator are then fixed for the
two possible labels of $Y_i$. The finite product law and the zero-mean score
identity give the exact coordinate factorization
\[
 \mathbb E[H_i(Y_{1:n})u_\theta(Y_i)]
 =\left(\sum_z p_\theta^{\otimes m}(z)\,[H_i(1,z)-H_i(0,z)]\right)
   \mathbb E[\mathbf 1_{\{R=1\}}u_\theta(Y)].
\]
For the binary RLOO signal, the sibling mean lies in $[0,1]$. Positive
factor clamps are at least $\eta$, while every sampled total length is at
most $nL_{\max}$. Therefore each contrast is at least
$\eta/(nL_{\max})$. The $n$ coordinates give
$\Lambda\ge\eta/L_{\max}$. On a homogeneous all-success group, the
uniform replacement adds $1/(n\sum_iL_{Y_i})$ and has nonnegative contrast;
on an all-failure $0/1$ group it adds zero. Thus the lower bound is preserved.
The finite conditioning, factorization, safeguard lower bound and the
resulting common multiplier are checked by
\path{coordinate_expectation}, \path{complete_update_expectation}, and
\path{implementation_policy_direction} in \path{formal/BinaryUpdate.lean}.
The label-dependent-length premise is explicit: if response length also
depends on within-class content, the coefficient is no longer a function of
the sibling labels alone and an additional covariance/error term is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Within-class length covariance decomposition]
\label{prop:binary-length-covariance}
Drop the class-dependent-length premise and let $C_i(y_{1:n})$ be the actual
response-score coefficient produced by the implementation, including its
length-weighted normalization, total-token denominator and optional
uniform-scale term. For a fixed direction $v$, write
$f_v(y)=\langle v,u_\theta(y)\rangle$. Assume both binary response classes
have positive finite probability. Define $\Lambda_C$ as the sum over
coordinates and sibling draws of the difference between the conditional
class means of $C_i$, and define
$\mathcal E_v$ as the corresponding weighted within-class covariance between
$C_i$ and $f_v$. Then the exact identity is
\begin{equation*}
 \mathbb E\!\left[\sum_i C_i(Y_{1:n})f_v(Y_i)\right]
   = \Lambda_C\,\mathbb E[\mathbf 1_{\{R=1\}}f_v(Y)] + \mathcal E_v,
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
 |\mathcal E_v|\le
 \sqrt{V_C\,n\,V_{f,v}},
\end{equation*}
Here, writing $Z$ for the $n-1$ independent sibling responses and inserting
$Y$ at coordinate $i$, define
$\bar C_i(q,Z):=\mathbb E[C_i(Y,Z)\mid R(Y)=q,Z]$ and
$\bar f_v(q):=\mathbb E[f_v(Y)\mid R(Y)=q]$. The variances are
\begin{align*}
 V_C&:=\sum_i\mathbb E_{Z,Y}
       [(C_i(Y,Z)-\bar C_i(R(Y),Z))^2],\\
 V_{f,v}&:=\mathbb E_Y[(f_v(Y)-\bar f_v(R(Y)))^2].
\end{align*}
Therefore
the complete update is guaranteed to have the same directional sign as the
reward gradient whenever the positive main term dominates this explicit
covariance margin. The bound does not assume independence between length,
normalization and the response score.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Condition on the sibling responses and split the current response support
into the two reward classes. Subtracting and adding the conditional class
means of both $C_i$ and $f_v$ gives the displayed identity; the terms with
one centered factor vanish within each class. Applying the finite
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality to the remaining product gives the square-root
bound. The exact decomposition is checked by
\path{general_update_decomposition}, its finite covariance bound by
\path{covariance_error_abs_bound}, and the implementation-specific
coefficient is connected by \path{response_update_decomposition} in
\path{formal/BinaryLength.lean}. If lengths are deterministic within each
class, the coefficient is sibling-label measurable and $\mathcal E_v=0$,
recovering \Cref{prop:binary-complete-update}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Local true-reward improvement of the complete binary update]
\label{cor:binary-local-improvement}
Fix a prompt, binary rewards $R\in\{0,1\}$, and $n\ge2$ iid responses
from the finite differentiable policy above, with parameters in a real
Hilbert space and a policy that is nonnegative and normalized in a
neighborhood of $\theta$. Assume $0<s(\theta)<1$, where
$s(\theta)=\Pr_{p_\theta}(R=1)$. At the on-policy point, let every prefix
gate accept and PPO clipping be inactive. Allow arbitrary content-dependent
active lengths $0<L(y)\le L_{\max}$ and retain the actual stabilizer,
product cap, factor clamps and identity fallback. The lower factor clamp
is $0<\eta\le1$ and the upper clamp is at least $\eta$.
The optional uniform-scale branch may be enabled or disabled; when enabled,
assume its uniformity test selects exactly homogeneous groups.
Using the actual response-score coefficients $C_i$ and variances in
\Cref{prop:binary-length-covariance}, set
\begin{equation*}
 g:=\nabla s(\theta),\qquad
 U:=\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_{i=1}^n C_i(Y_{1:n})u_\theta(Y_i)\right],
 \qquad f_g(y):=\langle g,u_\theta(y)\rangle.
\end{equation*}
The actual class multiplier satisfies $\Lambda_C\ge\eta/L_{\max}>0$, and
\begin{align*}
 \langle g,U\rangle
 &\ge \Lambda_C\|g\|^2-\sqrt{V_C\,n\,V_{f,g}}\\
 &\ge \frac{\eta}{L_{\max}}\|g\|^2-\sqrt{V_C\,n\,V_{f,g}}.
\end{align*}
If $\sqrt{V_C\,n\,V_{f,g}}<\Lambda_C\|g\|^2$, there exists
$r>0$ such that every $0<t<r$ satisfies
\begin{equation*}
 s(\theta+tU)>s(\theta).
\end{equation*}
The simpler condition
$\sqrt{V_C\,n\,V_{f,g}}<\eta\|g\|^2/L_{\max}$ is sufficient but can
be more conservative than using the actual $\Lambda_C$.
Under the class-dependent-length premise of
\Cref{prop:binary-complete-update}, $g\ne0$ alone suffices for this local
improvement: $U=\Lambda g$ with $\Lambda\ge\eta/L_{\max}>0$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For each fixed sibling draw, the sibling mean reward lies in $[0,1]$.
Positive normalization factors are bounded below by $\eta$, and the sampled
total length is at most $nL_{\max}$. Averaging the resulting success/failure
coefficient contrast within each class gives
$\Lambda_C\ge\eta/L_{\max}$ even when length varies with content.
The optional all-success replacement adds a nonnegative contrast.
Apply \Cref{prop:binary-length-covariance} with $v=g$ and use the
finite-policy identity
$\mathbb E[\mathbf 1_{\{R=1\}}f_g(Y)]=\|g\|^2$ to obtain the displayed
lower bound. A positive right-hand side implies
$\left.\frac{d}{dt}s(\theta+tU)\right|_{t=0}=\langle g,U\rangle>0$.
By differentiability, the difference quotient is positive for every
sufficiently small positive $t$, proving strict improvement.
For class-dependent lengths, \Cref{prop:binary-complete-update} directly
gives $\langle g,U\rangle=\Lambda\|g\|^2>0$ whenever $g\ne0$.
The multiplier bound is checked by
\path{response_class_multiplier_lower_bound} in
\path{formal/BinaryLength.lean}; the gradient connection, directional bound
and both local improvement statements are checked by
\path{success_gradient_correct}, \path{population_ascent_bound},
\path{population_local_improvement}, \path{response_local_improvement}
and \path{class_length_local_improvement}
in \path{formal/BinaryAscent.lean}.
\end{proof}

This result concerns a sufficiently small step along the complete population
update at a fixed prompt. It supplies no numerical step-size radius and does
not imply improvement for every stochastic or Adam step. Arbitrary
off-policy updates, active gates and clipping remain covered by the explicit
correction terms in \Cref{thm:promax-certificate}.

\paragraph{Reward translation and a complete counterexample.}
Adding a common offset $c$ to both reward levels preserves the reward
gradient and all raw RLOO signals, but changes the extra multiplier by
\begin{equation*}
    \lambda_n^{\mathrm{shifted}}(s)-\lambda_n(s)
      =\frac{c}{n}\bigl(s^{n-1}-(1-s)^{n-1}\bigr).
\end{equation*}
For an explicit example, use two one-token responses per group,
$p_\theta(\mathrm{high})=\theta$,
$p_\theta(\mathrm{low})=1-\theta$, and rewards $4$ and $3$.
At $\theta=1/10$, $J(\theta)=3+\theta$, so $J'(\theta)=1$.
Mixed-group RLOO coefficients are $+1,-1$; their ideal normalization
leaves them unchanged. The implemented real-arithmetic safeguards instead
multiply both by
\begin{equation*}
    a_\varepsilon
      =\min\!\left\{10,\max\!\left\{\varepsilon,
                  \sqrt{\frac{2}{2+\varepsilon}}\right\}\right\},
    \qquad 0<\varepsilon\le1,
\end{equation*}
so $0<a_\varepsilon\le1$; the product cap is inactive. The mixed rewards
have sample variance $1/2$, hence their standard deviation exceeds the
implementation's $10^{-8}$ uniformity tolerance, while homogeneous groups
have zero standard deviation. Thus this example follows the actual branch
selection. Direct expectation under the two-response product law gives
\begin{equation}
    \E[g_{\mathrm{off}}]=a_\varepsilon>0,
    \qquad
    \E[g_{\mathrm{on}}]=a_\varepsilon-\frac{23}{20}
       \le-\frac{3}{20}<0.
    \label{eq:uniform-offset-counterexample}
\end{equation}
This is an expected ascent-direction reversal in a valid bounded-reward
model, at an on-policy point with accepted gates and inactive clipping.
It does not require a floating-point failure. With rewards $0,1$ in the
same model, the corresponding expectation is
$a_\varepsilon+\theta/2>0$ for $0<\theta<1$.
The optional rule is therefore tied to the reward origin; these two reward
conventions cannot be treated as equivalent ablations.

\paragraph{A constant reward carries no population signal.}
If every response in the policy support has reward $c$, uniform scale
produces $g=(c/n^2)\sum_i u_\theta(y_i)$.
The expected score is zero, so $\E[g]=0$, as required by the constant
reward objective. For every parameter direction $v$, independence gives
\begin{equation*}
    \Var(\langle v,g\rangle)
      =\frac{c^2}{n^3}\E_p[\langle v,u_\theta(Y)\rangle^2].
\end{equation*}
Ordinary RLOO is identically zero in this setting. The optional branch
can therefore introduce noise without useful population signal, while
$c=0$ leaves the entire update zero.
The finite product-law expectation, differential connection, offset
counterexample with stabilizer/clamp, and directional variance are checked
in \path{formal/UniformScale.lean}.

\subsection{Proof of \texorpdfstring{\Cref{thm:prefix-tighter}}{Theorem}}
\label[appsec]{app:prefix-proof}

We provide a complete proof of \Cref{thm:prefix-tighter} under the definitions in \Cref{sec:prefix-is}. For convenience, write the
log-threshold interval as
\begin{equation*}
    [\log(1-\epslow), \log(1+\epshigh)] = [-\tau_-, \tau_+],
    \qquad
    \tau_+ := \log(1+\epshigh),
    \qquad
    \tau_- := -\log(1-\epslow),
\end{equation*}
so that $\rho_t \in [1-\epslow,1+\epshigh]$ is equivalent to $\log\rho_t \in [-\tau_-,\tau_+]$.

\paragraph{Mask Definitions} The three masks compared in \Cref{thm:prefix-tighter} are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Token-level (IcePop / token-level ratio mask):}
    \begin{equation*}
        M_t^{\mathrm{tok}}
        := \mathbb{I}\!\big[\rho_t \in [1-\epslow,1+\epshigh]\big]
        = \mathbb{I}\!\big[\log\rho_t \in [-\tau_-,\tau_+]\big].
    \end{equation*}
    \item \textbf{Sequence-level (GSPO / sequence-level ratio gate):}
    \begin{equation*}
        M_t^{\mathrm{seq}}
        := \mathbb{I}\!\big[\log\rhoseq \in [-\tau_-,\tau_+]\big],
        \qquad
        \log\rhoseq := \frac{1}{T}\sum_{s=1}^{T}\log\rho_s,
    \end{equation*}
    for all $t$.
    \item \textbf{Prefix-level (Causal Trust Region proxy):}
    \begin{equation*}
        M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}
        := \mathbb{I}\!\big[\rhoprefix{t} \in [1-\epslow,1+\epshigh]\big]
        = \mathbb{I}\!\big[\log\rhoprefix{t} \in [-\tau_-,\tau_+]\big],
    \end{equation*}
    where $\rhoprefix{t}$ is defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:prefix-is}.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:prefix-tighter}]
\noindent\textbf{Part (a): Early deviation.}
Assume $\rho_1 \notin [1-\epslow,1+\epshigh]$ and $|\log\rho_t| \le \varepsilon$ for all $t>1$, with
$0 \le \varepsilon \le \min(\tau_+,\tau_-)$. Then for every $t>1$,
\begin{equation*}
    \log\rho_t \in [-\varepsilon,\varepsilon] \subseteq [-\tau_-,\tau_+],
\end{equation*}
so $M_t^{\mathrm{tok}}=1$ for all $t>1$, while $M_1^{\mathrm{tok}}=0$ (since $\rho_1 \notin [1-\epslow,1+\epshigh]$). Hence token-level
IcePop masks only position $t=1$.

\smallskip
For the prefix mask, note first that $M_1^{\mathrm{pre}}=0$ because $\rhoprefix{1}=\rho_1\notin[1-\epslow,1+\epshigh]$. To quantify how
long the rejection can persist, use Eq.~\eqref{eq:prefix-is}:
\begin{equation*}
    \log\rhoprefix{t}
    = \frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t} \log\rho_s
    = \frac{1}{t}\left(\log\rho_1 + \sum_{s=2}^{t}\log\rho_s\right).
\end{equation*}
If $\log\rho_1>\tau_+$, then $\log\rho_s \ge -\varepsilon$ for all $s\ge2$, hence $\sum_{s=2}^{t}\log\rho_s \ge -(t-1)\varepsilon$ and therefore
\begin{align*}
    \log\rhoprefix{t}
    &\ge \frac{1}{t}\big(\log\rho_1-(t-1)\varepsilon\big)
    = \frac{\log\rho_1+\varepsilon}{t} - \varepsilon.
\end{align*}
Thus, if $t < (\log\rho_1+\varepsilon)/(\tau_+ + \varepsilon)$, then $\log\rhoprefix{t}>\tau_+$ and hence $M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}=0$.
The lower-violation case $\log\rho_1<-\tau_-$ is symmetric: since $\log\rho_s \le \varepsilon$ for $s\ge2$, we have
$\sum_{s=2}^{t}\log\rho_s \le \varepsilon(t-1)$ and therefore
\begin{align*}
    \log\rhoprefix{t}
    &\le \frac{1}{t}\big(\log\rho_1+\varepsilon(t-1)\big)
    = \frac{\log\rho_1-\varepsilon}{t} + \varepsilon.
\end{align*}
If $t < (-\log\rho_1+\varepsilon)/(\tau_-+\varepsilon)$, then $\log\rhoprefix{t} < -\tau_-$ and hence $M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}=0$.
This proves the sufficient conditions in \Cref{eq:early-upper-sufficient,eq:early-lower-sufficient}.

\par
\noindent\textbf{Part (b): Late deviation.}
Assume there exists $t^*\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$ such that $\rho_{t^*}\notin[1-\epslow,1+\epshigh]$ and $|\log\rho_t|\le \varepsilon$
for all $t\neq t^*$, with
$0 \le \varepsilon \le \min(\tau_+,\tau_-)$. For any $t<t^*$, all active log-ratios in the prefix satisfy $\log\rho_s \in [-\tau_-,\tau_+]$.
Since $\log\rhoprefix{t} = \frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}\log\rho_s$ is an average of values in $[-\tau_-,\tau_+]$, it follows that
$\log\rhoprefix{t} \in [-\tau_-,\tau_+]$, and hence $M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}=1$ for all $t<t^*$. Therefore prefix masking can only occur
for positions $t\ge t^*$, which implies
\begin{equation*}
    \big|\{t : M_t^{\mathrm{pre}} = 0\}\big| \le T - t^* + 1.
\end{equation*}
The sequence-level IS mask $M_t^{\mathrm{seq}}$ is constant across positions by definition, so it either rejects all tokens or rejects none.

For the claimed regime where prefix rejects but sequence-level accepts, consider the pure single-spike case where $\rho_t=1$ for
$t\neq t^*$ and $\rho_{t^*}>1$. Then
\[
    \log\rhoseq = \frac{1}{T}\log\rho_{t^*},
    \qquad
    \log\rhoprefix{t^*} = \frac{1}{t^*}\log\rho_{t^*}.
\]
Since $t^* \le T$ and $\log\rho_{t^*}>0$, we have $\log\rhoprefix{t^*} \ge \log\rhoseq$. Thus whenever
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{1}{t^*}\log\rho_{t^*} > \tau_+
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \frac{1}{T}\log\rho_{t^*} \le \tau_+,
\end{equation*}
we obtain $M_{t^*}^{\mathrm{pre}}=0$ while $M_t^{\mathrm{seq}}=1$ for all $t$.
For every $t^*<T$, the explicit choice
$\log\rho_{t^*}=\tau_+(t^*+T)/2$ satisfies these inequalities, since
\[
    \tau_+ < \tau_+\frac{t^*+T}{2t^*},
    \qquad
    0 < \tau_+\frac{t^*+T}{2T} < \tau_+.
\]
For the lower threshold, choose $\log\rho_{t^*}=-\tau_-(t^*+T)/2$. Then
\[
    -\tau_-\frac{t^*+T}{2t^*}< -\tau_-,
    \qquad
    -\tau_- < -\tau_-\frac{t^*+T}{2T}<0,
\]
which again rejects the spike under the prefix mask and accepts the whole
sequence under the sequence mask. Both constructions have only one
out-of-bound token. When $t^*=T$, the two statistics at that position are
identical for every trajectory, so strict separation there is impossible.

\par
\noindent\textbf{Part (c): Sustained one-sided violation.}
If $\log\rho_s > \tau_+$ for all $s$, then for every $t$ the prefix average satisfies $\log\rhoprefix{t} > \tau_+$, since it is an
average of values each strictly larger than $\tau_+$. Each token mask also rejects directly by its defining interval test.
Hence $M_t^{\mathrm{tok}}=M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}=0$ for all $t$. The sequence-level average
$\log\rhoseq$ is also strictly larger than $\tau_+$, so $M_t^{\mathrm{seq}}=0$ for all $t$. The lower-violation case $\log\rho_s < -\tau_-$
is symmetric.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Complete Finite-Tree Adaptive Bound}
\label[appsec]{app:adaptive-proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:adaptive-bound}]
Fix a prompt and write $p=\piroll$, $q=\pitheta$. For a history $h$ with
$n$ actions remaining, let $V_n^p(h)$ and $V_n^q(h)$ be the respective
expected terminal rewards. Let $S_n(h)$ be the rollout-occupancy surrogate
for this continuation, and set $E_n(h)=V_n^q(h)-V_n^p(h)-S_n(h)$.
Its local surrogate gain is
$\sum_a(q(a\mid h)-p(a\mid h))V_n^p(ha)$.
Expanding the normalized policy recursions and collecting terms gives the
exact identity
\begin{align*}
    E_{n+1}(h)
    &=\sum_a p(a\mid h)E_n(ha)+I_n(h),\\
    I_n(h)
    &:=\sum_a(q(a\mid h)-p(a\mid h))
       \bigl(V_n^q(ha)-V_n^p(ha)\bigr).
\end{align*}
Here $E_0(h)=0$. In particular, the recursion uses rollout probabilities in
its outer expectation; no policy-occupancy substitution is assumed.

For either continuation starting at $ha$, bounded reward gives
\begin{equation*}
    |V_n^q(ha)-V_n^p(ha)|
    \le 2R_{\max}\,
       \DTV\bigl(P^p(\cdot\mid ha),P^q(\cdot\mid ha)\bigr).
\end{equation*}
The continuation TV is at most $1$ by normalization. Applying the
expected-token-TV sum bound to this re-rooted tree gives $n\epsilon$.
The KL chain rule on the same tree gives continuation reverse KL at most
$n\delta$, so Pinsker gives the third bound $\sqrt{n\delta/2}$.
All required local contexts have length less than $T$; there is no
assumption on divergences beyond the generation horizon. Define
$b_n=\min(1,n\epsilon,\sqrt{n\delta/2})$. Then
\begin{equation*}
    |I_n(h)|
    \le 2R_{\max}b_n\sum_a|q(a\mid h)-p(a\mid h)|
    =4R_{\max}b_n\,\Dtvtok(h;p,q).
\end{equation*}
Unrolling the error recursion from $E_T(\varnothing)$ yields a sum of
these local interactions, averaged over rollout histories. At position
$t$, the continuation has $T-t$ actions. Taking absolute values and then
averaging over the finite prompt distribution therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
    |\mathrm{Error}(\pitheta)|
    \le 4R_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Dbar_t\,b_{T-t}.
\end{equation*}
The performance lower bound follows by subtracting this error allowance
from the surrogate. The trajectory identity in \Cref{rem:fh-tr-bound}
identifies $E_T$ with the negative reward-weighted remainder, so the same
absolute bound applies to that remainder. Shared absorbing EOS transitions
have zero local TV and zero interaction.
\end{proof}

The three future-TV bounds are combined in \path{futureTV_le_adaptive}, and

prove the root error and actual trajectory remainder bounds.

\subsection{Prefix Causal Trust Region Mechanism Bounds}
\label[appsec]{app:pre-causal-tr}

This subsection proves \Cref{lem:prefix-density-ratio,lem:tv-prefix-ratio,thm:pre-causal-mechanism}, which formalize a per-position
mechanism interpretation of prefix Causal Trust Region masking.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:prefix-density-ratio}]
Fix $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$ and a reachable context $c_{t+1}=(x,y_{\le t})$ with $d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})>0$. By the definition of the
context visitation distribution, for any policy $\pi$,
\begin{align*}
    d_{t+1}^{\pi}(c_{t+1})
    &= P(x)\,P^{\pi}(y_{\le t}\mid x)
    \\
    &= P(x)\prod_{s=1}^{t}\pi(y_s\mid c_s),
\end{align*}
where $c_s=(x,y_{<s})$ and the second line is the autoregressive factorization.
Under the mutual-support Assumption~\ref{asm:support}, the ratio $d_{t+1}^{\pitheta}(c_{t+1})/d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})$ is well-defined for all such
contexts. Therefore,
\begin{align*}
    \frac{d_{t+1}^{\pitheta}(c_{t+1})}{d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})}
    &=
    \prod_{s=1}^{t} \frac{\pitheta(y_s\mid c_s)}{\piroll(y_s\mid c_s)}
    \\
    &= \prod_{s=1}^{t}\rho_s
    = W_t,
\end{align*}
as claimed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:tv-prefix-ratio}]
Fix $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$. By definition of total variation distance,
\begin{align*}
    \bigl\|d_{t+1}^{\pitheta} - d_{t+1}^{\piroll}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
    &=
    \frac{1}{2}\sum_{c_{t+1}} \bigl|d_{t+1}^{\pitheta}(c_{t+1}) - d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})\bigr|.
\end{align*}
By \Cref{lem:prefix-density-ratio}, for every reachable $c_{t+1}$ with $d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})>0$,
\begin{equation*}
    d_{t+1}^{\pitheta}(c_{t+1})
    =
    d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})\,W_t.
\end{equation*}
Substituting this into the previous display yields
\begin{align*}
    \bigl\|d_{t+1}^{\pitheta} - d_{t+1}^{\piroll}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
    &=
    \frac{1}{2}\sum_{c_{t+1}} d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})\,\bigl|W_t - 1\bigr|
    \\
    &=
    \frac{1}{2}\E_{c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}}\!\left[\bigl|W_t - 1\bigr|\right],
\end{align*}
which proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:pre-causal-mechanism}]
Fix $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$. Recall that $\tau_+ := \log(1+\epshigh)$ and $\tau_- := -\log(1-\epslow)$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: Rewrite the acceptance event.}
By definition of the prefix mask in Eq.~\eqref{eq:prefix-mask},
\begin{equation*}
    M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}=1
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \log\rhoprefix{t}\in[-\tau_-,\tau_+].
\end{equation*}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:prefix-is}, this is equivalent to the cumulative log-ratio constraint
\begin{equation*}
    -t\tau_- \le \sum_{s=1}^{t}\log\rho_s \le t\tau_+.
\end{equation*}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: Convert to a bound on the prefix density ratio.}
Recall that $W_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t}\rho_s$. Equivalently,
\begin{equation*}
    W_t = \exp\!\Big(\sum_{s=1}^{t}\log\rho_s\Big).
\end{equation*}
Therefore, the previous display implies that, on $\{M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}=1\}$,
\begin{equation*}
    e^{-t\tau_-} \le W_t \le e^{t\tau_+}.
\end{equation*}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: Bound the ratio residual and take expectations.}
If a scalar $w$ lies in an interval $[a,b]$ with $0<a\le b$, then $|w-1|\le \max\{b-1,1-a\}$. Applying this with
$a=e^{-t\tau_-}$ and $b=e^{t\tau_+}$ yields that on $\{M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}=1\}$,
\begin{equation*}
    \bigl|W_t-1\bigr|
    \le
    \max\!\big\{e^{t\tau_+}-1,\;1-e^{-t\tau_-}\big\}.
\end{equation*}
Multiplying by $M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}$ and taking expectations under $c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}$ gives
\begin{align*}
    \E_{c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}}\!\left[M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}\,\bigl|W_t-1\bigr|\right]
    &\le
    \max\!\big\{e^{t\tau_+}-1,\;1-e^{-t\tau_-}\big\}\,
    \E_{c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}}\!\left[M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}\right]
    \\
    &\le
    \max\!\big\{e^{t\tau_+}-1,\;1-e^{-t\tau_-}\big\},
\end{align*}
which completes the proof.
\end{proof}

The complete finite-history statement is checked in
\path{formal/TreePrefixGate.lean}. The gate is defined by the actual
cumulative log-ratio divided by the active prefix length.
\path{accepted_prefix_density_envelope} proves the density-ratio and
residual bounds on positive-mass accepted prefixes;
\path{prefix_gate_residual_bound} and
\path{prefix_gate_residual_le_envelope} prove both expectation inequalities
under the normalized rollout tree. \path{prefix_gate_tv_bound} also identifies
the corresponding bound on the half-L1 distance of the filtered prefix
masses. These statements allow an action-dependent gate and common zero-mass
tokens. They concern filtered probability mass and do not assert an unbiased
masked policy gradient.

\subsection{Sub-Gaussian Strengthening: Per-Position Bounds via Prefix Density Ratios}
\label[appsec]{app:subgaussian-perpos}

This subsection proves \Cref{lem:wt-second-moment,cor:tv-prefix-subgaussian,thm:per-position-error-subgaussian,lem:tv-accepted-prefix,cor:tv-accepted-subgaussian,cor:effective-horizon}, which strengthen the
prefix density-ratio mechanism view with explicit per-position bounds under a conditional concentration assumption.

\subsubsection{Statements and Interpretation}
The mechanism in \Cref{sec:prefix-mechanism} is distributional: it identifies $W_t$ as the change-of-measure object that controls context shift.
To turn \Cref{lem:tv-prefix-ratio} into an explicit per-position bound, we introduce a quantitative concentration assumption on the per-token
log-ratio increments.

\begin{assumption}[Conditionally sub-Gaussian log-ratio increments]
\label{asm:subgaussian-logratio}
Under the rollout distribution $y_t\sim \piroll(\cdot|c_t)$, define the centered increment
\begin{equation*}
    X_t := \log\rho_t - \E\!\left[\log\rho_t \mid c_t\right].
\end{equation*}
Assume that $\log\rho_t$ is conditionally integrable and that
$\DKL(\piroll(\cdot\mid c_t)\,\|\,\pitheta(\cdot\mid c_t))<\infty$ on every reachable context. There exists a constant
$\sigma^2>0$ such that for all $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$, all reachable contexts $c_t$, and all $\lambda\in\R$,
\begin{equation*}
    \E\!\left[\exp(\lambda X_t)\mid c_t\right]
    \le
    \exp\!\left(\frac{\lambda^2\sigma^2}{2}\right).
\end{equation*}
\end{assumption}

\begin{lemma}[Second-moment control of the prefix density ratio]
\label{lem:wt-second-moment}
In the finite-vocabulary history-tree model, assume the mutual-support \Cref{asm:support} and \Cref{asm:subgaussian-logratio}.
Then for any $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$,
\begin{equation*}
    \E_{c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}}\!\left[W_t^2\right] \le \exp(2\sigma^2 t).
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[An explicit per-position context-shift bound]
\label{cor:tv-prefix-subgaussian}
In the same finite-vocabulary history-tree model, assume the mutual-support \Cref{asm:support} and
\Cref{asm:subgaussian-logratio}. Then for any $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$,
\begin{equation*}
    \bigl\|d_{t+1}^{\pitheta} - d_{t+1}^{\piroll}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
    \le
    \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\exp(2\sigma^2 t) - 1}.
\end{equation*}
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[A per-position surrogate-error bound under sub-Gaussian prefix drift]
\label{thm:per-position-error-subgaussian}
In the same finite-vocabulary history-tree model, assume the mutual-support \Cref{asm:support},
\Cref{asm:subgaussian-logratio}, and bounded rewards (i.e., $|R(x,y)|\le R_{\max}$). Then the surrogate error in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:error-decomp} satisfies
\begin{equation*}
    |\mathrm{Error}(\pitheta)|
    \le
    2R_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sqrt{\exp(2\sigma^2 (t-1)) - 1}.
\end{equation*}
\end{theorem}

On the finite history tree, the proof expands the cumulative advantage signal
into its expectations under each prefix distribution, applies the concentration
bound to their differences, and uses $|g_t|\le 2R_{\max}$.
This complete finite-model argument is verified by
\path{surrogate_error_of_mgf} in \path{formal/PrefixConcentration.lean}.
General measurable prompt spaces, almost-sure conditional assumptions, and
the general filtration formulation remain separate formalization obligations.

\begin{corollary}[A concentration-based improvement certificate]
\label{cor:concentration-improvement}
For each fixed prompt in the finite-vocabulary model, under the assumptions of
\Cref{thm:per-position-error-subgaussian}, define
\[
    B_T(\sigma):=2R_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}
        \sqrt{\exp(2\sigma^2(t-1))-1}.
\]
For this fixed prompt $x$, write
$J_x(\pi):=\E_{y\sim P^{\pi}(\cdot|x)}[R(x,y)]$ and let
$\mathcal{L}_{x,\piroll}^{\mathrm{LLM}}(\pitheta)$ denote the corresponding
rollout surrogate with the prompt held fixed. Then
\[
    J_x(\pitheta)-J_x(\piroll)
    \ge \mathcal{L}_{x,\piroll}^{\mathrm{LLM}}(\pitheta)-B_T(\sigma).
\]
In particular, $\mathcal{L}_{x,\piroll}^{\mathrm{LLM}}(\pitheta)>B_T(\sigma)$
implies strict improvement in expected reward.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply the lower side of the absolute surrogate-error bound in
\Cref{thm:per-position-error-subgaussian}. The lower bound and strict-improvement
implication are checked by \path{performance_lower_bound_of_mgf} and
\path{performance_improves_of_mgf} in \path{formal/PrefixConcentration.lean}.
\end{proof}
The certificate concerns the raw population surrogate.
\Cref{thm:promax-certificate} connects the actual ProMax objective to that
surrogate using explicit gate, normalization, clipping and sampling terms.
Its estimation argument also accepts the concentration error bound above
in place of the Adaptive cost whenever those assumptions hold.

\par
\noindent\textbf{Acceptance-coupled filtered drift bounds.}
The Pro Max loss (Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-loss}) multiplies each per-token surrogate term by the prefix gate $M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}$, so gradient
signal is received only from \emph{accepted} prefixes. To quantify policy drift on this filtered subset, define the accepted-prefix context
measure (a sub-probability measure)
\begin{equation*}
    \bar{d}_{t+1}^{\pi}(c_{t+1})
    :=
    d_{t+1}^{\pi}(c_{t+1})\,M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}(c_{t+1}),
    \qquad
    t\in\{1,\ldots,T\},
\end{equation*}
where we view $M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}$ as a deterministic function of the length-$t$ prefix $c_{t+1}=(x,y_{\le t})$ under the fixed policy pair
$(\piroll,\pitheta)$. For these possibly unequal-mass measures, we explicitly
use $\|\mu-\nu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}:=\tfrac12\sum_c|\mu(c)-\nu(c)|$,
the half-$L^1$ convention; this need not equal
$\sup_B|\mu(B)-\nu(B)|$ when the total masses differ. No renormalization is
applied. Its total mass under rollout is the acceptance rate
\begin{equation*}
    \alpha_t
    :=
    \E_{c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}}\!\left[M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}\right]
    =
    \sum_{c_{t+1}}\bar{d}_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1}).
\end{equation*}

\begin{lemma}[TV identity for accepted-prefix context measures]
\label{lem:tv-accepted-prefix}
In the finite-vocabulary, normalized history-tree model used above, assume the
mutual-support \Cref{asm:support}. Then for any $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$,
\begin{equation*}
    \bigl\|\bar{d}_{t+1}^{\pitheta} - \bar{d}_{t+1}^{\piroll}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
    =
    \frac{1}{2}\E_{c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}}\!\left[M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}\,\big|W_t-1\big|\right].
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}

\begin{corollary}[Filtered drift bound with acceptance rate]
\label{cor:tv-accepted-subgaussian}
In the same finite-vocabulary, normalized history-tree model, assume the
mutual-support \Cref{asm:support} and \Cref{asm:subgaussian-logratio}. Then for any
$t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$,
\begin{equation*}
    \E_{c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}}\!\left[M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}\,\big|W_t-1\big|\right]
    \le
    \sqrt{\alpha_t}\,\sqrt{\exp(2\sigma^2 t)-1}.
\end{equation*}
Equivalently,
\begin{equation*}
    \bigl\|\bar{d}_{t+1}^{\pitheta} - \bar{d}_{t+1}^{\piroll}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
    \le
    \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\alpha_t}\,\sqrt{\exp(2\sigma^2 t)-1}.
\end{equation*}
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Gate-induced effective horizon]
\label{cor:effective-horizon}
In the same finite-vocabulary, normalized history-tree model, assume the
mutual-support \Cref{asm:support} and \Cref{asm:subgaussian-logratio}. Define the
effective horizon
\begin{equation*}
    H_{\mathrm{eff}} := \sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_t = \sum_{t=1}^{T}\E_{c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}}\!\left[M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}\right].
\end{equation*}
Then the cumulative accepted-prefix drift satisfies
\begin{align*}
    \sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl\|\bar{d}_{t+1}^{\pitheta} - \bar{d}_{t+1}^{\piroll}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
    &\le
    \frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sqrt{\alpha_t}\,\sqrt{\exp(2\sigma^2 t)-1}
    \\
    &\le
    \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{H_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,
    \sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl(\exp(2\sigma^2 t)-1\bigr)}.
\end{align*}
\end{corollary}

\noindent In particular, \Cref{cor:effective-horizon} makes explicit the $\sqrt{\alpha_t}$ per-position attenuation and summarizes its
aggregate effect through $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$, formalizing a ``gate + concentration'' effective-horizon view.
The attenuation includes the effect of reduced accepted mass; it does not
assert that the renormalized conditional distributions become closer.

These accepted-prefix quantities are a filtered drift diagnostic for the
policy pair that supplies the prefix ratio (the implementation pair is
$(\piroll,\piold)$). They coincide with the behavior/current-policy pair at
the start of an update when $\piold=\pitheta$. Because
$M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}$ depends on the sampled prefix including $y_t$, it is measurable with respect to $c_{t+1}$ rather than $c_t$;
therefore the displayed TV identities do not by themselves assert an unbiased policy-gradient identity for the masked objective. The
mask is a causal sample-level filter, and a direct bound on the masked gradient would require an additional change-of-measure theorem on
the action-extended prefix space.

The remaining proofs follow below.

For a finite vocabulary and horizon, the concentration argument is verified
on the full autoregressive history tree in \path{formal/PrefixConcentration.lean}.
The construction multiplies normalized, history-dependent token kernels to
obtain prefix masses, proves their normalization and density-ratio identity,
and connects the resulting finite expectations to the recursive second moment.
Common zero-probability actions, including shared absorbing EOS transitions,
are allowed under mutual support. No independence across tokens is assumed.
The centered-MGF condition is needed only at $\lambda=2$ and only at rollout-reachable histories
shorter than the chosen horizon; zero-probability branches vanish in the second-moment recursion.
The all-$\lambda$ assumption below implies
this condition. The conditional mean is proved non-positive from the local
policy probabilities, rather than supplied as an additional hypothesis.
The same development proves the prefix and accepted-prefix TV bounds, their
effective-horizon aggregation, and the resulting surrogate-error bound in
\Cref{lem:wt-second-moment,cor:tv-prefix-subgaussian,lem:tv-accepted-prefix,cor:tv-accepted-subgaussian,cor:effective-horizon,thm:per-position-error-subgaussian}.

\subsubsection{Proofs}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:wt-second-moment}]
Fix $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$. Throughout this proof, expectations are taken under the rollout distribution
$c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}$ (equivalently, $(x,y_{\le t})$ generated by $\piroll$).
Recall $W_t=\prod_{s=1}^{t}\rho_s$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: Decompose log-ratios into mean + centered increments.}
For each $s\in\{1,\ldots,t\}$, define
\begin{equation*}
    \mu_s := \E\!\left[\log\rho_s\mid c_s\right],
    \qquad
    X_s := \log\rho_s - \mu_s.
\end{equation*}
Under mutual support and the conditional integrability in \Cref{asm:subgaussian-logratio}, the conditional expectation is well-defined, and moreover
\begin{equation*}
    \mu_s
    =
    \E_{y_s\sim \piroll(\cdot|c_s)}\!\left[\log\frac{\pitheta(y_s\mid c_s)}{\piroll(y_s\mid c_s)}\right]
    =
    -\DKL\bigl(\piroll(\cdot|c_s)\,\|\,\pitheta(\cdot|c_s)\bigr)
    \le 0.
\end{equation*}
Therefore,
\begin{align*}
    W_t^2
    &= \exp\!\Big(2\sum_{s=1}^{t}\log\rho_s\Big)
    \\
    &= \exp\!\Big(2\sum_{s=1}^{t}\mu_s\Big)\cdot \exp\!\Big(2\sum_{s=1}^{t}X_s\Big)
    \\
    &\le \exp\!\Big(2\sum_{s=1}^{t}X_s\Big),
\end{align*}
and hence
\begin{equation*}
    \E\!\left[W_t^2\right]
    \le
    \E\!\left[\exp\!\Big(2\sum_{s=1}^{t}X_s\Big)\right].
\end{equation*}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: Control the MGF of the adapted sum by tower property.}
Let $\mathcal{F}_s:=\sigma(x,y_{\le s})$ denote the natural filtration.
Since $c_s=(x,y_{<s})$ is $\mathcal{F}_{s-1}$-measurable and $X_s$ depends only on $(c_s,y_s)$, we have
\begin{equation*}
    \E\!\left[\exp(2X_s)\mid \mathcal{F}_{s-1}\right]
    =
    \E\!\left[\exp(2X_s)\mid c_s\right].
\end{equation*}
By \Cref{asm:subgaussian-logratio} with $\lambda=2$,
\begin{equation*}
    \E\!\left[\exp(2X_s)\mid c_s\right]
    \le \exp\!\left(\frac{2^2\sigma^2}{2}\right)
    = \exp(2\sigma^2).
\end{equation*}
Therefore, applying the tower property yields
\begin{align*}
    \E\!\left[\exp\!\Big(2\sum_{s=1}^{t}X_s\Big)\right]
    &=
    \E\!\left[
        \exp\!\Big(2\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}X_s\Big)\cdot \E\!\left[\exp(2X_t)\mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]
    \right]
    \\
    &\le
    \exp(2\sigma^2)\,\E\!\left[\exp\!\Big(2\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}X_s\Big)\right].
\end{align*}
Iterating this inequality gives
\begin{equation*}
    \E\!\left[\exp\!\Big(2\sum_{s=1}^{t}X_s\Big)\right]
    \le
    \exp(2\sigma^2 t).
\end{equation*}
Combining with Step~1 completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{cor:tv-prefix-subgaussian}]
Fix $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$. By \Cref{lem:tv-prefix-ratio},
\begin{equation*}
    \bigl\|d_{t+1}^{\pitheta} - d_{t+1}^{\piroll}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
    =
    \frac{1}{2}\E\!\left[\bigl|W_t-1\bigr|\right].
\end{equation*}
By Cauchy--Schwarz, $\E[|Z|]\le \sqrt{\E[Z^2]}$, so
\begin{equation*}
    \E\!\left[\bigl|W_t-1\bigr|\right]
    \le
    \sqrt{\E\!\left[(W_t-1)^2\right]}.
\end{equation*}
Expanding the square yields
\begin{equation*}
    \E\!\left[(W_t-1)^2\right]
    =
    \E\!\left[W_t^2\right] - 2\E[W_t] + 1.
\end{equation*}
By \Cref{lem:prefix-density-ratio}, $W_t = d_{t+1}^{\pitheta}(c_{t+1})/d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})$, so
\begin{equation*}
    \E[W_t]
    =
    \sum_{c_{t+1}} d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})\frac{d_{t+1}^{\pitheta}(c_{t+1})}{d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})}
    =
    \sum_{c_{t+1}} d_{t+1}^{\pitheta}(c_{t+1})
    =
    1.
\end{equation*}
Together with \Cref{lem:wt-second-moment}, this implies
\begin{equation*}
    \E\!\left[(W_t-1)^2\right]
    \le \exp(2\sigma^2 t) - 1.
\end{equation*}
Substituting into the first display gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{thm:per-position-error-subgaussian}]
Recall the error decomposition in Eq.~\eqref{eq:error-decomp}:
\begin{equation*}
    |\mathrm{Error}(\pitheta)|
    \le
    \sum_{t=1}^{T} 2\|g_t\|_\infty \cdot \|d_t^{\pitheta} - d_t^{\piroll}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}.
\end{equation*}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: Bound the advantage factor by bounded rewards.}
Fix $t$ and a context $c_t$. By definition,
\begin{equation*}
    g_t(c_t) := \E_{y_t \sim \pitheta(\cdot\mid c_t)}\!\big[A_t^{\piroll}(c_t, y_t)\big],
    \qquad
    A_t^{\piroll}(c_t, y_t)
    :=
    \E_{\piroll}\!\big[R(x,y)\mid c_t, y_t\big] - \E_{\piroll}\!\big[R(x,y)\mid c_t\big].
\end{equation*}
Under bounded rewards $|R(x,y)|\le R_{\max}$, both conditional expectations lie in $[-R_{\max},R_{\max}]$, hence
$|A_t^{\piroll}(c_t,y_t)|\le 2R_{\max}$ and consequently $|g_t(c_t)|\le 2R_{\max}$. Therefore $\|g_t\|_\infty \le 2R_{\max}$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: Apply the per-position TV bound.}
For each $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$, observe that $d_t$ is the context distribution indexed by token position $t$, i.e., it corresponds to a length-$(t-1)$ prefix.
Thus \Cref{cor:tv-prefix-subgaussian} with index $(t-1)$ gives
\begin{equation*}
    \|d_t^{\pitheta} - d_t^{\piroll}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
    \le
    \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\exp(2\sigma^2 (t-1)) - 1}.
\end{equation*}

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: Combine.}
Substituting the bounds from Steps~1--2 into the error decomposition yields
\begin{align*}
    |\mathrm{Error}(\pitheta)|
    &\le
    \sum_{t=1}^{T} 2(2R_{\max})\cdot \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\exp(2\sigma^2 (t-1)) - 1}
    \\
    &=
    2R_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sqrt{\exp(2\sigma^2 (t-1)) - 1},
\end{align*}
which completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{lem:tv-accepted-prefix}]
Fix $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$. Recall the accepted-prefix context measure
\begin{equation*}
    \bar{d}_{t+1}^{\pi}(c_{t+1}) := d_{t+1}^{\pi}(c_{t+1})\,M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}(c_{t+1}).
\end{equation*}
By definition of total variation distance,
\begin{align*}
    \bigl\|\bar{d}_{t+1}^{\pitheta} - \bar{d}_{t+1}^{\piroll}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
    &=
    \frac{1}{2}\sum_{c_{t+1}} \bigl|\bar{d}_{t+1}^{\pitheta}(c_{t+1}) - \bar{d}_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})\bigr|
    \\
    &=
    \frac{1}{2}\sum_{c_{t+1}} M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}(c_{t+1})\,\bigl|d_{t+1}^{\pitheta}(c_{t+1}) - d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})\bigr|.
\end{align*}
By \Cref{lem:prefix-density-ratio}, for every reachable $c_{t+1}$ with $d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})>0$ one has
$d_{t+1}^{\pitheta}(c_{t+1})=d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})W_t$. Substituting yields
\begin{align*}
    \bigl\|\bar{d}_{t+1}^{\pitheta} - \bar{d}_{t+1}^{\piroll}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
    &=
    \frac{1}{2}\sum_{c_{t+1}} d_{t+1}^{\piroll}(c_{t+1})\,M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}(c_{t+1})\,\bigl|W_t-1\bigr|
    \\
    &=
    \frac{1}{2}\E_{c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}}\!\left[M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}\,\bigl|W_t-1\bigr|\right],
\end{align*}
which proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{cor:tv-accepted-subgaussian}]
Fix $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$ and write $\alpha_t := \E_{c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}}\!\left[M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}\right]$. By
Cauchy--Schwarz,
\begin{align*}
    \E\!\left[M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}\,\bigl|W_t-1\bigr|\right]
    &\le
    \sqrt{\E\!\left[M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}\right]}\,\sqrt{\E\!\left[(W_t-1)^2\right]}
    =
    \sqrt{\alpha_t}\,\sqrt{\E\!\left[(W_t-1)^2\right]}.
\end{align*}
As in the proof of \Cref{cor:tv-prefix-subgaussian}, one has $\E[W_t]=1$ and hence
\begin{equation*}
    \E\!\left[(W_t-1)^2\right]
    =
    \E[W_t^2] - 1.
\end{equation*}
By \Cref{lem:wt-second-moment}, $\E[W_t^2]\le \exp(2\sigma^2 t)$, so $\E[(W_t-1)^2]\le \exp(2\sigma^2 t)-1$.
Substituting into the previous display gives
\begin{equation*}
    \E\!\left[M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}\,\bigl|W_t-1\bigr|\right]
    \le
    \sqrt{\alpha_t}\,\sqrt{\exp(2\sigma^2 t)-1}.
\end{equation*}
The equivalent TV bound follows immediately from \Cref{lem:tv-accepted-prefix}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{cor:effective-horizon}]
Define $\alpha_t := \E_{c_{t+1}\sim d_{t+1}^{\piroll}}\!\left[M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}\right]$ and
$H_{\mathrm{eff}}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_t$.
Applying \Cref{cor:tv-accepted-subgaussian} for each $t$ and summing yields
\begin{equation*}
    \sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl\|\bar{d}_{t+1}^{\pitheta} - \bar{d}_{t+1}^{\piroll}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
    \le
    \frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sqrt{\alpha_t}\,\sqrt{\exp(2\sigma^2 t)-1}.
\end{equation*}
For the compressed effective-horizon form, apply Cauchy--Schwarz to the right-hand side with
$b_t:=\sqrt{\exp(2\sigma^2 t)-1}$:
\begin{align*}
    \sum_{t=1}^{T}\sqrt{\alpha_t}\,b_t
    &\le
    \sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_t}\,\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}b_t^2}
    \\
    &=
    \sqrt{H_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl(\exp(2\sigma^2 t)-1\bigr)},
\end{align*}
which proves the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Alternative Trust-Region Masking Variants}
\label[appsec]{app:mask-variants}

This appendix collects several masking variants that are compatible with the ratio setup in the main text
(\Cref{sec:offpolicy-problem}). Throughout, samples are generated by the behavior policy $\piroll$ and gradients are computed under the
training policy $\pitheta$. We write the per-token importance ratio as
\begin{equation*}
    \rho_t := \frac{\pitheta(y_t \mid c_t)}{\piroll(y_t \mid c_t)}.
\end{equation*}
We also reuse the prefix statistic $\rhoprefix{t}$ from \Cref{def:prefix-is}.
For thresholds $\epslow\in(0,1)$ and $\epshigh>0$, define the log-threshold interval
\begin{equation*}
    [\log(1-\epslow),\log(1+\epshigh)] = [-\tau_-,\tau_+],
    \qquad
    \tau_+ := \log(1+\epshigh),
    \qquad
    \tau_- := -\log(1-\epslow).
\end{equation*}

\paragraph{Variant I: Absorbing Prefix-Average Mask}

The main text uses a pointwise prefix gate at each position $t$. A more rejection-style variant enforces an \emph{absorbing} semantics:
once a prefix violates the threshold, all subsequent positions are rejected.

\begin{equation*}
    \widetilde{M}_t^{\mathrm{pre}}
    := \mathbb{I}\!\big[\rhoprefix{t} \in [1-\epslow,1+\epshigh]\big],
    \qquad
    M_t^{\mathrm{abs\text{-}pre}}
    := \prod_{u=1}^{t}\widetilde{M}_u^{\mathrm{pre}}.
\end{equation*}

\paragraph{Variant II: Prefix-Max Mask / Max-Filtering}

Max-based trust-region criteria can be expressed via a sample-based proxy $k_2(\rho)$:
\begin{equation*}
    k_2(\rho) := \tfrac{1}{2}(\log\rho)^2.
\end{equation*}
The corresponding prefix-max statistic and mask are
\begin{equation*}
    S_t^{\max} := \max_{1\le s\le t} k_2(\rho_s),
    \qquad
    M_t^{\mathrm{pre\text{-}max}} := \mathbb{I}\!\big[S_t^{\max} \le \delta_{\max}\big].
\end{equation*}
Equivalently, $M_t^{\mathrm{pre\text{-}max}}=1$ iff $\max_{s\le t}|\log\rho_s| \le \sqrt{2\delta_{\max}}$.

\paragraph{Variant III: Prefix-Score Mask / Bound-Driven}

Score-based masking controls a prefix-level aggregate of per-token ratio deviations. Define a nonnegative per-token deviation
\begin{equation*}
    a_t := |\rho_t - 1|
\end{equation*}
and deterministic nonnegative weights $(w_t)_{t\ge1}$ with $\sum_{s\le t} w_s > 0$. The prefix score is
\begin{equation*}
    \widetilde{W}_t
    := \frac{\sum_{s=1}^{t} w_s a_s}{\sum_{s=1}^{t} w_s},
\end{equation*}
and the corresponding mask is
\begin{equation*}
    M_t^{\mathrm{pre\text{-}score}} := \mathbb{I}\!\big[\widetilde{W}_t \le \delta_W\big].
\end{equation*}
A special case is the uniform-weight choice $w_s\equiv 1$, which yields $\widetilde{W}_T = \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}|\rho_t-1|$ at the
full horizon.

\paragraph{Variant IV: Prefix Abnormal-Token Rate / TRM-Style}

TRM~\citep{li2025trm} uses per-token divergence estimates (or their sample-based proxies) to decide whether a trajectory lies inside a
trust region. We can adapt this idea to a prefix-level gate by counting \emph{abnormal} tokens in the prefix. Concretely, define the
abnormal-token indicator
\begin{equation*}
    B_t := \mathbb{I}\!\big[\Dkltok(c_t;\piroll,\pitheta) > \delta_{\mathrm{TRM}}\big],
\end{equation*}
and the prefix abnormal count and rate
\begin{equation*}
    N_t := \sum_{s=1}^{t} B_s,
    \qquad
    \widehat{p}_t := \frac{N_t}{t}.
\end{equation*}
Given a rate threshold $\xi\in[0,1]$, the corresponding prefix mask is
\begin{equation*}
    M_t^{\mathrm{pre\text{-}anom}}
    := \mathbb{I}\!\big[\widehat{p}_t \le \xi\big].
\end{equation*}
In practice, one may instantiate $B_t$ using TRM's computation method for $\Dkltok(c_t;\piroll,\pitheta)$; for example, via the
sample-based proxy $k_3(\rho):=\rho-1-\log\rho$. Under the mutual-support Assumption~\ref{asm:support}, the ratio
definition $\rho_t=\pitheta/\piroll$ gives
\begin{equation*}
    \E_{\piroll}\!\left[k_3(\rho_t)\mid c_t\right]
    = \DKL\!\left(\piroll(\cdot\mid c_t)\,\|\,\pitheta(\cdot\mid c_t)\right).
\end{equation*}
The finite-support normalization algebra behind this equality is checked by
the Lean theorem \path{finite_k3_proxy_eq_reverse_kl}; its general
conditional-expectation version still requires the probability-space
formalization listed in the audit report.
The following inverse certificate connects a sample-level $k_3$ threshold to the
inlier condition used below. A threshold on the conditional mean KL does not
give this pointwise implication for each sampled token.

\begin{lemma}[A Log-Ratio Interval from the Sample $k_3$ Proxy]
\label{lem:k3-inlier-certificate}
Write $F(z)=e^z-1-z$, so $k_3(\rho)=F(\log\rho)$ for $\rho>0$.
For $\tau_-,\tau_+\ge0$, if
\begin{equation*}
    k_3(\rho)\le\delta
    \quad\text{and}\quad
    \delta\le\min\{F(-\tau_-),F(\tau_+)\},
\end{equation*}
then $-\tau_-\le\log\rho\le\tau_+$.
In particular, for any $\tau_0>0$, the threshold
\begin{equation*}
    \delta_0(\tau_0):=\min\{e^{-\tau_0}-1+\tau_0,\ e^{\tau_0}-1-\tau_0\}>0
\end{equation*}
ensures that $B_s=\mathbb I[k_3(\rho_s)>\delta]$ with
$0\le\delta\le\delta_0(\tau_0)$ satisfies
$B_s=0\Rightarrow|\log\rho_s|\le\tau_0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$F$ is strictly decreasing on $(-\infty,0]$ and strictly increasing on $[0,\infty)$,
with $F(0)=0$. If $\log\rho<-\tau_-$ or $\log\rho>\tau_+$,
then $F(\log\rho)>\delta$, a contradiction. The same strict monotonicity
gives positivity of both terms defining $\delta_0(\tau_0)$.
The interval certificate, strict monotonicity, and positive threshold are checked
in \path{formal/K3Inlier.lean}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[From Abnormal-Token Rate to a Prefix-Average Constraint]
\label{lem:anom-rate-implies-pre}
Fix a position $t\in\{1,\ldots,T\}$ and write $z_s := \log\rho_s$ for $s\le t$. Assume there exist finite constants
$\Lambda_+,\Lambda_->0$ and an inlier threshold $\tau_0\ge 0$ such that
\begin{equation*}
    -\Lambda_- \le z_s \le \Lambda_+,
    \qquad
    \forall s\le t.
\end{equation*}
Let $B_s\in\{0,1\}$ be an abnormal-token indicator satisfying the explicit inlier condition
\begin{equation*}
    B_s=0 \Longrightarrow |z_s|\le \tau_0,
    \qquad
    \Lambda_+\ge\tau_0,\quad \Lambda_-\ge\tau_0.
\end{equation*}
Define its prefix rate by
\begin{equation*}
    \widehat{p}_t := \frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t} B_s,
\end{equation*}
For any threshold $\xi\in[0,1]$, if $\widehat{p}_t \le \xi$, then the prefix-average log-ratio obeys
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t} \log\rho_s
    \in
    \Big[-\tau_0 - \xi(\Lambda_- - \tau_0),\;\tau_0 + \xi(\Lambda_+ - \tau_0)\Big].
\end{equation*}
In particular, choosing log-thresholds $(\tau_-,\tau_+)$ that satisfy
\begin{equation*}
    \tau_- \ge \tau_0 + \xi(\Lambda_- - \tau_0),
    \qquad
    \tau_+ \ge \tau_0 + \xi(\Lambda_+ - \tau_0)
\end{equation*}
ensures $\widehat{p}_t\le\xi$ implies the prefix-average constraint $\frac{1}{t}\sum_{s\le t}\log\rho_s\in[-\tau_-,\tau_+]$, and hence
$\rhoprefix{t}\in [e^{-\tau_-}, e^{\tau_+}]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $N_t := \sum_{s=1}^{t} B_s$ so that $\widehat{p}_t = N_t/t \le \xi$. For the upper bound, split the sum into inliers and outliers:
\begin{equation*}
    \sum_{s=1}^{t} z_s
    \le
    (t-N_t)\,\tau_0 + N_t\,\Lambda_+,
\end{equation*}
since inliers satisfy $z_s \le \tau_0$ and outliers satisfy $z_s \le \Lambda_+$. Dividing by $t$ and using $N_t/t\le\xi$ yields
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t} z_s
    \le
    \tau_0 + \xi(\Lambda_+ - \tau_0).
\end{equation*}
Similarly, using $z_s \ge -\tau_0$ for inliers and $z_s \ge -\Lambda_-$ for outliers gives
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{1}{t}\sum_{s=1}^{t} z_s
    \ge
    -\tau_0 - \xi(\Lambda_- - \tau_0).
\end{equation*}
The final implication to $\rhoprefix{t}$ follows from $\log\rhoprefix{t} = \frac{1}{t}\sum_{s\le t}\log\rho_s$.
\end{proof}

For the sample-based indicator in \Cref{lem:k3-inlier-certificate}, the composition
of the inverse certificate with this prefix-rate bound is checked by
\path{k3_abnormal_prefix_average_bound} in \path{formal/K3Inlier.lean}.
This supplies the inlier condition from the actual proxy threshold while
retaining the stated bounds $[-\Lambda_-,\Lambda_+]$ on all log-ratios.

\subsection{Centered Surrogate Equivalence and Error Decomposition}
\label[appsec]{app:surrogate-equivalence}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \Cref{prop:surrogate-equivalence}]
Write $Q_t(c_t,a)=\E_{\piroll}[R\mid c_t,a]$, $V_t(c_t)=\E_{\piroll}[R\mid c_t]$, and
$\bar b(x)=\E[b\mid x]$. Conditional independence of $b$ and policy normalization give
\begin{equation*}
    \E_{\piroll}[(R-b)\rho_t\mid c_t]
    =\sum_a\pitheta(a\mid c_t)(Q_t(c_t,a)-V_t(c_t))
      +V_t(c_t)-\bar b(x).
\end{equation*}
The last two terms are independent of $\pitheta$. At $\pitheta=\piroll$ the advantage sum is zero.
Multiplying by the predictable mask $m_t(c_t)$, averaging over rollout contexts, and summing over $t$ proves the first equality.
The second follows by replacing $b$ by zero and using
$\E_{\piroll}[R(\rho_t-1)\mid c_t]=\sum_a\pitheta(a\mid c_t)(Q_t(c_t,a)-V_t(c_t))$.
On post-EOS padding states the advantage is zero and the ratio is one.
\end{proof}

The finite conditional-law proof, including rollout continuation sums and fixed context/action-mask weights, is checked by
\path{finite_surrogate_equivalence} and \path{finite_context_surrogate_equivalence} in
\path{formal/SurrogateBridge.lean}; \path{finite_independent_baseline_average} integrates an explicitly independent baseline
sampling law, and \path{finite_surrogate_derivative_equivalence} transfers the derivative through the constant offset.
This gives an exact population connection for the stated finite conditional model;
it does not equate individual Monte Carlo estimates. Random-length batch reductions
are treated separately in \Cref{prop:promax-objective-split,sec:promax-certificate};
a general measure-theoretic interface remains outside this formalization.

The finite-state TV step behind Eq.~\eqref{eq:error-decomp} is the elementary bound
$|\E_{d_t^{\pitheta}}[g_t]-\E_{d_t^{\piroll}}[g_t]|\le
2\|g_t\|_\infty\,\|d_t^{\pitheta}-d_t^{\piroll}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}$; its finite-sum form is
checked by the Lean theorem \path{finite_tv_expectation_bound}, and summing this
step over a finite horizon is checked by
\path{finite_error_decomposition_bound} in \path{formal/ErrorDecomp.lean}.
The finite history-tree modules construct the context-visitation laws,
conditional token kernels, and corresponding autoregressive identities

\subsection{Cited Supporting Results for IS Correction}
\label{sec:cited-is-results}

This subsection records several standard identities and bounds used in our comparisons of importance sampling (IS) correction mechanisms.
All statements are adapted from existing trust-region analyses for LLM-RL (e.g., \citet{qi2026rethinkingtrustregionllm,li2025trm}). We

\begin{remark}[TV as an Expectation of the Ratio Residual]
\label{rem:tv-ratio-identity}
Under the mutual-support Assumption~\ref{asm:support}, for any context $c_t$,
\begin{equation*}
    \Dtvtok(c_t;\piroll,\pitheta)
    = \frac{1}{2}\E_{y_t \sim \piroll(\cdot|c_t)}\!\left[\big|\rho_t - 1\big|\right],
    \qquad
    \rho_t = \frac{\pitheta(y_t|c_t)}{\piroll(y_t|c_t)}.
\end{equation*}
See, e.g., \citet{qi2026rethinkingtrustregionllm}.
For finite token support, the ratio identity is checked by
\path{tv_ratio_identity_of_reverse_support} in
\path{formal/ProMax.lean}; the normalized autoregressive history-tree
instantiation is checked by the path-mass results in
\path{formal/TreeSurrogate.lean} and \path{formal/PrefixConcentration.lean}.
Only the general measurable-space version remains outside the present
formalization.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Coarse-Graining Lower Bounds]
\label{rem:coarse-grain-lower}
Let $\mathcal{V}$ be the vocabulary and let $\mathcal{C}=\{C_1,\ldots,C_m\}$ be any partition of $\mathcal{V}$. For a policy $\pi$,
define the coarse-grained distribution $\pi^{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot|c_t)$ by $\pi^{\mathcal{C}}(C_j|c_t):=\sum_{v\in C_j}\pi(v|c_t)$.
Then for any policies $\pi,\pi'$ and any context $c_t$,
\begin{equation*}
    \DTV\big(\pi(\cdot|c_t)\,\|\,\pi'(\cdot|c_t)\big)
    \ge \DTV\big(\pi^{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot|c_t)\,\|\,{\pi'}^{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot|c_t)\big),
\end{equation*}
and, if $\pi(\cdot|c_t)$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\pi'(\cdot|c_t)$,
\begin{equation*}
    \DKL\big(\pi(\cdot|c_t)\,\|\,\pi'(\cdot|c_t)\big)
    \ge \DKL\big(\pi^{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot|c_t)\,\|\,{\pi'}^{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot|c_t)\big).
\end{equation*}
See, e.g., \citet{qi2026rethinkingtrustregionllm}.
For a finite vocabulary and finite partition map, the TV contraction in this
remark is checked by
\path{REINFORCEProMax.finite_coarse_tv_lower_bound} in
\path{formal/CoarseGrain.lean}. The KL contraction and its full equality
condition, including zero-mass cells, are checked by
\path{finite_coarse_kl_contraction} and
\path{finite_coarse_kl_equality_iff_proportional} in
\path{formal/CoarseKL.lean}; only the stated one-sided absolute continuity is required.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Binary TV Lower Bound from a Sampled Token]
\label{rem:binary-tv-lower}
Fix a context $c_t$ and a token $y_t \in \mathcal{V}$ such that $\piroll(y_t|c_t)>0$. Consider the binary partition
$\mathcal{C}=\{\{y_t\},\mathcal{V}\setminus\{y_t\}\}$. Then
\begin{equation*}
    \Dtvtok(c_t;\piroll,\pitheta)
    \ge \big|\piroll(y_t|c_t) - \pitheta(y_t|c_t)\big|
    = \piroll(y_t|c_t)\,\big|\rho_t-1\big|.
\end{equation*}
For a finite token support, this binary-partition instance is checked by
\path{binary_tv_lower_bound} in
\path{formal/CoarseGrain.lean}; the arbitrary finite-partition TV and KL statements
are verified in that module and \path{formal/CoarseKL.lean}, respectively.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Sequence-Level TV Bound by Expected Token-Level TV]
\label{rem:seq-tv-sum}
Fix a prompt $x$, a finite vocabulary, and normalized autoregressive policies
that may depend on the complete prefix context. Write $P^{\pi}(\cdot|x)$ for
the resulting distribution over length-$T$ responses. Then
\begin{equation*}
    \DTV\big(P^{\piroll}(\cdot|x)\,\|\,P^{\pitheta}(\cdot|x)\big)
    \le \sum_{t=1}^{T} \E_{c_t \sim d_t^{\piroll}}\!\big[\Dtvtok(c_t;\piroll,\pitheta)\big].
\end{equation*}
Here $d_t^{\pi}$ is the distribution of the length-$(t-1)$ prefix context
before token $t$ (so the initial context is $d_1^{\pi}$ and the terminal
length-$T$ response distribution is $d_{T+1}^{\pi}$). The one-step estimate is
\begin{equation*}
    \|d_{t+1}^{\pitheta}-d_{t+1}^{\piroll}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}
    \le \|d_t^{\pitheta}-d_t^{\piroll}\|_{\mathrm{TV}} + \Dbar_t.
\end{equation*}
Summing from the common initial context proves the displayed bound. The
weighting by rollout visitation is retained; a uniform maximum over contexts
is not required. Both statements are proved directly for the generated path
probabilities by \path{prefixTV_step_weighted} and
\path{sequenceTV_le_expected_tokenTV} in \path{formal/TreeTV.lean}.
No policy-support condition is needed. Shared absorbing EOS padding is
allowed. See also \citet{qi2026rethinkingtrustregionllm}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Coarse-Graining Gap for TV]
\label{rem:tv-gap}
Fix a context $c_t$ and a partition $\mathcal{C}=\{C_1,\ldots,C_m\}$. For policies $\pi,\pi'$, define the cell-wise slack
\begin{equation*}
    s_j(c_t)
    :=
    \sum_{v\in C_j} \big|\pi(v|c_t)-\pi'(v|c_t)\big|
    -
    \big|\pi(C_j|c_t)-\pi'(C_j|c_t)\big|.
\end{equation*}
Then $s_j(c_t)\ge 0$ and the gap between the true and coarse-grained TV divergences satisfies
\begin{equation*}
    \DTV\big(\pi(\cdot|c_t)\,\|\,\pi'(\cdot|c_t)\big)
    -
    \DTV\big(\pi^{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot|c_t)\,\|\,{\pi'}^{\mathcal{C}}(\cdot|c_t)\big)
    =
    \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m} s_j(c_t).
\end{equation*}
Moreover, each slack term is bounded by the total mass of its cell:
\begin{equation*}
    0 \le s_j(c_t) \le \pi(C_j|c_t) + \pi'(C_j|c_t).
\end{equation*}
See, e.g., \citet{qi2026rethinkingtrustregionllm}.
For finite supports, the displayed slack decomposition, its non-negativity,
and the cell-mass bound are checked by
\path{finite_tv_gap_slack_decomposition},
\path{finite_tv_gap_slack_nonneg}, and
\path{finite_tv_gap_slack_mass_bound} in
\path{formal/CoarseGrain.lean}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Equality Condition for Coarse-Grained KL]
\label{rem:kl-equality}
Under the absolute continuity condition in \Cref{rem:coarse-grain-lower}, equality in the KL coarse-graining inequality holds if and only
if, for every cell $C_j$ with $\pi(C_j|c_t)>0$, there is a constant $\lambda_j>0$ such that
\begin{equation*}
    \pi(v|c_t)=\lambda_j\,\pi'(v|c_t)
    \qquad\text{for every }v\in C_j.
\end{equation*}
Equivalently, the two conditional distributions inside each positive-mass cell agree after normalization. The condition must
include tokens with $\pi(v|c_t)=0$ but $\pi'(v|c_t)>0$; restricting the ratio test to the positive support of $\pi$ is insufficient under one-sided absolute continuity.
For arbitrary finite cells, including zero-probability tokens, the full partition characterization is checked by
\path{finite_coarse_kl_equality_iff_proportional} in \path{formal/CoarseKL.lean}.
The proportionality constant is necessarily $\lambda_j=\pi(C_j|c_t)/\pi'(C_j|c_t)$.
Cells with $\pi(C_j|c_t)=0$ contribute zero to both KL expressions and impose no condition on $\pi'$.
The concrete two-token counterexample
\path{coarse_grain_kl_positive_support_condition_counterexample} in \path{formal/KLEquality.lean}
records why the weaker wording fails.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[A Finite-Horizon Surrogate for Sequence Rewards]
\label{def:llm-surrogate}
For this finite-horizon statement, fix a deterministic horizon $T$. If responses terminate at different lengths, we pad them with an
absorbing end-of-sequence token and include the corresponding action mask. The post-termination transition is deterministic and shared
by both policies (hence its ratio is $1$), so the identities below apply to the padded horizon without adding spurious likelihood-ratio
terms.
Define the rollout-based, sequence-reward surrogate
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{L}_{\piroll}^{\mathrm{LLM}}(\pitheta)
    :=
    \E_{x \sim P(x),\, y \sim P^{\piroll}(\cdot|x)}\!\left[
        R(x,y)\cdot \sum_{t=1}^{T}(\rho_t-1)
    \right],
\end{equation*}
and the remainder term
\begin{equation*}
    \Delta^{\mathrm{LLM}}(\piroll,\pitheta)
    :=
    \E_{x \sim P(x),\, y \sim P^{\piroll}(\cdot|x)}\!\left[
        R(x,y)\cdot \sum_{t=1}^{T}(\rho_t-1)\left(1-\prod_{j=t+1}^{T}\rho_j\right)
    \right],
\end{equation*}
where $\rho_t=\pitheta(y_t|c_t)/\piroll(y_t|c_t)$ and $P^{\piroll}(\cdot|x)$ denotes the rollout trajectory distribution.
See, e.g., \citet{qi2026rethinkingtrustregionllm}.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Interpretation of the surrogate--remainder split.}
For a fixed prompt, write $p(y):=P^{\piroll}(y|x)$ and
$q(y):=P^{\pitheta}(y|x)$. Under the finite-horizon mutual-support convention,
$q(y)/p(y)=\prod_{t=1}^{T}\rho_t(y)$. Thus the left-hand side of the performance
difference identity is
$\sum_y q(y)R(x,y)-\sum_y p(y)R(x,y)$, the change in expected sequence reward.
The product telescoping identity gives
\begin{equation*}
    \prod_{t=1}^{T}\rho_t-1
    = \sum_{t=1}^{T}(\rho_t-1)\prod_{j=t+1}^{T}\rho_j
    = \underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\rho_t-1)}_{\mathrm{Surrogate}(y)}
      -\underbrace{\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\rho_t-1)
        \left(1-\prod_{j=t+1}^{T}\rho_j\right)}_{\mathrm{Remainder}(y)}.
\end{equation*}
Multiplying by $p(y)R(x,y)$ and summing over trajectories yields
\begin{equation*}
    \sum_y q(y)R(x,y)-\sum_y p(y)R(x,y)
    = \sum_y p(y)R(x,y)\,\mathrm{Surrogate}(y)
      -\sum_y p(y)R(x,y)\,\mathrm{Remainder}(y).
\end{equation*}
The surrogate is therefore the additive, rollout-estimable part, while the
remainder records the interaction with ratios at later positions. This is an
exact finite-horizon algebraic identity, not a claim that the remainder is
always nonnegative or negligible. It vanishes for $T=1$; for longer responses
it is small only under additional policy-coupling or trust-region conditions.
By \Cref{prop:surrogate-equivalence}, this sequence-reward surrogate equals the standard per-step surrogate, and also equals the
unclipped trajectory-level surrogate after subtracting its rollout-policy value. Independent baselines do not break this equality.
The constructed finite-history-tree version of this identity is checked by
\path{trajectory_surrogate_remainder_identity} in
\path{formal/TreeSurrogate.lean}; the abstract finite-support algebra is checked by
\path{finite_horizon_surrogate_identity}. The additional effects of the actual ProMax gate, normalization, clipping, and batch reduction are identified separately in
\Cref{prop:promax-objective-split}.

\begin{remark}[A Finite-Horizon Performance Difference Bound]
\label{rem:fh-tr-bound}
Under the mutual-support Assumption~\ref{asm:support} and the fixed/EOS-padded horizon convention in
\Cref{def:llm-surrogate} (with finite-length Assumption~\ref{asm:finite-length}), one has the exact identity
\begin{equation*}
    J(\pitheta) - J(\piroll)
    =
    \mathcal{L}_{\piroll}^{\mathrm{LLM}}(\pitheta)
    -
    \Delta^{\mathrm{LLM}}(\piroll,\pitheta).
\end{equation*}
This display is conditional on the shown expectations being well-defined and on the
conditional change-of-measure identity being integrable under the prompt law $P(x)$.
Here the Lean-certified statement is for a finite-vocabulary, normalized
autoregressive history tree, pointwise in a fixed prompt. It extends to a
finite empirical prompt distribution by a finite weighted sum;
an arbitrary prompt measure requires the usual integrability and change-of-measure hypotheses in addition to the finite conditional model.
The displayed identity is the finite-horizon change-of-measure statement; its telescoping proof uses mutual support so that
\(\E_{\piroll}[R\prod_{s=1}^{T}\rho_s]=\E_{\pitheta}[R]\). For each fixed prompt, a finite vocabulary and a uniform bound
\(\Dtvtok(c_t)\le\epsilon\) at every history, the finite autoregressive-tree recursion gives the following quadratic estimate
without an additional ratio-coupling premise:
\begin{equation*}
    \bigl|\Delta^{\mathrm{LLM}}(\piroll,\pitheta)\bigr|
    \le
    2R_{\max}\,T(T-1)\,\epsilon^2,
\end{equation*}
This estimate is checked by \path{surrogate_error_quadratic} in
\path{formal/AutoregressiveTree.lean}; the proof allows history-dependent policies and uses rollout occupancy for the
surrogate. The same finite-tree recursion also gives the expected-sum-TV estimate
\begin{equation*}
    \bigl|\Delta^{\mathrm{LLM}}(\piroll,\pitheta)\bigr|
    \le
    4R_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Dbar_t.
\end{equation*}
Consequently, whenever the quadratic estimate holds, and also whenever the separate linear estimate holds,
\begin{equation*}
    J(\pitheta) - J(\piroll)
    \ge
    \mathcal{L}_{\piroll}^{\mathrm{LLM}}(\pitheta)
    -
    \min\!\left(
        2R_{\max}\,T(T-1)\,\epsilon^2,\;
        4R_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Dbar_t
    \right).
\end{equation*}
Both estimates are formalized for the finite autoregressive tree under the stated bounded-reward and policy-normalization
conditions, and the finite prompt mixture is handled by linearity. The exact change-of-measure identity remains valid independently
of either bound. \Cref{thm:adaptive-bound} further supplies the complete
position-weighted Adaptive bound on this same remainder. The random-length
reduction identities and finite sampling margins are handled separately in
\Cref{prop:promax-objective-split,sec:promax-certificate}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Comparison with Other Advantage Estimation Methods}

We compare REINFORCE Pro Max with existing critic-free LLM-RL methods along four dimensions: baseline type, advantage normalization
strategy, IS correction granularity, and whether the IS correction preserves the causal structure of autoregressive generation. We
organize the comparison into two parts: (i) baseline and normalization choices (this subsection) and (ii) IS correction mechanisms (the
next subsection). These dimensions correspond to the key design choices that affect the two factors in the error decomposition
(\Cref{eq:error-decomp}): the advantage factor (targeted by baseline and normalization) and the context shift factor (targeted by IS
correction).

\textbf{Baseline and normalization.} REINFORCE++~\citep{hu2025reinforce} and GRPO~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath} include the current reward in
their group mean baselines. RLOO~\citep{ahmadian2024back} supplies a baseline conditionally independent of each sample's own response given the prompt, with the exact $(n-1)/n$ relation to the
mean-baseline estimator described in \Cref{app:rloo-proof}. Normalization is a separate choice: a common positive scale preserves relative
magnitudes, while centering can change signs. ProMax's asymmetric scales solve the token-weighted mean and second-moment constraints
while preserving advantage signs, including when response lengths differ.

\subsection{Comparison with Other Importance Sampling Correction Methods}

We adopt a trust-region masking viewpoint: when mismatch between $\piroll$ and $\pitheta$ is large, one should attenuate or reject
unreliable gradient contributions. Existing methods differ mainly in the granularity of this attenuation (see \Cref{sec:existing-is} and
\Cref{tab:is-methods} for a unified notation).

\textbf{Single-sample ratio statistics vs.\ distributional drift.} Although the per-token ratio residual $|\rho_t-1|$ is a single-sample
quantity, it admits a clean divergence interpretation. Conditional on the context $c_t$, \Cref{rem:tv-ratio-identity} implies that
$|\rho_t-1|$ has expectation $2\Dtvtok(c_t;\piroll,\pitheta)$ under $y_t\sim\piroll(\cdot|c_t)$, so thresholding $\rho_t$ can be viewed
as thresholding a noisy proxy for a TV-based trust region. At the same time, the binary lower bound in \Cref{rem:binary-tv-lower} shows
that the effect of a large ratio depends on the behavior-policy mass at the sampled token: for low-probability tokens, even a large
$\rho_t$ can correspond to a small absolute probability shift. Finally, policy drift at the level of full response distributions admits an
additive upper bound by the expected token-level TVs along the trajectory (\Cref{rem:seq-tv-sum}), which motivates per-position and
prefix-structured filtering mechanisms.

\textbf{Finite-horizon remainder bounds.} For a finite vocabulary, the autoregressive-tree recursion gives both a quadratic
max-TV bound and a linear expected-sum-TV bound for the higher-order term in the rollout-based performance-difference identity
(recorded in \Cref{rem:fh-tr-bound}). These bounds use the rollout occupancy for the surrogate and do not require a ratio bound.
The linear expression
$4R_{\max}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Dbar_t$ makes the per-position structure explicit and clarifies why long-horizon stability can benefit
from controlling divergence along the trajectory rather than only via a single global trust-region statistic. The finite-tree
instances of both inequalities are Lean-verified in `formal/AutoregressiveTree.lean`; the finite prompt-mixture extension is checked
by \path{family_remainder_quadratic} and \path{family_remainder_linear} in \path{formal/PromptMixture.lean}. The EOS/action-mask interface is
checked for a context-predictable absorbing continuation by \path{trajectory_masked_surrogate_eq} in \path{formal/TreeSurrogate.lean}.
The full Adaptive refinement is checked by
\path{trajectory_remainder_adaptive} and \path{family_remainder_adaptive}
in \path{formal/AdaptiveBound.lean}. It retains the expected local TV at
each position and multiplies it by a bound on the remaining continuation
drift (\Cref{thm:adaptive-bound}). Random-length reduction changes the
estimator's population target as quantified in \Cref{sec:promax-certificate}.

\textbf{Coarse-grained divergence proxies as controllable trust-region gates.} Full next-token divergences (TV/KL) are expensive to
compute over the entire vocabulary, motivating coarse-grained approximations such as Binary or Top-$K$ schemes. Formally, divergences
computed on any partition are lower bounds on the true divergences (\Cref{rem:coarse-grain-lower}). Therefore, a gate that masks whenever
a coarse-grained proxy exceeds a fixed threshold is \emph{conservative}: it does not reject updates that are provably within the trust
region defined by the corresponding full-vocabulary divergence. The remaining question is when such proxies may under-detect drift.

For TV divergence, the coarse-graining gap admits an explicit slack decomposition and a mass-based upper bound (\Cref{rem:tv-gap}). In
particular, for Top-$K$ style partitions that isolate head tokens and aggregate the tail into a single ``other'' cell, the approximation
error is controlled by the tail probability mass. When this aggregated tail mass is small, Top-$K$ TV can serve as a quantitatively
controlled proxy for per-position divergence gating, and such per-position proxies can be aggregated along prefixes to yield a
prefix-structured gate that respects the causal order of generation. For KL divergence, equality of coarse-grained KL holds exactly when, in every
cell with positive $p$-mass, the two distributions are proportional
throughout that cell (\Cref{rem:kl-equality}). The finite identity in
\path{formal/CoarseKL.lean} expresses the full-minus-coarse KL as a sum of
nonnegative cell-wise Gibbs gaps; consequently, a quantitative near-equality
claim should be stated as small total Gibbs gap unless an additional ratio
metric is specified. This identifies when KL computed on a reduced support
faithfully represents the full-vocabulary KL.

As a concrete causal instantiation, \Cref{app:mask-variants} includes a prefix abnormal-token rate gate (Variant~IV) based on TRM-style
per-token divergence proxies. Under mild boundedness conditions on per-token log-ratios, controlling the prefix abnormal-token rate yields
a deterministic prefix-average log-ratio bound (\Cref{lem:anom-rate-implies-pre}), making the coarse-grained gate a controllable proxy for
prefix-level ratio filtering.

\textbf{Token-level truncation and masking.} Under this view, TIS and IcePop are token-level analogues of trust-region masking: they operate
directly on the per-token ratio $\rho_t$. TIS clamps $\rho_t$ to a fixed interval $[1-\epslow, 1+\epshigh]$, retaining a biased but bounded
weight at every position. IcePop instead masks out tokens whose ratio falls outside the interval, removing extreme sampled coefficients at the
cost of possible selection bias and discarded gradient signal. Both apply a local decision at each position and therefore do not account for the downstream context shift caused
by an early deviation (\Cref{rem:token-is-fails}).

The implementation of \texttt{seq-mask-tis} and \texttt{reinforce\_pro} uses a
different separation: the sequence or prefix statistic is a detached gate,
while the retained mismatch coefficient is the detached, *unclipped* token
weight $\exp(\log\piold-\log\piroll)$. Thus these modes should not be described
as TIS clipping. The prefix gate controls where the coefficient is applied;
it does not by itself bound the magnitude of an accepted coefficient, so the
threshold choice and numerical stability remain part of the method's
implementation contract.

\textbf{Sequence-level aggregation and gating.} Sequence-level corrections aggregate ratios over the whole trajectory. GSPO~\citep{gspo2025}
clips the length-normalized sequence likelihood ratio $\rhoseq$ (a geometric mean of token ratios) and applies a single coefficient to all
tokens, which aligns with sequence-level rewards but loses positional information and can dilute isolated deviations at long horizons
(\Cref{rem:seq-is-fails}). TRM~\citep{li2025trm} applies a binary sequence gate (e.g., a max-KL trust-region criterion) and rejects entire
trajectories, which is theoretically principled but sacrifices per-position granularity.

\textbf{Prefix-level (causal) filtering.} Token- and sequence-level mechanisms leave a gap for long-horizon generation: token-level methods
ignore how an early deviation propagates to future contexts (\Cref{rem:token-is-fails}), while sequence-level gates share a single
decision across all positions. Our prefix mask $M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}$ (defined in \Cref{sec:prefix-is}) provides a causal, per-position gate
constructed from prefix ratio statistics. Compared with TRM's sequence-level gate $M^{\mathrm{TRM}}(x,y)$, it preserves positional
granularity by masking only the token-level loss terms whose prefixes violate the threshold, while still respecting the autoregressive
order. The resulting filtering can propagate early-deviation rejection and detect sustained same-side threshold violations
(\Cref{thm:prefix-tighter}).

\textbf{REINFORCE Pro Max.} Taken together with the advantage-estimation choices discussed in this appendix, REINFORCE Pro Max targets all four
dimensions discussed in this appendix. The RLOO
baseline provides statistical independence (\Cref{rem:rloo-variance}); adaptive normalization preserves advantage sign and, in its
nondegenerate ideal form, enforces zero empirical mean and unit empirical variance on non-zero tokens,
with explicit moment residuals for the implemented safeguards
(\Cref{eq:alpha-beta,prop:normalization-safeguards}, \Cref{par:adv-sign-preservation}); and prefix
cumulative IS provides causal, per-position masking with conditional early/late comparisons and sustained-violation guarantees
(\Cref{thm:prefix-tighter}). Their combined effect on the actual batch objective is given by
\Cref{prop:promax-objective-split}: empirical advantage reweighting and prefix filtering contribute separate, explicitly measurable terms.
