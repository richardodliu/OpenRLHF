\section{The Unified Framework}
\label{sec:unified}

\subsection{Why the Two Components Are Complementary}

The error decomposition (\Cref{eq:error-decomp}) has two factors at each position $t$:
\begin{equation*}
    |\mathrm{Error}| \le \sum_{t=1}^T \underbrace{2\|g_t\|_\infty}_{\text{advantage factor}} \cdot \underbrace{\|d_t^{\pitheta} - d_t^{\piroll}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}}_{\text{context shift factor}}.
\end{equation*}

\textbf{Component 1 (REINFORCE Max)} controls the sampled advantage statistics. The leave-one-out baseline provides conditional independence between
each sample and its baseline given the prompt (\Cref{rem:rloo-variance}), and the ideal adaptive normalization enforces zero empirical mean and unit empirical
variance on non-zero tokens when both sign groups are nonempty and the ideal factors are used without the additive stabilizer or clamps
(\Cref{def:adaptive-norm}). The implemented safeguards have explicit moment residuals (\Cref{prop:normalization-safeguards}).
This controls empirical scale; it does not directly bound the true $\|g_t\|_\infty$ in the population error decomposition.

\textbf{Component 2 (REINFORCE Pro)} targets the context-shift factor by filtering updates that exhibit large prefix drift. In the
implementation this drift is measured between the rollout policy $\piroll$ and the
frozen PPO snapshot $\piold$; when the snapshot is current at the start of an
update, this is the same pair as $(\piroll,\pitheta)$. The prefix-level mask
rejects tokens whose prefix average log-ratio leaves the threshold interval
(\Cref{rem:per-position-trust}). The KL chain rule yields an expectation
identity relating cumulative log-ratios to a joint KL divergence between prefix
distributions (\Cref{rem:cumsum-kl}); together with the threshold equivalence
(\Cref{rem:prefix-proxy}), this motivates viewing prefix masking as a
sample-level proxy for Causal Trust Region filtering. For stale snapshots, the
finite-prefix coupling bound in \Cref{sec:prefix-is} states the extra condition
needed to transfer the proxy to the current policy.

\begin{remark}[Heuristic Complementarity]
The discussion above should be interpreted as heuristic rather than as a formal inequality. It highlights that REINFORCE Max targets the
advantage factor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:error-decomp}, while REINFORCE Pro targets policy drift between $\piroll$ and $\pitheta$ through sample-level
prefix filtering.
Together, they address two distinct sources of instability in the surrogate optimization pipeline.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Moment Control and Gradient Variance]
The RLOO second-moment decomposition (\Cref{rem:rloo-variance}) and the normalization constraints (\Cref{eq:alpha-beta}) quantify
different parts of the estimator: a conditionally independent leave-one-out baseline contribution and realized token moments, respectively.
Neither alone proves that ProMax has lower gradient variance than every unnormalized estimator.
Prefix masking changes the retained contributions, with the exact signed change characterized next.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Exact Connection to the Implemented Objective}

The population equivalence in \Cref{prop:surrogate-equivalence} provides the reference objective for critic-free learning.
The following identity isolates the changes introduced by ProMax on an actual finite batch, preserving the prefix gate and
asymmetric normalization.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-Batch ProMax Objective Decomposition]
\label{prop:promax-objective-split}
Let $k=(i,t)$ index a sampled response and position. Write $A_k=R_i-b_i$ for its raw RLOO signal, $\hat A_k$ for the actual normalized
signal (including any configured fallback), $r_k=\pitheta/\piold$, $w_k=\piold/\piroll$, $\rho_k=r_kw_k$, and
$M_k\in\{0,1\}$ for the cached prefix gate. Let $\omega_k\ge0$ include the action mask and the selected reduction:
$\omega_{i,t}=m_{i,t}/\sum_{j,s}m_{j,s}$ for a token mean, or
$\omega_{i,t}=m_{i,t}/(B\sum_s m_{i,s})$ for a mean of $B$ response means, with positive denominators.
Assume the ratio coefficients satisfy $r_k\ge0$ and $w_k\ge0$ on the represented support, as they do for probability ratios.
Let $c_k$ be the clipped value of $r_k$. For the standard PPO branch in Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-loss}, define
\begin{align*}
    S_{\mathrm{PM}}&=-\mathcal L^{\mathrm{ProMax}}
      =\sum_k\omega_k M_k w_k\min(r_k\hat A_k,c_k\hat A_k),
    &U&=\sum_k\omega_k\rho_k A_k,\\
    G&=\sum_k\omega_k\rho_k(1-M_k)A_k,
    &N&=\sum_k\omega_k\rho_k M_k(\hat A_k-A_k),\\
    C_{\mathrm{clip}}&=\sum_k\omega_k M_k w_k
      \bigl(r_k\hat A_k-\min(r_k\hat A_k,c_k\hat A_k)\bigr).
\end{align*}
Then
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathrm{PM}}=U-G+N-C_{\mathrm{clip}},
    \qquad C_{\mathrm{clip}}\ge0,
    \label{eq:promax-objective-split}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation*}
    |S_{\mathrm{PM}}-U|
    \le\sum_k\omega_k\rho_k(1-M_k)|A_k|
      +\sum_k\omega_k\rho_k M_k|\hat A_k-A_k|+C_{\mathrm{clip}}.
\end{equation*}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Add and subtract $\sum_k\omega_k\rho_k M_k A_k$ and
$\sum_k\omega_k\rho_k M_k\hat A_k$, and use $\rho_k=r_kw_k$.
Each clipping deduction is nonnegative because $\min(r_k\hat A_k,c_k\hat A_k)\le r_k\hat A_k$.
The last inequality follows from the triangle inequality and nonnegative weights.
These finite-batch identities and bounds are verified in
\path{formal/ProMaxObjective.lean}; the theorem mapping is recorded in the proof audit.
\end{proof}

The gate term $G$ and normalization term $N$ are signed: removing a negative raw contribution can increase the batch objective,
and asymmetric scaling can move it in either direction. The identity therefore exposes exactly where the chosen stabilization
changes the raw surrogate. It applies for every realized batch, including random response lengths and cached mismatch ratios.
Taking an expectation of a randomly length-normalized batch is a separate weighting question; the identity does not identify
$\E[U]$ with the unweighted population surrogate without checking that reduction.

The standard PPO restriction is essential for the nonnegative clipping
deduction. With normalized advantage $-1$, current-to-snapshot ratio $4$,
upper clipped ratio $6/5$, and unit gate and IS weight, standard PPO gives
the maximization contribution $\min(-4,-6/5)=-4$. The optional dual-clip
coefficient $3$ changes this to $\max(-4,-3)=-3$, so the deduction from
the unclipped contribution is $-4-(-3)=-1<0$.
This counterexample is verified by
\path{dual_clip_negative_penalty_counterexample} in
\path{formal/ProMaxObjective.lean}. The proposition and certificates using
its standard PPO correction do not directly describe a dual-clip run.

The reduction determines the population target. For one sampled response, let $Z$ be its raw surrogate contribution and
$L>0$ its number of active action tokens. A response-level reduction targets $\E[Z/L]$, whereas a global token-level
reduction targets the ratio $\E[Z]/\E[L]$. These are different under random response lengths: with
$\kappa=\E[Z]/\E[L]$,
\begin{equation*}
    \E[Z/L]-\kappa
    =-\frac{\E[(L-\E[L])Z/L]}{\E[L]}.
\end{equation*}
Thus the difference is an explicit length--contribution covariance term.
For concentration with RLOO, redefine $Z$ and $L$ as the sums over a full
prompt group. The response-level statistic of that group is
$n^{-1}\sum_i Z_i/L_i$, which generally differs from $Z/L$;
grouping for independence does not change the chosen reduction.
For $n$ i.i.d. blocks with $L\ge \ell>0$,
the self-normalized token estimator obeys
\begin{equation*}
    \E\!\left[\left(\frac{\bar Z_n}{\bar L_n}-\kappa\right)^2\right]
    \le \frac{\E[(Z-\kappa L)^2]}{n\ell^2},\qquad
    \Pr\!\left[\left|\frac{\bar Z_n}{\bar L_n}-\kappa\right|\ge\varepsilon\right]
    \le \frac{\E[(Z-\kappa L)^2]}{n\ell^2\varepsilon^2}.
\end{equation*}
The corresponding finite identities and bounds are checked by
\path{ratio_expectation_gap}, \path{iid_token_mean_mse_of_ratio}, and
\path{iid_token_mean_tail} in \path{formal/BatchReduction.lean}.
For the implemented PPO path, \texttt{PolicyLoss} uses the active-token mean
within each loss call; GSPO is the separate path that switches to a
sequence-level reduction. There is no independent CLI switch that makes the
standard PPO path a response-mean estimator. The dynamic replay-buffer scale
weights variable-size microbatches by response count, so it does not in
general reconstruct a global token mean when response lengths differ. The
finite reduction identities above therefore remain a qualification on how the
implemented microbatch update relates to the population target; a token-level
claim requires active-token weighting across the relevant accumulation.

There is also a useful population identity for the gate alone. At a fixed context, let
$Q(a)=\E_{\piroll}[R\mid c,a]$, $V=\E_{\piroll}[R\mid c]$, and $\bar b=\E[b\mid c]$ for an independent baseline.
For a fixed gate $M(c,a)$,
\begin{equation*}
    \E_{\piroll}[(R-b)\rho M\mid c]
    =\sum_a\pitheta(a\mid c)M(c,a)(Q(a)-V)
      +(V-\bar b)\sum_a\pitheta(a\mid c)M(c,a).
\end{equation*}
The finite Lean audit verifies this identity under the name
\texttt{finite\_masked\_surrogate\_identity}.
When $M\equiv1$, the last sum is one and the baseline offset is constant.
For the actual prefix gate, which depends on the current sampled token, this sum is the current policy's acceptance mass and
can change during optimization even though the cached gate is detached. The formula identifies the resulting baseline term explicitly.

\subsection{From the ProMax Objective to Reward Improvement}
\label{sec:promax-certificate}

The preceding decomposition can be combined with the Adaptive bound to give
a finite-sample performance certificate for the complete objective. The
sampling unit must be a full RLOO group: sibling contributions share a
baseline, whereas independently sampled groups provide independent blocks.
This also permits random response lengths and an explicit finite prompt law.

\begin{theorem}[A finite-sample ProMax performance certificate]
\label{thm:promax-certificate}
Let $p=\piroll$ and let $\{q_j:j\in\mathcal J\}$ be a finite family of
candidate policies fixed before the evaluation data are sampled. Assume the
finite prompt and vocabulary model, shared absorbing EOS padding to $T$,
and the bounded-reward and mutual-support conditions of
\Cref{thm:adaptive-bound} for every candidate. Let $B_j$ denote its Adaptive
bound from Eq.~\eqref{eq:adaptive-bound}. Use the sparse-reward,
KL-free, reward-unclipped core mode of \Cref{sec:reinforce-max}.

Sample $K\ge1$ independent blocks. Each block samples a prompt from the
same prompt law and then $n\ge2$ conditionally i.i.d. responses from $p$.
Write $A_i=R_i-b_i$ for the within-block RLOO signal and assume each
response has at least $\ell>0$ active tokens. For a block, define
\begin{equation*}
    Z_j=\sum_{i=1}^{n} A_i\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\rho_{j,i,t}-1),
    \qquad L=\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_i,
    \qquad \rho_{j,i,t}=\frac{q_j(y_{i,t}\mid c_{i,t})}{p(y_{i,t}\mid c_{i,t})}.
\end{equation*}
Here $T_i$ counts active tokens, and post-EOS ratio residuals are zero.
Let $\mu_L=\E[L]/n>0$, $\mathcal S_j=\mathcal L_p(q_j)$, and
\begin{equation*}
    \kappa_j=\frac{\mathcal S_j}{\mu_L},\qquad
    v_j=\E[(Z_j-\kappa_j L)^2],\qquad
    \widehat\kappa_j=\frac{\sum_{b=1}^{K}Z_{j,b}}{\sum_{b=1}^{K}L_b}.
\end{equation*}
Then $\E[Z_j]=n\mathcal S_j$, and for any fixed $\eta_j>0$,
\begin{equation}
    \Pr\!\left[\exists j\in\mathcal J:
       |\widehat\kappa_j-\kappa_j|\ge\eta_j\right]
    \le \sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\frac{v_j}{K(n\ell)^2\eta_j^2}.
    \label{eq:promax-uniform-confidence}
\end{equation}

Evaluate the standard PPO ProMax objective at $q_j$ and at $p$ on this
same batch, using a common frozen snapshot, prefix gate, advantages, and
global active-token reduction. The snapshot has positive probability on the
rollout support, and all three policies share the absorbing EOS transition.
For any quantity $F$ in
\Cref{prop:promax-objective-split}, write $\Delta_jF=F(q_j)-F(p)$. Then
\begin{equation}
    \widehat\kappa_j
    =\Delta_j S_{\mathrm{PM}}+\Delta_jG-\Delta_jN+\Delta_j C_{\mathrm{clip}}.
    \label{eq:promax-corrected-increment}
\end{equation}
Outside the event in Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-uniform-confidence}, simultaneously
for every $j$,
\begin{equation}
    J(q_j)-J(p)\ge
    \mu_L\bigl(\Delta_j S_{\mathrm{PM}}+\Delta_jG-\Delta_jN
                 +\Delta_j C_{\mathrm{clip}}-\eta_j\bigr)-B_j.
    \label{eq:promax-performance-certificate}
\end{equation}
Consequently, any selector $\hat j$ based on all these evaluation blocks
has probability at most the right-hand side of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-uniform-confidence} of both satisfying
$\widehat\kappa_{\hat j}>B_{\hat j}/\mu_L+\eta_{\hat j}$ and failing to
improve true expected reward.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Within each prompt group, the RLOO baseline cancels because it is independent
of its own response and the response ratio-residual sum has rollout mean
zero. Averaging over prompts gives $\E[Z_j]=n\mathcal S_j$.
Center $Z_j$ by $\kappa_j L$, use $L\ge n\ell$, and apply the independent-block
second-moment bound and a finite union bound. The exact batch decomposition
at the two policies gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-corrected-increment}; the
Adaptive bound then proves Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-performance-certificate}.
The full finite model, objective conversion and selection event are checked
in \path{formal/ProMaxBatch.lean} and
\path{formal/ProMaxCertificate.lean}; see \Cref{app:promax-certificate}.
\end{proof}

The certificate retains the method's prefix gate, asymmetric normalization,
configured advantage fallback and PPO clipping. It quantifies the observed
objective gain needed to overcome their signed changes, sampling uncertainty
and policy-distribution shift. It does not require the gate or normalization
to be independent of the batch. At the reference evaluation $p$, the PPO
ratio is $p/\piold$, which need not be one; hence the reference clipping
penalty cannot generally be discarded, and its difference can have either
sign. The frozen gate remains the implemented $\piold/p$ gate.

\paragraph{Evaluation sampling and group filtering.}
The independent-block assumption refers to the unconditioned group law
$P(x)\prod_i p(y_i\mid x)$. The implementation's optional
\texttt{dynamic\_filtering} rejects whole groups according to their average
score. Conditioning on acceptance can couple sibling responses and reweight
prompts, so the retained groups do not automatically satisfy this theorem's
sampling assumptions. This is distinct from the prefix gate inside the loss.
For a concrete counterexample, fix one prompt, take two independent
Bernoulli$(1/2)$ responses with reward equal to score, and retain only groups
with mean score strictly between zero and one. Only mixed pairs survive:
the retained rewards have covariance $-1/4$, although their marginal means
remain $1/2$. With the score derivative taken with respect to the Bernoulli
success probability, the raw group RLOO estimator has expectation $1$
before filtering and $2$ after filtering, while the reward derivative is $1$.
These exact finite identities are verified in
\path{formal/GroupFiltering.lean}. Thus a policy trained with group filtering
may still be assessed by this certificate on a separate, unfiltered
conditionally i.i.d. evaluation batch, subject to the other hypotheses;
reusing accepted training groups requires a separate sampling-law analysis.

The family may comprise policies trained on separate data, with the theorem
applied conditionally on that training data. Retaining finitely many checkpoints
fitted on the evaluation blocks does not by itself meet the fixed-family
condition. \Cref{cor:covered-policy-certificate} below extends the guarantee
to fitting within a pre-specified, finitely covered policy class, with an
explicit approximation and complexity cost. The quantities $v_j$,
$\mu_L$, and $B_j$ are population quantities; a numerical use of the
certificate needs valid values or conservative bounds, rather than replacing
them by uncalibrated estimates from the same batch. In particular, the
prefix gate alone does not establish the local TV/KL premises of $B_j$.

The unknown mean length can be removed by an exact rescaling with the
observed token count. The resulting sampling bound exploits the additive
ratio residual's conditional mean-zero structure.

\begin{corollary}[An observed-length certificate with additive drift energy]
\label{cor:promax-energy-certificate}
Under the model and evaluation conditions of
\Cref{thm:promax-certificate}, let $D=\sum_b L_b$ and define
\begin{align}
    \widehat{\mathcal S}_j
    &:=\frac{D}{Kn}\widehat\kappa_j
      =\frac{1}{K}\sum_{b=1}^{K}\frac{Z_{j,b}}{n},
      \label{eq:promax-observed-rescaling}\\
    \mathcal E_j
    &:=\E_x\sum_{t=1}^{T}\E_{c_t\sim p(\cdot\mid x)}
       \sum_a p(a\mid c_t)
          \left(\frac{q_j(a\mid c_t)}{p(a\mid c_t)}-1\right)^2.
      \label{eq:promax-ratio-energy}
\end{align}
Zero-probability actions contribute zero. Then
$\E[\widehat{\mathcal S}_j]=\mathcal S_j$, and, for fixed $\xi_j>0$,
\begin{equation}
    \Pr\!\left[\exists j:\,
      |\widehat{\mathcal S}_j-\mathcal S_j|\ge\xi_j\right]
    \le\sum_j\frac{4R_{\max}^2\mathcal E_j}{K\xi_j^2}.
    \label{eq:promax-energy-confidence}
\end{equation}
Outside this event, simultaneously for all candidates,
\begin{equation}
    J(q_j)-J(p)\ge
    \frac{D}{Kn}\bigl(\Delta_j S_{\mathrm{PM}}+\Delta_jG
      -\Delta_jN+\Delta_j C_{\mathrm{clip}}\bigr)-\xi_j-B_j.
    \label{eq:promax-observed-certificate}
\end{equation}
Any data-dependent selector from the fixed family therefore has probability
at most the right-hand side of Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-energy-confidence} of
passing $\widehat{\mathcal S}_{\hat j}>\xi_{\hat j}+B_{\hat j}$ with
nonpositive true reward gain.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Write $H_j(y)=\sum_t(\rho_{j,t}(y)-1)$. Normalization and support give
$\E_p[\rho_{j,t}-1\mid c_t]=0$. Expanding the square along the history tree
therefore yields $\E_{x,y\sim p}[H_j(y)^2]=\mathcal E_j$.
Since $|A_i|\le2R_{\max}$, the squared group mean satisfies
$(Z_j/n)^2\le n^{-1}\sum_i A_i^2H_j(y_i)^2$ and thus
$\E[(Z_j/n)^2]\le4R_{\max}^2\mathcal E_j$.
The exact rescaling gives an unbiased average of independent blocks.
Variance is bounded by the second moment, so Chebyshev's inequality and a
union bound prove the stated probability bound. Apply the Adaptive
performance bound and Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-corrected-increment}.
The history-tree identity and full RLOO/objective composition are checked in
\path{formal/SurrogateEnergy.lean} and
\path{formal/EmpiricalCertificate.lean}; details appear in
\Cref{app:promax-energy-certificate}.
\end{proof}

This rescaling uses the observed $D$ as part of a new estimator; it does not
substitute a sample mean into the population-length certificate. The
original objective, including all stabilization terms, remains explicit in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-observed-certificate}. If
$|q_j(a\mid c)/p(a\mid c)-1|\le d_j$ on positive-$p$ actions at every
history within the horizon, then $\mathcal E_j\le Td_j^2$.
Consequently, the sampling margin for an individual fixed failure budget
$\alpha_j>0$ can be controlled on the scale
$R_{\max}d_j\sqrt{T/(K\alpha_j)}$.
The exact energy may be smaller, and shared absorbing EOS transitions add
zero energy after termination. This is a statement about sampling error;
the separate Adaptive cost $B_j$ retains its own horizon dependence.
The group-square argument alone does not quantify the $n$ dependence;
\Cref{prop:rloo-group-variance} computes it with shared baselines retained. Numerical use
still requires a valid bound on $\mathcal E_j$ (or $d_j$) and $B_j$;
a maximum ratio observed only in the evaluation batch is insufficient.

\begin{proposition}[Exact RLOO group variance and prompt sampling]
\label{prop:rloo-group-variance}
Retain the finite model and sampling scheme of
\Cref{cor:promax-energy-certificate}. At a fixed prompt $x$, let
$X=R(x,Y)-\E_p[R(x,Y)\mid x]$, $H_j=\sum_t(\rho_{j,t}-1)$, and define
\begin{equation*}
    \theta_{j,x}=\E_p[XH_j\mid x]=\mathcal S_{j,x},\quad
    M_{j,x}=\E_p[X^2H_j^2\mid x],\quad
    \sigma_{R,x}^2=\E_p[X^2\mid x],\quad
    \mathcal E_{j,x}=\E_p[H_j^2\mid x].
\end{equation*}
For the actual RLOO group mean $U_j=Z_j/n$, including its shared baselines,
\begin{equation}
    V_{j,x}(n):=\Var(U_j\mid x)
    =\frac{M_{j,x}-\theta_{j,x}^2}{n}
      +\frac{\sigma_{R,x}^2\mathcal E_{j,x}+\theta_{j,x}^2}{n(n-1)}.
    \label{eq:rloo-group-variance}
\end{equation}
Let $\tau_j^2=\E_x[(\mathcal S_{j,x}-\mathcal S_j)^2]$ and
$V_j(n)=\tau_j^2+\E_x[V_{j,x}(n)]$. Then
\begin{equation}
    \E[(\widehat{\mathcal S}_j-\mathcal S_j)^2]=\frac{V_j(n)}{K},
    \qquad
    \Pr\!\left[\exists j:
       |\widehat{\mathcal S}_j-\mathcal S_j|\ge\xi_j\right]
       \le\sum_j\frac{V_j(n)}{K\xi_j^2}.
    \label{eq:rloo-group-confidence}
\end{equation}
With $c_n=4/n+1/[n(n-1)]\le5/n$, bounded rewards imply
\begin{equation}
    V_j(n)\le\min\!\left\{4R_{\max}^2\mathcal E_j,
                   \ \tau_j^2+c_nR_{\max}^2\mathcal E_j\right\}.
    \label{eq:rloo-group-energy-bound}
\end{equation}
The reward certificate in Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-observed-certificate} and
the guarantee for selection from a fixed candidate family therefore hold
with the failure probability in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rloo-group-confidence}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
RLOO is invariant to a common reward translation. For centered rewards,
its group mean is the unbiased sample covariance
$U_j=(n\sum_iX_iH_{j,i}-(\sum_iX_i)(\sum_iH_{j,i}))/[n(n-1)]$.
Expanding its square under the actual i.i.d. response law gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:rloo-group-variance}; the mixed moments retain all shared
sample terms. The law of total variance gives $V_j(n)$, and independent
blocks give the exact $1/K$ mean-square factor. Chebyshev's inequality and
a union bound yield the confidence result. Finally,
$M_{j,x}\le4R_{\max}^2\mathcal E_{j,x}$ and
$\sigma_{R,x}^2\le R_{\max}^2$; the total coefficient of
$\theta_{j,x}^2$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rloo-group-variance} is nonpositive for
$n\ge2$. Combine this bound with the previous group second-moment bound.
The full finite-law calculation and its connection to the objective are
checked in \path{formal/RLOOCovariance.lean} and
\path{formal/GroupCertificate.lean}; see \Cref{app:rloo-group-variance}.
\end{proof}

The first term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rloo-group-variance} is the variance with
the population mean reward as baseline, divided by $n$. The second term
quantifies the additional variance of estimating that baseline from the
other responses in the same group. Both coefficients are nonnegative.
For a single fixed prompt, $\tau_j^2=0$, so the sampling margin can scale
as $R_{\max}\sqrt{\mathcal E_j/(Kn\alpha_j)}$ for a fixed failure budget
$\alpha_j>0$. For heterogeneous prompts, increasing $n$ reduces the
conditional response-sampling component, while $\tau_j^2/K$ remains a
separate component under this prompt-group sampling scheme.
The estimator is still the exact corrected ProMax objective in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-observed-rescaling}; its gate, normalization and
clipping terms remain present in the resulting reward guarantee.

\begin{corollary}[Policy fitting with a fixed finite cover]
\label{cor:covered-policy-certificate}
Retain the finite model and group sampling scheme of
\Cref{prop:rloo-group-variance}. Let $\{q_\theta:\theta\in\Theta\}$
be a policy class fixed before evaluation, where $\Theta$ may be continuous.
Assume each member satisfies the reward and support conditions, and write
$H_\theta(x,y)=\sum_t(\rho_{\theta,t}-1)$.
Fix finitely many centers $q_{\theta_j}$, $j\in\mathcal J$, a map
$\nu:\Theta\to\mathcal J$, and a radius $\zeta\ge0$, all before evaluation,
such that
\begin{equation}
    |H_\theta(x,y)-H_{\theta_{\nu(\theta)}}(x,y)|\le\zeta
    \quad\text{for every }\theta,x,y.
    \label{eq:policy-cover-condition}
\end{equation}
For fixed $\xi_j>0$, the exact corrected statistic from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-observed-rescaling} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \Pr\!\left[\exists\theta\in\Theta:
      |\widehat{\mathcal S}_\theta-\mathcal S_\theta|
        \ge\xi_{\nu(\theta)}+3R_{\max}\zeta\right]
    \le\sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\frac{V_j(n)}{K\xi_j^2},
    \label{eq:covered-policy-confidence}
\end{equation}
where $V_j(n)$ is the exact group variance at center $q_{\theta_j}$.
If the Adaptive bound applies to each member with cost $B_\theta$,
then, outside the same event, simultaneously for all $\theta$,
\begin{equation}
    J(q_\theta)-J(p)\ge
      \widehat{\mathcal S}_\theta-B_\theta
      -\xi_{\nu(\theta)}-3R_{\max}\zeta.
    \label{eq:covered-policy-reward}
\end{equation}
Thus an arbitrary fit $\hat\theta$ based on all evaluation blocks has
probability at most the right-hand side of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:covered-policy-confidence} of passing the positive-improvement
threshold while failing to improve expected reward. The fit need not be a
cover center.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $|R_i-b_i|\le2R_{\max}$, the cover condition gives
$|\widehat{\mathcal S}_\theta-\widehat{\mathcal S}_{\theta_{\nu(\theta)}}|
\le2R_{\max}\zeta$ on every realized batch. The population representation
$\mathcal S_\theta=\E_p[RH_\theta]$ gives
$|\mathcal S_\theta-\mathcal S_{\theta_{\nu(\theta)}}|\le R_{\max}\zeta$.
The triangle inequality transfers the simultaneous center bound in
\Cref{prop:rloo-group-variance} to the whole class. The Adaptive lower bound
and the exact objective identity hold pointwise for the fitted policy.
The finite-law transfer and false-improvement event are checked in
\path{formal/PolicyCover.lean}; see \Cref{app:policy-cover}.
\end{proof}

A sufficient construction for Eq.~\eqref{eq:policy-cover-condition} is a
uniform token-ratio cover of radius $d$: if
$|q_\theta(a\mid h)/p(a\mid h)
 -q_{\theta_{\nu(\theta)}}(a\mid h)/p(a\mid h)|\le d$
at every prompt and represented history of length less than $T$, then
$\zeta=Td$ suffices. Shared zero-probability actions use the same ratio
convention as the finite model. A cover of the additive residual itself can
be tighter. The fixed cover costs a finite sum of center error budgets;
finer covers reduce the approximation term but can increase this sum.
This result allows data reuse within the specified class without changing
ProMax's gate, advantages, or clipping. It does not establish a small cover
for an unrestricted neural policy class, and checking the cover only on the
observed responses is insufficient.

\begin{corollary}[Calibrating the certificate from logit changes]
\label{cor:logit-calibration}
Retain the finite model and evaluation scheme of
\Cref{cor:promax-energy-certificate}. Suppose that, for each candidate $j$,
every context within the horizon admits positive weights satisfying
\begin{equation}
    q_j(a\mid c)=\frac{p(a\mid c)v_j(a\mid c)}
      {\sum_b p(b\mid c)v_j(b\mid c)},
    \qquad 1\le v_j(a\mid c)\le e^{\omega_j},\quad \omega_j\ge0.
    \label{eq:logit-tilt-envelope}
\end{equation}
Set $C_j=(e^{\omega_j}-1)^2/(4e^{\omega_j})$ and
$\epsilon_j=\sqrt{C_j}/2$, and instantiate the Adaptive bound with local
TV/KL bounds $(\epsilon_j,C_j)$. Then
\begin{align}
    \mathcal E_j&\le TC_j,\qquad B_j\le\overline B_j,
      \label{eq:logit-certificate-inputs}\\
    \overline B_j&:=4R_{\max}\epsilon_j\sum_{t=1}^{T}
      \min\!\left\{1,(T-t)\epsilon_j,
                         \sqrt{(T-t)C_j/2}\right\}.
      \nonumber
\end{align}
Consequently, the corrected statistic in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-observed-rescaling} satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \Pr\!\left[\exists j:\,
      |\widehat{\mathcal S}_j-\mathcal S_j|\ge\xi_j\right]
    \le\sum_j\frac{4R_{\max}^2TC_j}{K\xi_j^2}.
    \label{eq:logit-confidence}
\end{equation}
Outside this event, $J(q_j)-J(p)\ge
\widehat{\mathcal S}_j-\xi_j-\overline B_j$ simultaneously for all $j$.
Thus a positive right-hand side certifies reward improvement, including
after selection from the fixed family.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For a normalized tilt $q_a=p_av_a/\E_p[v]$ with $v_a\in[\ell,u]$,
$\ell>0$, finite weighted-moment inequalities give
$\chi^2(q\|p),D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)\le(u-\ell)^2/(4\ell u)$
and $\mathrm{TV}(p,q)\le\frac12\sqrt{(u-\ell)^2/(4\ell u)}$.
Use $(\ell,u)=(1,e^{\omega_j})$, sum the conditional energy over the
history tree, and bound each rollout-averaged local TV by $\epsilon_j$.
Substitute these bounds into \Cref{cor:promax-energy-certificate}.
The complete finite-policy composition is checked in
\path{formal/LogitBounds.lean}; see \Cref{app:logit-calibration}.
\end{proof}

For softmax rows, let $\Delta z_{j,a}(c)$ be the change from rollout to
candidate logits. If its span over actions is at most $\omega_j$, set
$v_j(a\mid c)=\exp(\Delta z_{j,a}(c)-\min_b\Delta z_{j,b}(c))$.
Softmax normalization gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:logit-tilt-envelope} exactly;
no lower bound on individual positive token probabilities is needed.
Common masked supports use the same construction on their allowed actions,
and common absorbing EOS rows take $v_j=1$.
Similarly, a fixed logit cover whose centers have rollout-relative span at
most $\omega$, and whose members have center-relative span at most $r$,
gives the response-residual radius
$\zeta=Te^\omega(e^r-1)$ in \Cref{cor:covered-policy-certificate}.
These are optional, uniform conditions over all represented contexts;
prefix gating or PPO clipping alone does not ensure them, and observed
batch maxima do not replace them. The exact energy, occupancy-weighted
Adaptive cost, and shared-baseline variance remain available when tighter.

\subsection{Algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:promax} presents the complete REINFORCE Pro Max training procedure. It unifies adaptive advantage normalization
(REINFORCE Max, \Cref{sec:reinforce-max}) and prefix-level Causal Trust Region masking (REINFORCE Pro, \Cref{sec:reinforce-pro}) into a single
training loop. The algorithm processes one prompt at a time: it generates $n$ rollout samples from the behavior policy, computes
advantages with the RLOO baseline and adaptive normalization, then applies the prefix-level mask during the loss computation. We
highlight the key design choices below.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{REINFORCE Pro Max: minimizing $\mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{ProMax}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-loss}.}
\label{alg:promax}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Prompt $x$; $n$ samples; thresholds $[1-\epslow, 1+\epshigh]$; KL-free, reward-unclipped core mode
($\texttt{init\_kl\_coef}=0$, no KL loss, and no post-RLOO reward clipping)
    \STATE \textbf{Rollout:} Generate $\{y^{(1)}, \ldots, y^{(n)}\} \sim \piroll(\cdot|x)$; store $\log \piroll(y_t|c_t)$ and action masks $m_t$
    \STATE \textbf{Reward:} Compute bounded scalar rewards $\{r_1, \ldots, r_n\}$ (any length penalty is included in $r_i$)
    \STATE \textbf{Baseline:} $\tilde{r}_i \leftarrow r_i - \frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{j \neq i} r_j$ \hfill \COMMENT{leave-one-out}
    \STATE \textbf{Token Expansion:} $A_{i,t} \leftarrow \tilde{r}_i$ \hfill \COMMENT{sparse reward, $\gamma\!=\!1$}
    \STATE \textbf{Adaptive Normalization:} If the reward group is selected by \texttt{uniform\_scale} as homogeneous (the implementation test is $\mathrm{std}(r)<10^{-8}$), set $\hat A_{i,t}\leftarrow r_i/n$ and skip normalization; otherwise compute $\alpha,\beta$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha-beta}
    \STATE \hspace{2.6em} and set $\hat{A}_{i,t} \leftarrow \alpha A_{i,t}$ or $\beta A_{i,t}$. If $\mathcal{P}=\emptyset$, $\mathcal{N}=\emptyset$, a signed sum is below $\epsilon$, or a factor is nonfinite, keep $\hat A=A$.
    \STATE \hspace{2.6em} Apply the implementation's product cap, $\epsilon$ additive stabilizer, and factor clamp $[\epsilon,10]$; these are not the ideal unit-variance branch.
    \STATE \textbf{Forward Pass:} Compute $\log \pitheta(y_t|c_t)$ and $\log \piold(y_t|c_t)$; use cached $\log \piroll(y_t|c_t)$
    \STATE \textbf{Ratios:} $r_t^{\theta/\piold} \leftarrow \exp(\log \pitheta-\log \piold)$; $w_t^{\piold/\piroll} \leftarrow \exp(\log \piold-\log \piroll)$
    \STATE \textbf{Prefix Proxy:} Compute $\log w_t^{\piold/\piroll}$
    \STATE \hspace{2.6em} Compute $\rhoprefix{t}$ from the prefix of $w_s^{\piold/\piroll}$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:prefix-is}
    \STATE \hspace{2.6em} Compute $M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}$ as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:prefix-mask}
    \STATE \textbf{Loss:} $\mathcal{L} \leftarrow \mathcal{L}^{\mathrm{ProMax}}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-loss}) \hfill \COMMENT{negative surrogate}
    \STATE \textbf{Update:} $\theta \leftarrow \theta - \alpha_{\mathrm{lr}} \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\textbf{Step-by-step walkthrough.}

\emph{Rollout (Line~1).} Samples are generated by a rollout engine (e.g., vLLM)
running the behavior policy $\piroll$. The per-token log-probabilities
$\log\piroll(y_t|c_t)$ are stored alongside the generated tokens. Together
with the frozen snapshot log-probabilities, these cached values form
$w_t^{\piold/\piroll}$ and the total ratio factorization in
\Cref{sec:preliminaries}. This design follows the trust-region anchoring
choice analyzed in \citet{qi2026rethinkingtrustregionllm}; see
\Cref{app:mismatch}.

\emph{Baseline and Token Expansion (Lines~3--4).} The leave-one-out baseline (\Cref{def:rloo}) ensures that, under conditionally i.i.d. response sampling, each sample's baseline $b_i$ is independent of its own response and reward given the prompt, avoiding the correlated baseline artifacts of the mean baseline (\Cref{app:rloo-proof}). Under sparse reward with $\gamma = 1$ (\Cref{app:gamma-one}), the token-level advantage reduces to the shaped reward: $A_{i,t} = \tilde{r}_i$ for all positions $t$, eliminating the need for per-token reward assignment or discounted cumulative sums.

\emph{Adaptive Normalization (Lines~5--6).} The scaling factors $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are computed in closed form from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:alpha-beta}. In the nondegenerate ideal form, without the additive stabilizer or clamps, they enforce the empirical constraints in
\Cref{def:adaptive-norm} on non-zero tokens. The actual safeguarded factors have the moment residuals in
\Cref{prop:normalization-safeguards} while preserving the sign and within-group ordering of advantages
(\Cref{par:adv-sign-preservation}). When all $n$ samples receive exactly the same
reward, the RLOO baseline yields zero advantages; in this degenerate case,
normalization is skipped and the optional uniform-scale mode
(\Cref{app:uniform-scale}) supplies nonzero coefficients for nonzero
common rewards. All-zero groups remain zero; \Cref{prop:uniform-population}
quantifies the added expected score term and its reward-origin dependence.
When active response length is determined by the binary reward class,
\Cref{prop:binary-complete-update} further shows that the complete
token-reduced update retains a strictly positive population multiplier after
the implemented stabilizer, cap, clamp and fallback rules.
The implementation also
permits a configured near-uniformity tolerance; that branch is defined directly by
$\tilde r_i=r_i/n$ and is covered by the scalar coefficient statement in
\Cref{app:uniform-scale}, without treating the rewards as exactly equal.

\emph{Forward Pass (Line~7).} The training framework computes log-probabilities under the current policy $\pitheta$ and the frozen PPO
snapshot $\piold$. The rollout-policy log-probabilities $\log\piroll(y_t|c_t)$ are reused from the rollout cache.

\emph{Prefix Causal Trust Region Masking (Lines~9--11).} The implementation log-ratio
$\log w_t^{\piold/\piroll}$ captures rollout-to-snapshot drift. The prefix
cumulative statistic $\rhoprefix{t}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:prefix-is} aggregates
this drift causally: it depends only on positions $s\le t$, respecting the
autoregressive order (\Cref{def:prefix-is}). The corresponding binary mask
$M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:prefix-mask} filters out positions where the prefix average log-ratio leaves
$[\log(1-\epslow),\log(1+\epshigh)]$, enforcing a per-position, sample-level constraint (\Cref{rem:per-position-trust}).

\emph{Loss Computation (Line~12).} The loss in Eq.~\eqref{eq:promax-loss} multiplies the clipped PPO term by the action mask, the
detached vLLM coefficient, and the detached prefix gate. Concretely, $m_t$ removes padded positions, $M_t^{\mathrm{pre}}$ is a
binary mask (detached), and the normalized
advantage $\hat{A}_t$ is detached, so gradients propagate only through
$r_t^{\theta/\piold}$. The negative sign ensures that minimizing
$\mathcal{L}$ maximizes the surrogate objective.

\emph{Computational overhead.} Compared to standard PPO implementations that cache rollout-time log-probabilities, REINFORCE Pro Max adds
only lightweight arithmetic overhead. The RLOO baseline requires $O(n)$ arithmetic operations per prompt group. Adaptive normalization
computes the required first- and second-moment aggregates over positive and negative advantage tokens in a single pass. The prefix-level
mask requires one cumulative sum over the sequence,
which is $O(T)$ per sample. All components are computed from cached rollout log-probabilities and current-policy log-probabilities,
requiring no additional model evaluations beyond those already used to compute $\log\pitheta$.

\subsection{Positioning the Method}

REINFORCE Pro Max combines three choices whose effects are separated by the
preceding results. The standard RLOO baseline removes self-dependence from
each response's baseline; \Cref{prop:rloo-group-variance} quantifies the
sampling benefit without assuming independence among sibling contributions.
The asymmetric normalization solves the token-weighted empirical moment
constraints while preserving advantage signs, including with unequal
response lengths (\Cref{def:adaptive-norm,par:adv-sign-preservation}).
The prefix gate supplies a causal decision at each position, with the
conditional pattern comparisons in \Cref{thm:prefix-tighter} and the
accepted-prefix density bound in \Cref{thm:pre-causal-mechanism}.

Unlike token-only filters, a prefix decision retains information about
earlier deviations; unlike a sequence-wide gate, it can retain a prefix
before a late deviation. These are conditional mechanism guarantees.
The joint reward guarantee follows from \Cref{sec:promax-certificate},
which retains the signed objective corrections and sampling costs of the
complete method. \Cref{app:is-foundations} collects the supporting TV/KL
identities, finite-horizon remainder derivation, and detailed comparisons
with token, sequence, and coarse-grained alternatives.
