\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented REINFORCE Pro Max, a unified framework for critic-free LLM reinforcement learning that addresses two fundamental challenges:
high-variance advantage estimation and causal off-policy mismatch between behavior and training policies.

The first component, REINFORCE Max, combines a leave-one-out baseline (conditionally independent of each sample's own response given the prompt, enabling clean
second-moment analysis, \Cref{rem:rloo-variance}) with adaptive asymmetric normalization, which preserves advantage sign and enforces the
empirical constraints in \Cref{def:adaptive-norm}, \Cref{eq:alpha-beta} in its nondegenerate ideal form. The implemented stabilizer and
clamps have explicit moment formulas and residual bounds (\Cref{prop:normalization-safeguards}).
For binary RLOO groups, the ideal normalized amplitudes depend only on the
two token counts, while the factor ratio equals the class-conditional mean
length ratio (\Cref{prop:binary-rloo-normalization}). Exact scale invariance
also isolates this normalization mechanism from the removable common
mean-baseline/RLOO scale (\Cref{prop:normalization-scale-invariance}).
The second component, REINFORCE Pro, introduces
prefix-level Causal Trust Region masking (a ratio-based prefix proxy) that preserves the causal structure of autoregressive generation.
The implementation computes this proxy from the cached $(\piold,\piroll)$ ratio
while the PPO gradient uses the current-to-snapshot ratio. We showed that the
resulting prefix mask propagates early-deviation rejection and localizes late-deviation rejection under the conditions in
\Cref{thm:prefix-tighter}. The exact centered-surrogate equivalence and finite-batch objective decomposition connect the critic-free
estimator to the per-step surrogate and separate the effects of the ProMax gate, normalization, and clipping.
The complete finite-tree Adaptive remainder bound
(\Cref{thm:adaptive-bound}), attributed to the trust-region framework,
connects rollout surrogate gain to actual reward improvement with an explicit
error allowance. The prefix mask has its own accepted-prefix density-ratio
and expectation bounds (\Cref{thm:pre-causal-mechanism}).
\Cref{thm:promax-certificate} further connects the complete ProMax objective
to a finite-sample sufficient condition for reward improvement. It retains
random response lengths and RLOO group dependence, and controls selection
from a finite candidate family fixed before evaluation.
\Cref{cor:promax-energy-certificate} removes the unknown mean response
length through exact observed-token rescaling. Its sampling error is
controlled by additive conditional ratio energy on the history tree,
retaining dependence between tokens and between sibling RLOO terms.
\Cref{prop:rloo-group-variance} sharpens this result with the exact RLOO
group-average variance, exhibiting the response-count benefit within each
prompt and the separate contribution of prompt sampling.
\Cref{cor:covered-policy-certificate} extends the guarantee to an arbitrary
fit on the evaluation data within a fixed policy class with a finite cover.
The extra margin $3R_{\max}\zeta$ and the finite sum of center error budgets
make the approximation and complexity costs explicit.
\Cref{cor:logit-calibration} supplies explicit energy, Adaptive-cost,
and cover-radius bounds from uniform logit-change envelopes, without
requiring a minimum positive token probability.
The KL chain rule yields an
expectation identity for the cumulative log-ratio (\Cref{rem:cumsum-kl}), while the prefix thresholding rule enforces a per-position
sample constraint (\Cref{rem:prefix-proxy}). For stale snapshots, the
finite-prefix coupling condition is required to transfer this diagnostic to
the current policy.

\textbf{Limitations and future work.} The prefix-mask thresholds $[1-\epslow, 1+\epshigh]$ are hyperparameters that require tuning. The
sample-level prefix constraint controls accepted, unnormalized prefix mass;
it does not certify the maximum population TV/KL assumptions needed by the
Adaptive bound. The exact objective decomposition and sampling margins
retain the effects of filtering, normalization, clipping, and reduction.
The selection guarantee uses either a fixed finite candidate family or a
pre-specified policy class with a fixed finite cover. A useful cover size
for unrestricted neural policy fitting is not established; fitting or
checking the cover only on the evaluation batch does not meet its premises.
The logit-based calibration supplies one such numerical instantiation;
establishing its uniform envelope for a trained neural policy remains a
separate requirement, and batch-only measurements cannot establish it.
The optional uniform-scale branch adds an on-policy per-prompt score term
aligned with the $0/1$ reward gradient, but depends on the reward zero
point; a common reward offset can reverse its update
(\Cref{prop:uniform-population}).
Under the explicit sufficient condition that active response length depends
only on the binary reward class, the complete token-reduced update remains a
strictly positive multiple of the success-probability gradient even with the
implemented stabilizer, cap, clamps and identity fallback
(\Cref{prop:binary-complete-update}). If within-class length variation is
correlated with response scores, this alignment needs a separate covariance
bound and is not claimed by the theorem. The exact within-class covariance
decomposition and its finite Cauchy--Schwarz margin are given in
\Cref{prop:binary-length-covariance}.
When this covariance margin is dominated by the positive gradient component,
\Cref{cor:binary-local-improvement} proves strict true-reward improvement
for every sufficiently small step along the fixed-prompt population update.
The result permits content-dependent lengths and retains numerical
safeguards; its on-policy, accepted-gate and inactive-clipping premises
remain explicit.
The theoretical analysis assumes bounded rewards and mutual policy support (\Cref{sec:assumptions}), which may not
hold in all practical settings. The finite-sample certificate also applies to the unconditioned iid evaluation law; the optional
training-time \texttt{dynamic\_filtering} branch changes the retained group law, as shown by the exact counterexample in \Cref{sec:unified}.
A filtered training run therefore needs a separate independent evaluation batch, or a new analysis of the accepted-group law.
The current artifact contains only the synthetic structural checks in
\Cref{sec:experiments}; a large-scale language-model benchmark has not yet
been run. The paper therefore does not make an empirical performance claim.
An ICLR submission requires matched AIME/MATH or comparable reasoning-task
experiments, independent evaluation, multiple training seeds, and ablations
for each component under the protocol in
\path{reinforce_pro_max/EXPERIMENT_PROTOCOL.md}.
