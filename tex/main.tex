\title{REINFORCE Pro Max: Adaptive Advantage Normalization\\
with Causal Prefix Filtering for LLM-RL}

\author{Jason Klein Liu}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Critic-free reinforcement learning for large language models avoids a value
network, but token-weighted advantage imbalance and inference--training
policy mismatch can destabilize the sampled update. We present REINFORCE
Pro Max, combining a leave-one-out (RLOO) baseline with adaptive asymmetric
advantage normalization and causal prefix filtering. The ideal normalization
preserves advantage signs while enforcing zero empirical mean and unit
empirical variance on nonzero tokens; its implemented numerical safeguards
admit explicit moment residuals. For binary RLOO rewards, its ideal scale
ratio equals the class-conditional mean response-length ratio.
Under stated deviation conditions, prefix
filtering propagates early rejection and confines late rejection to a suffix,
with additional bounds on accepted prefix mass.
We analyze the sparse-terminal-reward core without KL shaping or post-RLOO
reward clipping on a finite autoregressive model with bounded rewards and
mutual policy support. An exact decomposition connects the implemented
clipped objective to the raw critic-free surrogate, retaining the frozen
snapshot ratios, prefix gate, normalization, and random response lengths.
Combining this identity with the Adaptive trust-region bound yields a
finite-sample sufficient condition for true reward improvement. An observed
token-count rescaling removes the unknown mean response length, while an
exact shared-baseline RLOO variance formula separates response-sampling
uncertainty from prompt-sampling uncertainty and quantifies the benefit of
additional responses per prompt. The guarantee supports selection from a
fixed finite candidate family, and extends to policies fitted on the same
evaluation data within a pre-specified class admitting a fixed finite cover,
with an explicit approximation and complexity cost. Uniform logit-change
bounds provide explicit energy, remainder, and cover-radius calibrations
without a minimum token-probability assumption. These mathematical
identities, variance formulas, and conditional reward guarantees are
verified in Lean on the same finite autoregressive policy model used by the
formal statements; this does not formalize a floating-point neural-network
implementation.
\end{abstract}
