\begin{abstract}
Critic-free reinforcement learning for language models must control both
token-mass imbalance in sampled advantages and the mismatch between rollout and
training policies. We present REINFORCE Pro Max, which combines leave-one-out
advantages, sign-preserving asymmetric normalization, and a prefix-cumulative
importance-sampling gate. The gate is computed from the cached rollout/snapshot
ratio but gradients flow only through the current/snapshot PPO ratio. On a
finite autoregressive tree, we prove an exact decomposition of the implemented
standard-PPO objective into the raw surrogate, gate and normalization
corrections, and a nonnegative clipping deduction. A finite-tree adaptive
bound then gives a conditional sufficient certificate for reward improvement.
The mathematical statements and their finite-tree proofs are checked in Lean;
the formalization does not claim to verify floating-point neural-network
training. The current artifact contains structural synthetic checks only and
does not report an LLM benchmark.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and scope}
Critic-free objectives such as RLOO and GRPO are widely used for reasoning
tasks \citep{ahmadian2024back,shao2024deepseekmath}, while PPO remains a
standard clipped policy-gradient baseline \citep{schulman2017proximal}. Recent
long-horizon trust-region analyses motivate retaining per-position causal
structure \citep{li2025trm}.
LLM-RL rollouts are generated by $\pi_{\rm roll}$ while the actor is updated
from a frozen snapshot $\pi_{\rm old}$. For response $y=(y_1,\ldots,y_T)$,
write $\rho_t=\pi_\theta(y_t\mid c_t)/\pi_{\rm roll}(y_t\mid c_t)$ and
$w_t=\pi_{\rm old}(y_t\mid c_t)/\pi_{\rm roll}(y_t\mid c_t)$. The analyzed
configuration is standard PPO (\texttt{policy\_loss\_type=ppo}),
\texttt{vllm\_is\_correction\_type=reinforce\_pro}, no dual clip, no KL or entropy
term, and sparse terminal reward with zero KL coefficient. These restrictions
are essential: optional dual clipping and auxiliary losses change the objective.

We study a finite response alphabet and finite horizon with mutual support,
bounded reward $|R|\le R_{\max}$, and explicitly stated trust-region or
moment assumptions. The results are conditional certificates, not guarantees
that an arbitrary trained neural policy satisfies those assumptions.

\section{Method}
For a prompt group of $n$ responses, the raw leave-one-out signal is
$A_i=R_i-(n-1)^{-1}\sum_{j\ne i}R_j$. After token expansion, positive and
negative active tokens need not have equal mass. Let $P,N$ be the positive and
negative token sets. The ideal ProMax factors are chosen so that
$\sum_{P}\alpha A+\sum_N\beta A=0$ and
$|P|^{-1}|N|^{-1}$-free second-moment normalization equals one on nonzero
tokens, with $\alpha,\beta>0$. Thus signs and within-sign ordering are
preserved. The implementation adds an epsilon, cap, clamp, and fallback; the
formal result treats their moment effects as explicit residuals.

For each active token, the prefix proxy is
\[
 M_t^{\rm pre}=\mathbf 1\!\left\{
 \ell_-\le \frac{1}{t}\sum_{s\le t}\log w_s\le \ell_+\right\},
 \qquad w_t=\exp(\log\pi_{\rm old}-\log\pi_{\rm roll}).
\]
The implemented loss is
\[
 \mathcal L=\operatorname{MaskedMean}_t\left[
 M_t^{\rm pre}\,\operatorname{stopgrad}(w_t)
 \left(-\min(r_t\hat A_t,\operatorname{clip}(r_t)\hat A_t)\right)\right],
\quad r_t=\frac{\pi_\theta}{\pi_{\rm old}}.
\]
The prefix gate, cached importance coefficient, action mask, and normalized
advantage are detached. Only $r_t$ carries policy gradient.

\section{Formal results}
Let $k$ index represented response/token positions, let $\omega_k\ge0$ be the
chosen reduction weight, $M_k$ the gate, $r_k$ and $w_k$ the two probability
ratios, $A_k$ the raw signal, and $H_k$ the implemented normalized signal. For
standard PPO define
\[
 U=\sum_k\omega_k r_kw_kA_k,\quad
 G=\sum_k\omega_k r_kw_k(1-M_k)A_k,\quad
 N=\sum_k\omega_k r_kw_kM_k(H_k-A_k),
\]
and
\[
 C=\sum_k\omega_kM_kw_k\bigl(r_kH_k-
 \min(r_kH_k,\operatorname{clip}(r_k)H_k)\bigr).
\]
Then the finite-batch identity is
\[
 -\mathcal L=U-G+N-C,\qquad C\ge0.
\]
The gate and normalization terms are signed corrections. The nonnegativity of
$C$ is a standard-PPO fact and does not extend to dual clip; a Lean-checked
counterexample is included in \texttt{formal/ProMaxObjective.lean}.

For a finite autoregressive tree, if every rollout-reachable history of length
less than $T$ satisfies either a local TV bound $\varepsilon$ or a local
reverse-KL bound $\delta$, the future continuation discrepancy after history
length $t$ is bounded by
\[
 \min\{1,(T-t)\varepsilon,\sqrt{(T-t)\delta/2}\}.
\]
With bounded rewards, summing these continuation errors bounds the difference
between the raw surrogate and the true reward change. Consequently, a
corrected empirical ProMax gain exceeding the explicit gate, normalization,
clipping, sampling, and adaptive remainder terms is sufficient for positive
true reward gain. The statement is for independent evaluation blocks and a
fixed finite candidate family, or a pre-specified class with a fixed finite
cover.

The finite-tree identities, adaptive recursion, RLOO moments, reduction
bounds, and binary-length covariance conditions are checked by the Lean files
listed in \texttt{formal/PROOF\_AUDIT.md}. The audit currently covers 31 labeled
mathematical environments and 911 project theorems.

For binary rewards, the ideal normalization has two class amplitudes whose
ratio is the ratio of class-conditional mean response lengths. With the actual
epsilon stabilizer, cap, clamp, fallback, and token denominator, the complete
on-policy update remains a positive multiple of the success-probability
gradient when active length depends only on the reward class and both classes
have positive mass. If length also varies within a reward class, the update
decomposes into this class multiplier plus a within-class covariance error; a
strict local-improvement conclusion then requires an explicit covariance
margin. The theorem does not claim that arbitrary content-dependent lengths or
an arbitrary Adam step improve reward.

\section{Implementation correspondence}
The code path uses active-token masking and distinguishes the differentiable
PPO ratio from the detached rollout correction. Standard PPO is fixed to the
configuration above; GSPO, dual clip, KL loss, entropy loss, MoE auxiliary
loss, reward shaping, and dynamic group filtering require separate objective
terms and are outside the stated certificate. Variable-length dynamic
microbatch response-count weighting does not automatically equal a global
token mean, so the population reduction must be reported explicitly.


\section{REINFORCE Max}
For response $i$, define $A_i=R_i-b_i$. Sparse terminal reward with $\gamma=1$
expands to the same signal on every active token. Let $m_{i,t}$ be its action
mask and let $P=\{(i,t):A_i>0,m_{i,t}=1\}$ and
$N=\{(i,t):A_i<0,m_{i,t}=1\}$. The ideal asymmetric factors $\alpha,\beta>0$
are chosen from the token masses and second moments so that
\[
 \sum_{P}\alpha A_i+\sum_N\beta A_i=0,\qquad
 \frac{1}{|P|+|N|}\left(\sum_P(\alpha A_i)^2+\sum_N(\beta A_i)^2\right)=1.
\]
The normalized signal is $H_{i,t}=\alpha A_i$ on $P$, $\beta A_i$ on $N$,
and zero on raw zero tokens. Consequently signs and within-sign ordering are
preserved while positive and negative token mass are balanced. The code adds
an epsilon, cap, clamp, and fallback; their effects are retained as explicit
normalization residuals in the objective decomposition.

\begin{proposition}[Binary class scaling]
For binary rewards with positive and negative class masses and class-conditional
mean active lengths $\bar L_+$ and $\bar L_-$, the ideal scale ratio satisfies
$\alpha/\beta=\bar L_-/\bar L_+$. Thus the token-weighted update is a positive
class-dependent multiple of the success-probability direction when length is
constant within each reward class.
\end{proposition}

When response length varies within a class, the update contains a covariance
term. A strict local-improvement statement therefore requires a covariance
margin; the theorem does not assert that arbitrary content-dependent lengths
or an arbitrary Adam step improve reward. The Lean files
\texttt{BinaryLength.lean}, \texttt{BinaryUpdate.lean}, and
\texttt{RLOOCovariance.lean} verify these distinctions.

\section{REINFORCE Pro: Causal Prefix Gate}
The cached correction uses the old-to-rollout ratio $w_t$. For thresholds
$0<\ell_-<1<\ell_+$, define the detached prefix gate
\[
 M_t=\mathbf 1\left\{\ell_-\le
 \exp\left(\frac1{\nu_t}\sum_{s\le t}m_s\log w_s\right)\le\ell_+\right\},
 \qquad \nu_t=\max\left(1,\sum_{s\le t}m_s\right).
\]
The implemented standard-PPO loss is
\[
 \mathcal L=-\sum_{i,t}\omega_{i,t}M_{i,t}\,\operatorname{stopgrad}(w_{i,t})
 \min\{r_{i,t}H_{i,t},\operatorname{clip}(r_{i,t})H_{i,t}\},
\]
where $omega$ includes the detached action mask and selected active-token
reduction. The gate, $w$, $H$, and mask are detached; only the current PPO
ratio $r$ carries the actor gradient.

\begin{lemma}[Prefix stability]
If each represented prefix ratio lies in $[\ell_-,\ell_+]$ and one additional
log-ratio perturbation is bounded by $b$, the next prefix geometric mean changes
by at most $b/(t+1)$ in log scale. Therefore a gate crossing can be localized
to a suffix after the first rejected prefix.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Causal continuation bound]
Under the local trust-region assumption, the total variation between the
continuation distributions after a history of depth $t$ is at most
\[
 d_t\le \min\left\{1,(T-t)\varepsilon,
 \sqrt{(T-t)\delta/2}\right\}.
\]
For bounded terminal rewards, the corresponding continuation-value error is
at most $2R_{\max}d_t$. Summing over reachable prefixes yields the adaptive
remainder used in the performance certificate.
\end{theorem}

The causal gate differs from token-level TIS and sequence-level GSPO. A token
outside the range is filtered according to its own prefix history, so early
drift propagates forward while a late violation affects only a suffix. The
implementation uses detached coefficients, avoiding an unintended gradient
through the rollout correction or through the gate.

\section{Unified objective identity}
Let $k$ enumerate represented active positions, with nonnegative reduction
weights $\omega_k$, gate $M_k$, raw signal $A_k$, normalized signal $H_k$, and
ratios $r_k,w_k$. Define
\begin{align*}
 U&=\sum_k\omega_kr_kw_kA_k,&
 G&=\sum_k\omega_kr_kw_k(1-M_k)A_k,\\
 N&=\sum_k\omega_kr_kw_kM_k(H_k-A_k),&
 C&=\sum_k\omega_kM_kw_k\bigl(r_kH_k-\min(r_kH_k,\operatorname{clip}(r_k)H_k)\bigr).
\end{align*}
\begin{proposition}[Finite-batch decomposition]
For standard PPO,
\[
 -\mathcal L=U-G+N-C,\qquad C\ge0,
\]
and therefore $|-\mathcal L-U|\le |G|+|N|+C$. The identity holds for every
realized batch, including random lengths and cached policy mismatch.
\end{proposition}

The three correction terms have different meanings: $G$ is the signed cost of
prefix rejection, $N$ is the signed cost of numerical normalization, and $C$
is the standard-PPO clipping deduction. The latter nonnegativity is not true
for dual clipping; the checked counterexample uses advantage $-1$, current ratio
$4$, clipped ratio $6/5$, and dual coefficient $3$, yielding deduction $-1$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-sample reward certificate]
For a finite candidate family fixed before evaluation, $K$ independent RLOO
blocks, and minimum active length $\ell$, let $\widehat\kappa_j$ be the
self-normalized corrected increment for candidate $q_j$, and let $B_j$ be the
summed continuation remainder from the causal bound. With probability at least
$1-\sum_j v_j/[K(n\ell)^2\eta_j^2]$, simultaneously for all candidates,
\[
 J(q_j)-J(p)\ge \mu_L\bigl(\Delta S_{\rm PM}+\Delta G-\Delta N
 +\Delta C-\eta_j\bigr)-B_j.
\]
Thus a positive corrected gain exceeding sampling, gate, normalization,
clipping, and adaptive remainders is a sufficient certificate for true reward
improvement. It is conditional on the stated finite-tree and trust-region
assumptions, not a guarantee for an arbitrary neural policy.
\end{theorem}

\section{Sampling, reduction, and implementation correspondence}
RLOO is unbiased for the response-level policy-gradient direction under
conditional i.i.d. responses. For a response contribution $Z$ and active
length $L$, response-mean reduction targets $\mathbb E[Z/L]$, whereas global
token reduction targets $\mathbb E[Z]/\mathbb E[L]$. Their difference is
\[
 \mathbb E[Z/L]-\frac{\mathbb E[Z]}{\mathbb E[L]}
 =-\frac{\mathbb E[(L-\mathbb E[L])Z/L]}{\mathbb E[L]}.
\]
This length covariance is why the theorem states the selected reduction
explicitly. The standard PPO code path uses active-token mean; dynamic
response-count replay weighting does not automatically reconstruct a global
token mean for variable lengths. GSPO, dual clip, KL loss, entropy loss,
reward shaping, and auxiliary losses are separate objectives outside this
certificate.

\section{Proof roadmap and formal scope}
The main proof chain is: RLOO cancellation and moments; token expansion and
normalization; prefix-gate stability and continuation discrepancy; exact
finite-batch decomposition; self-normalized sampling concentration; and the
finite-tree adaptive reward bound. Each theorem is mapped to a Lean declaration
in \texttt{formal/PROOF\_AUDIT.md}. The formal development checks exact finite
models and real arithmetic, including the dual-clip counterexample and the
binary-length covariance boundary. It does not claim to formalize floating
point, GPU kernels, Adam, Ray/vLLM scheduling, or general neural-network
optimization.

\section{Theory scope and limitations}
This is a theory paper. Deterministic structural checks for normalization and
prefix masking are included as consistency checks, rather than as an empirical
benchmark. The paper makes no claim about benchmark accuracy, sample
efficiency, or wall-clock performance. The formal proofs use exact real
arithmetic on finite models; they do not formalize floating-point overflow, GPU
kernels, Adam, Ray/vLLM execution, or the assumptions of a general neural
policy. These boundaries are part of the theorem statements and determine the
conditions under which the results apply.
